## Definitions And Objects
- `Formal spine`: structural rather than algebraic; the lecture formalizes itself through definition chains, numbered lists, allocation rules, feedback loops, and measurable checkpoints.
- `Wish`: weak hoped-for outcome without disciplined execution.
- `Desire`: the wanted end; stronger than a wish but not yet enough.
- `Disciplined eager desire`: Rohn’s hard definition of true ambition.
- `Dream`: projection of the kind of life one wants to lead; has motivational “pull” only when vivid and well-defined.
- `Legitimate ambition`: ambition morally filtered by service to others.
- `Enlightened self-interest`: self-interest educated away from greed and toward mutual benefit, giving, investment, and service.
- `Visual chain thinking`: seeing each act, task, and discipline as one link in a longer chain leading to destination.
- `Game plan`: graph-paper schedule with activities listed against days or time blocks.
- `Resource classes` in self-preparation: sure thing, good bet, even chance, unlikely, long shot.
- `Active capital`: money directly used to seek profit.
- `Passive capital`: money placed where someone else uses it and pays return / interest.
- `Results windows`: end of day, end of week, last 90 days, last six months.
- `Numerical checkpoints`: money saved and invested, books read in 90 days, classes taken in six months.
- `Resilience` seven-part list: insight, independence, tie to others, initiative, creativity, humor, morality.
- `Patience` six-part list: right timing of opportunity, alert waiting, continued preparation during delay, steady handling of setbacks/successes, patience with others’ timing, temporary withdrawal/rest.
- `Integrity`: honesty, fair price for value, finishing what one signs up for, success at service rather than expense.
- `Focused concentration`: single-task attention, staying where you are, excluding noise/clutter.
- `Self-appreciation`: acknowledging accomplishments and potential so present gains fuel future ambition.

## Derivation Steps
Wish-to-ambition chain
1. A wish names a hoped-for change.
2. The wish must harden into desire.
3. Desire without discipline stalls.
4. Discipline converts eager desire into ambition.
5. Ambition requires day-by-day action.
6. Repeated daily action changes life.

Dream pull
1. Dreams project the life one wants.
2. A fuzzy dream has weak pull.
3. A vivid dream has strong pull.
4. Strong pull helps one endure obstacles.
5. Repetition of `until` keeps action going until discomfort becomes tolerable.

Franklin/James formalization
1. The lecture first motivates through examples and anecdotes.
2. It then stabilizes motivation with Franklin’s three principles.
3. It adds James’s two-part account of success.
4. The listener is moved from inspiration to repeatable structure.
5. That structure becomes the base for later daily methods.

Positive self-direction to self-preparation
1. Ask whether current disciplines are taking you where you want to go.
2. Separate the issue into self-knowledge and self-preparation.
3. For each major goal, list possible resources/opportunity sources.
4. Classify those resources by likelihood.
5. Break preparation for each promising resource into concrete steps.
6. Do extra work to make the opportunity more likely.
7. Activity in preparation both advances the goal and refuels ambition.

Discipline versus procrastination
1. Awareness of the need for action appears.
2. If implementation follows immediately, the sequence is discipline.
3. If delay opens up, the sequence is procrastination.
4. Repeated discipline becomes habit.
5. Repeated delay becomes neglect.
6. Long neglect ends in regret.

Positive-reinforcement loop
1. Apply knowledge.
2. Observe results.
3. Keep what works and change what does not.
4. Success reinforces the disciplines that produced it.
5. Reinforced disciplines become habits.
6. Habits generate energy for further disciplined action.

Game-plan / visual-chain method
1. Fix a destination.
2. Break the destination into projects and activities.
3. Lay activities across days or time blocks.
4. Treat each completed task as a link in a chain.
5. Visible linkage reduces discouragement on slow days.
6. Repetition of planned action turns dream into reality.

70/10/10/10 path
1. Cap spending at the 70% bucket.
2. Give the first 10% to charity.
3. Put the next 10% into active capital.
4. Put the last 10% into passive capital.
5. Keep strict accounts so the buckets are real rather than imagined.
6. Repeat over time.
7. The accumulated resource income is what later defines financial independence.

Success / failure / measurement
1. Define success as steady progress toward one’s own goals.
2. Therefore measure success by results, not mood or slogans.
3. Ask for results at reasonable intervals.
4. Use numbers instead of stories where possible.
5. If the numbers improve, continue/refine.
6. If action stops and progress stops, the lecture names that failure.

Patience and persistence
1. Ambition begins movement toward a goal.
2. Worthwhile goals take time.
3. Patience handles the passing of time without collapse.
4. Persistence is patience turned into continued action.
5. Continued action through setbacks prevents discouragement from becoming surrender.
6. Setback is then re-read as adjustment material rather than final defeat.

Six principles to three cornerstones
1. Practice the six principles together, not one at a time in isolation.
2. Their joint practice builds focused concentration.
3. Their joint practice builds resilience.
4. Their joint practice builds integrity.
5. Those three stabilize ambition.
6. Stabilized ambition serves the person instead of ruling the person.
