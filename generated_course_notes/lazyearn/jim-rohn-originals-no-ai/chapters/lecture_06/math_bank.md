## Definitions And Objects

- \(W\): wish; a stated want without committed process.
- \(D\): desire; stronger wanting that begins to bind present conduct.
- \(A\): ambition; disciplined, eager desire.
- \(\text{discipline}\): the present-tense backbone that turns tomorrow’s goal into today’s action.
- \(\text{dream}\): a projection of the kind of life one wants to lead.
- \(\text{pull power}\): the motivating force exerted by a vivid, well-defined future.
- \(S\): success.
- \(I\): the Jamesian inner ideal followed persistently with courage.
- \(O\): outer achievement related to that ideal.
- \(G\): greed / avarice / gain sought at the expense of others.
- \(\text{enlightened self-interest}\): self-interest educated so that one benefits by serving others, not by damaging them.
- \(\text{deserve}\): the lecture’s operative category for return produced by planting, service, effort, discipline, and growth.
- \(\text{need}\): mere lack, explicitly denied causal power in the lecture’s “reap” logic.
- \(\text{seed}\): whatever is actually planted into life’s field: effort, discipline, skill, service, willingness.
- \(\text{discipline network}\): the lecture’s systems claim that one habit spreads into other habits, negatively or positively.

## Derivation Steps

Wish to ambition
1. Start from an ordinary wish: lose weight, make more money, live better.
2. Distinguish wishing from real desire.
3. Add discipline as the backbone of desire.
4. Translate future gain into present action: prepare today, save today, work today.
5. Name the resulting structure ambition.

Dream pull and vivid future
1. Ask why some people move toward fortune while others remain stuck.
2. Introduce dreams as projections of the life one wants.
3. Distinguish vivid dreams from fuzzy wishes.
4. Assert that only well-defined dreams generate pull power.
5. Conclude that the future must be seen as finished in advance if it is to move present conduct.

Franklin accumulation logic
1. Reject the idea that happiness comes from one large success-event.
2. Replace it with small advantages accumulated day by day.
3. Add the claim that life is plastic and can be molded.
4. Add the claim that success must be pleasurable in the present.
5. Conclude that achievement is incremental and must be enjoyed along the way.

William James decomposition
1. Decompose success into an inner term and an outer term.
2. Define the first term as an inner ideal pursued persistently with courage.
3. Define the second term as outward achievement related to that ideal.
4. Rank the first term above the second.
5. Conclude that one may already count as successful while still in pursuit, provided the inner ideal is intact.

“Until” persistence rule
1. Fix a goal or ideal.
2. Read, practice, attend, and work until understanding changes.
3. Continue until skill changes.
4. Continue until growth occurs.
5. Treat non-abandonment, not immediate payoff, as the operative rule.

Ambition versus greed
1. Begin from desire for more.
2. Separate constructive from destructive paths.
3. Destructive path: tear others down to appear higher.
4. Constructive path: dream, plan, build, and endure the weather.
5. Reserve the name ambition for the constructive path.

Deserve-not-need law
1. State the false law: need should produce return.
2. Reject it.
3. State the lecture’s law: planting produces reaping.
4. Expand planting into seed, effort, discipline, service, willingness, and skill.
5. Conclude that return follows deserving action, not mere lack.

Giving as investment
1. Start from the wish to receive.
2. Replace taking-first logic with giving-first logic.
3. Reclassify giving as investment rather than loss.
4. Treat multiplied return as the expected outcome of true investment.
5. Apply this to work: do more than you are paid for now to enlarge future return.

Discipline cascade
1. Assert that no discipline stands alone.
2. Show the negative cascade: one letdown spreads into others.
3. Show the positive cascade: one new discipline inspires another.
4. Emphasize that even the smallest action matters.
5. Conclude that reversal begins locally but propagates system-wide.
