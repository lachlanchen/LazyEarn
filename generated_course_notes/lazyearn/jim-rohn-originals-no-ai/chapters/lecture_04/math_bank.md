## Definitions And Objects
- \(s\): shakes per day in Rohn's recovery testimony
- \(W\): body weight; used only for the directional claim that weight increased by more than \(30\) lb
- \(C\): number of countries in which Herbalife is represented
- \(M\): reachable market size
- \(N_{\text{market}}(t)\): people who could still be served at time \(t\)
- \(N_{\text{served}}(t)\): people already reached by the organization at time \(t\)
- \(\Delta(t)\): service gap between total reachable need and those reached
- \(r_{\text{need}}\): rate at which new unmet need appears
- \(r_{\text{reach}}\): rate at which the organization reaches new people
- \(I_{\min}\): illustrative low-income benchmark, \(\$1/\text{day}\)
- \(N_{\text{meeting}}\): attendance scale for a large Herbalife gathering
- `training`: information channel, associated with explanation, answers, and understanding
- `testimonials`: experiential channel, associated with persuasion, inspiration, and emotional uptake
- `place`: not a geometric room but the social field made by distributors, culture, and welcome
- `always say yes`: iterative leadership rule for accepting small participatory duties

## Derivation Steps
**Recovery testimony**
1. Start with illness and severe weight loss.
2. Introduce \(3\) shakes per day plus as much food as possible.
3. Regain appetite and begin gaining weight.
4. Record \(\Delta W > 30\) lb.
5. Reduce later to \(2\) shakes per day.
6. Use the bodily change as testimonial evidence rather than as a medical proof.

**Market-size estimate**
1. Ask how many people are in the \(70\) countries.
2. Float rough lower estimates near \(2\) billion.
3. Revise upward to about \(3\) billion.
4. Fix the public claim as ``over \(3\times 10^9\) people.''
5. Turn the count into strategic scale rather than trivia.

**Why the organization is still behind**
1. State that Herbalife is in \(70\) countries with a market over \(3\times 10^9\).
2. Observe that reaching many people does not eliminate unmet need.
3. Add the local comparison: each new customer is offset by three new babies somewhere else.
4. Conclude that the rate of new need exceeds the current rate of reach.
5. Encode this as \(r_{\text{reach}} < r_{\text{need}}\).
6. Therefore the organization can grow and still remain ``behind.''

**From scale to mission**
1. Begin with the large market \(M\).
2. Recast the market as people who remain unhealthy, poor, or unrecognized until reached.
3. Add the \(\$1/\text{day}\) benchmark for extreme unmet economic opportunity.
4. Replace abstract market talk with ``until you and your organization arrive.''
5. Convert scale into duty and recruiting urgency.

**Institutional expansion**
1. Recall Mark Hughes's first \(50\) countries.
2. Add roughly \(20\) more under Michael Johnson.
3. Compress the expansion to \(50+20=70\).
4. Present executive management as handling an ``impossible'' job.
5. Use that impossibility claim to motivate broader distributor responsibility.

**Training and testimonials**
1. State that early testimonials show immediate change.
2. Distinguish the two channels explicitly.
3. Assign training to the head.
4. Assign testimonials to the heart.
5. Reconstruct this as
   \[
   \text{training} \to \text{understanding}, \qquad
   \text{testimonials} \to \text{motivation}.
   \]
6. Use both channels together as the mechanism of recruitment and retention.

**Leadership by repeated assent**
1. Start with a beginner asked to do a small task.
2. Require ``always say yes.''
3. Enumerate small duties: greet, stand at the door, shake hands, welcome.
4. Treat these as culture-forming acts, not trivial errands.
5. Reconstruct the ladder from participation to leadership.

## Notation Choices
- Use \(C\) for countries and \(M\) for market size throughout.
- Use \(s_{\text{ill}}\) and \(s_{\text{later}}\) for the two shake rates; avoid introducing nutrition notation beyond this.
- Use \(W\) only for weight change; keep units explicit as pounds.
- Use \(N_{\text{market}}(t)\), \(N_{\text{served}}(t)\), and \(\Delta(t)\) only for editorial reconstruction of the ``we're behind'' logic.
- Use \(r_{\text{need}}\) and \(r_{\text{reach}}\) for rate comparison; do not introduce continuous population models beyond the inequality they support.
- Prefer \(\to\) for conceptual flow and organizational causation; avoid \(=\) unless the transcript gives a direct numerical identity.
- Keep `training` and `testimonials` verbal inside equations; do not over-symbolize them.
- Keep the ``1 new customer to 3 new babies'' relation as a slogan-like mapping, not a literal demographic law.
- Use plain text labels such as `head`, `heart`, `understanding`, `motivation`, `hospitality`, and `leadership` rather than abbreviations.
- Treat \(3 \to 4 \to 5\) billion as rhetorical horizon notation, not forecast notation.
