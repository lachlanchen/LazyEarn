## Definitions And Objects

- `Philosophy of government waiting`: wait for external policy change to raise the floor.
- `Philosophy of company waiting`: wait for the employer to raise pay.
- `Philosophy of demand`: collective pressure for more money; locally effective but not a path to wealth in Rohn’s framing.
- `Philosophy of performance`: perform so well that higher pay becomes justified or “embarrassing” to withhold.
- `Hourly wage / hourly income`: the lecture’s main scalar quantity in the opening ladder.
- `Income multiplication by 10`: the lecture’s main arithmetic operator for expanding ambition.
- `Economic ladder`: the ordered scale from low wage to very high hourly value.
- `Work on yourself`: the operative transformation rule connecting arithmetic possibility to personal development.
- `Success as attraction`: success is treated as a consequence of becoming a different kind of person, not as direct pursuit.
- `Extraordinary life`: the broader target state beyond simple survival or ordinary living.
- `Unprecedented opportunity`: first half of the twenty-first-century diagnosis.
- `Keen / world competition`: second half of the twenty-first-century diagnosis.
- `Communication`: divided into three objects: training, teaching, inspiring.
- `Training`: showing someone how to do the business or job.
- `Teaching`: transmitting life skills, leadership, management, and related capacities.
- `Inspiring`: helping people see themselves and their future differently.
- `Fruitful`: explicitly glossed by Rohn as “productive.”
- `Productivity ladder`: producing for self, then spouse, then family, then generosity, then large-scale surplus for others.
- `Law of averages`: repeated action yields a ratio of outcomes.
- `80/20 rule`: stable asymmetric distribution of effort and results.
- `Personal philosophy`: largest determining factor in how life works out.
- `Guidance system`: Rohn’s operational definition of personal philosophy.
- `Danger channel`: part of the guidance system that spots and minimizes threats.
- `Opportunity channel`: part of the guidance system that spots and maximizes openings.
- `Learning channels`: sight, hearing, reading.
- `Selective listening`: filtering inputs rather than consuming indiscriminately.
- `Profits`: category contrasted with wages; linked to fortune.
- `Wages`: category linked to making a living.

## Derivation Steps

**Opening wage-philosophy elimination**
1. Start from a low hourly wage.
2. Test external remedies: government change, then company change.
3. Test collective pressure: demand more money.
4. Conclude that demand may move the wage but does not scale to wealth.
5. Introduce performance as the philosophy that changes the value of the worker rather than merely the pressure applied.

**Income-multiplication ladder**
1. Take a familiar base case, \(w=\$5/\text{hour}\).
2. Ask whether a known higher case exists, \(10w=\$50/\text{hour}\).
3. If \(10w\) exists, ask whether \(100w\) exists.
4. Repeat the question for \(1000w\) and \(10000w\).
5. Use concrete examples to keep each step socially plausible.
6. Conclude that the arithmetic possibility is not the barrier; the operative question is how to climb.

**From multiplication to self-work**
1. Admit the ladder is conceptually possible.
2. Ask for the mechanism of ascent.
3. Replace vague wishing with a rule: work harder on yourself than on your job.
4. Restate the economic consequence as a two-branch outcome:
5. Hard work on the job yields a living.
6. Hard work on oneself yields a fortune.

**Success-as-attraction move**
1. Reject “success as pursuit” as the main frame.
2. Recast success as a response to the person one becomes.
3. Make personal development the hidden independent variable.
4. Use that reformulation to move from economics to broader life design.

**Twenty-first-century opportunity frame**
1. State that the century offers unusual opportunity.
2. Add that it also brings world competition.
3. Use that mixed condition to justify the need for stronger internal equipment.
4. Pivot from historical condition to mind and words as means of taking advantage.

**Communication triad**
1. Start from words as a way of giving light or sight.
2. Split communication into training, teaching, and inspiring.
3. Assign training to operational skill transfer.
4. Assign teaching to life-skill formation.
5. Assign inspiring to changed self-conception and future vision.
6. Conclude that language is economically productive, not merely ornamental.

**Fruitfulness/productivity ladder**
1. Define “be fruitful” as “be productive.”
2. Set the first threshold: produce enough to survive.
3. Add a spouse: now produce more than self-need.
4. Add children: now produce for family-scale need.
5. Add generosity: produce more than family need.
6. Add large surplus: produce far more than needed for self and family.
7. Conclude that higher life corresponds to wider circles of provision.

**Surplus example**
1. Take yearly production \(P=\$10\,\text{million}\).
2. Take yearly family need \(N=\$3\,\text{million}\).
3. Compute surplus \(S=P-N=\$7\,\text{million}\).
4. Interpret \(S\) as giving capacity rather than consumption capacity.
5. Use the example to motivate the “higher life” claim.

**Law of averages**
1. Perform an action repeatedly.
2. Track attempts \(n\) and successful outcomes \(s\).
3. Observe that a ratio \(s/n\) emerges.
4. Example: 10 more contacts yields 1 result.
5. Repeat and improve performance.
6. Observe a better ratio: 10 more contacts yields 2 results.
7. Attribute the improved ratio to increasing skill, not to a new law.

**80/20 rule**
1. Start with the claim that outcomes are asymmetrically distributed.
2. State the base rule: \(20\%\) do \(80\%\), \(80\%\) do \(20\%\).
3. Apply the same pattern to time allocation.
4. Spend \(80\%\) of time with the productive \(20\%\).
5. Spend \(20\%\) of time with the less productive \(80\%\).
6. Use seminar-product stocking as an application: stock for the \(20\%\), not the whole audience.

**Personal philosophy as guidance system**
1. Define philosophy operationally rather than abstractly.
2. Give it two functions only.
3. Function one: identify and minimize dangers.
4. Function two: identify and maximize opportunities.
5. Conclude that a good life is navigational rather than accidental.

**Closing wage/profit distinction**
1. Return to the economic frame at the end.
2. Distinguish wages from profits.
3. Assign wages to making a living.
4. Assign profits to making a fortune.
5. Use the distinction as the final compression of the lecture’s economic thesis.

## Notation Choices

- Use \(w\) for hourly wage or hourly income rate throughout the bank.
- Use \(w_0,w_1,w_2,\dots\) only for local ladder stages; do not imply that Rohn formally indexed them.
- Use \(\mapsto\) for “scaled to” or “transformed into” in the income-ladder examples.
- Use \(\times 10\) only for the repeated multiplication motif; do not generalize into compound-interest or growth-model notation.
- Use \(P\) for production, \(N\) for need, and \(S\) for surplus only in the generosity example.
- Use \(n\) for attempts and \(s\) for successful outcomes in the law-of-averages shorthand.
- Use \(R=s/n\) only as editorial shorthand; the lecture states a ratio verbally but does not write a formula.
- Use percentages explicitly as \(20\%\) and \(80\%\); do not replace them with decimals.
- Use plain-language labels alongside symbols for non-formal objects:
  - philosophy
  - guidance system
  - danger
  - opportunity
  - training
  - teaching
  - inspiring
- Keep arrows in conceptual chains simple:
  - \(\text{words} \to \text{light} \to \text{sight}\)
  - \(\text{self work} \to \text{fortune}\)
- Avoid introducing calculus, optimization symbols, stochastic notation, or economic-production functions; the lecture does not support them.
- Prefer “hourly income” or “hourly value” in prose when moving beyond the literal wage examples, but keep \(w\) as the numeric placeholder.
- For the productivity ladder, prefer ordered arrows over equations:
  - \(\text{self} \to \text{spouse} \to \text{family} \to \text{generosity} \to \text{surplus for others}\)
