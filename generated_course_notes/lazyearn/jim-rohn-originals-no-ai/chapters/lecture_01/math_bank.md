## Derivation Steps
1. Start with weeds as the model object; infer that negative conditions are normal; conclude that denial is the wrong operator and handling is the right one.
2. Lift the discussion into the war frame; test the frame with short counterfactuals; conclude that absence of active good produces takeover by darkness, evil, or tyranny.
3. Convert the war frame into a work/rest rule; compress it to `6:1`; infer that excess ease or rest loses ground to the jungle.
4. Shift from global frame to local diagnosis; name the disease list; treat indifference as drift and mildness rather than open conflict.
5. Rank commitment states; reject lukewarm middle; conclude that choosing a direction and going all out is epistemically useful because error is exposed faster.
6. Group indecision, doubt, worry, and over caution as hesitation states; flip each by reversal toward decision, belief, release, and adventure.
7. [Question & Answer] Fix the same quantity at one-half; read it as “half empty” or “half full”; conclude that effect depends on interpretation more than on the raw measure itself.
8. Promote the viewpoint result into a mechanism; model the mind as a factory; let thoughts enter as ingredients and produce the fabric of life.
9. Add the guarding rule; if bad inputs enter the factory, bad outputs follow; therefore “stand guard” is the control instruction.
10. Close with complaining as a destructive feedback loop; prolonged indulgence degrades future possibility; return to the opening war frame.

## Notation Choices
- Use `\text{...}` for all lecture-native labels and disease names.
- Use `\Rightarrow` or `\leadsto` for rhetorical consequence, not for formal theorem implication.
- Use `\rightarrow` only for process flow or input-output chains.
- Use `>` for lecture comparisons like enterprise versus ease.
- Use `\succ` for ranked preference states like hot, cold, lukewarm.
- Use `6:1` exactly for the labor/rest maxim; do not convert it into percentage notation.
- Use `\tfrac12` only in the half-glass `Question & Answer` subsection.
- Keep disease names lexical; do not introduce compressed symbols like `I`, `D`, `P` unless absolutely needed for a table.
- Do not use `=` for the short separator strokes visible in `lecture_01_figure_03.png`.
- Treat board labels such as `\text{Attitude Diseases}` and `\text{pessimism}` as textual objects, not algebraic statements.
