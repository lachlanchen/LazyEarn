## Derivation Steps
1. Seriousness is first framed as universal life pressure; that pressure is then converted into a target; the target is made actionable by dividing the $400$ million into per-person shares.
2. The lecture rejects the inference `seriousness \Rightarrow motivation is enough`; it replaces it with `seriousness \Rightarrow learning is necessary`.
3. Learning is then given a control variable: the same wind blows on everyone; therefore outcome variation is not assigned to the wind; it is assigned to philosophy, i.e. sail-setting.
4. Philosophy is not allowed to remain abstract; ideas are treated as seeds; seeds do nothing without planting; therefore `get smart` must pass into `get going`.
5. Under `get going`, repeated omissions are grouped into a common form: if one should/could act and does not, neglect accumulates; neglect puts one on the wrong track.
6. Small acts are then upgraded into structural causes: small disciplines affect other disciplines; everything affects everything else; therefore small disciplines become foundational rather than trivial.
7. The time model is introduced next: life is organized as winter, spring, summer, harvest; each season asks for a different operator rather than the same response.
8. Winter is derived by subtraction: you cannot change the season; what remains adjustable is the self; therefore the response becomes `W_3 = (\text{wiser},\text{stronger},\text{better})`.
9. Spring is derived by correcting false inference: opportunity is present, but opportunity does not equal harvest; therefore one must take advantage within a limited window.
10. Summer is derived as a dual task: growth creates value to nourish and enemies to resist; therefore summer work splits into nourishment and combat.
11. Internal enemies are turned into practical constraints: indifference delays work, indecision delays commitment, doubt shrinks belief, worry escapes mastery, pessimism collapses a two-sided truth into one negative side.
12. The glass example resolves pessimism by conjunction rather than exclusion: half-empty is true, half-full is also true; the error is treating one truth as the whole state.
13. The bloodstream analogy sharpens summer logic: nourishment alone fails without defense; defense alone fails without nourishment; both must coexist.
14. Harvest is derived from prior causes: calls made or not made, letters written or not written, steadiness present or absent; therefore the crop is owned by the sower.
15. Reflection is modeled as a feedback loop: revisit experience at multiple scales; extract usable content; reinvest that content into next year’s action.
16. Communication closes the system: absorb first, then respond, reflect, share; words and testimony extend internal improvement outward into other people’s perception and action.

## Notation Choices
- Use `:=` for editorial definitions and named tuples only.
- Use `\Rightarrow` for practical implication, causal direction, or lecture-level consequence; do not read it as a formal theorem arrow.
- Use `\to` for staged process flow when the lecture describes repetition or escalation over time.
- Use `\approx` only for compressed metaphorical translation, especially the wind/sail model.
- Use `\land` only where the lecture insists that two apparently competing truths hold together, e.g. half-empty and half-full.
- Keep imperative phrases and nontechnical objects inside `\text{}`.
- Keep the lecture’s tuple names consistent:
  - `G_6` for the six-part program
  - `S_4` for the seasons
  - `W_3` for winter responses
  - `E_5` for internal enemies
  - `B_4` for absorb/respond/reflect/share
- Use `\text{sail}` rather than `\text{sale}` despite transcript noise.
- Use `\text{harvest}` in formal notation, even when the transcript briefly says “autumn harvest time.”
- Write money as `\$400\,\text{million}`, `\$1\,\text{million}`, `\$0.5\,\text{million}`.
- Do not introduce calculus, probability, optimization, or finance notation; the lecture’s formalism is enumerative and metaphorical.
