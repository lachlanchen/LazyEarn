## Definitions And Objects
- \(\textbf{Institutional investors}\): professional managers of pooled or delegated assets; the lecture includes pension funds, mutual funds, life insurers, endowments, trust companies, and related money managers.
- \(\textbf{Household net worth}\): measured household assets minus measured household liabilities; the slide treats households and nonprofits together.
- \(\textbf{Human capital}\): the value of people and what they can produce; introduced as omitted wealth that must be capitalized to get a fuller national-wealth measure.
- \(\textbf{National wealth}\): broader than the household balance sheet; in this lecture it is enlarged by adding capitalized human income.
- \(\textbf{Fiduciary duty}\): duty of a manager of other people’s money to act in the interest of the beneficiary.
- \(\textbf{Prudent person rule}\): legal standard requiring fiduciaries to act as a prudent person would act in a like capacity and with like aims.
- \(\textbf{Prudential standards}\): post-crisis regulatory standards emphasized in Dodd-Frank, especially around leverage and risk-taking.
- \(\textbf{Leverage}\): borrowing to buy assets; treated as a measure of risk imposed on a portfolio.
- \(\textbf{Mutual fund}\): publicly accessible investment company owned by participants and distributing returns to them.
- \(\textbf{UCITS}\): European standardized collective-investment form, analogous to a mutual fund but with different tax treatment in the lecture’s comparison.
- \(\textbf{Trust}\): assets managed on behalf of another person or cause, often by a trust company but not necessarily.
- \(\textbf{Spendthrift trust}\): trust that pays income but restricts access to principal.
- \(\textbf{Defined benefit plan}\): pension plan that specifies the retirement payout.
- \(\textbf{Defined contribution plan}\): pension plan that specifies the contribution, not the retirement payout.
- \(\textbf{PBGC}\): Pension Benefits Guarantee Corporation, government insurer of pension funding.
- \(\textbf{Family office}\): private management structure for a wealthy family’s portfolio and planning.
- \(\textbf{Family foundation}\): charitable organization created by a family, often endowed and tax-advantaged.

## Derivation Steps
1. Household net worth from the balance sheet
   1. Take measured household assets: \(A_{\mathrm{hh}} = 70{,}740\) billion dollars.
   2. Take measured household liabilities: \(L_{\mathrm{hh}} = 13{,}918\) billion dollars.
   3. Subtract liabilities from assets.
   4. Conclude \(NW_{\mathrm{hh}} = A_{\mathrm{hh}} - L_{\mathrm{hh}} = 56{,}822\) billion dollars.

2. Real-estate netting intuition used in the spoken lecture
   1. Start with household real estate of about \(15\) trillion dollars.
   2. Subtract home mortgage debt of about \(10\) trillion dollars.
   3. Use the lecturer’s rounded spoken result of about \(8\) trillion dollars as a rough net-real-estate figure.
   4. Treat this as intuition, not as the more exact slide arithmetic.

3. Per-capita translation
   1. Take household net worth per person: \(184{,}000\) dollars.
   2. Multiply by \(4\) for a family-of-four heuristic.
   3. Obtain \(736{,}000\) dollars.
   4. Preserve the lecturer’s rhetorical rounding to “almost \(800{,}000\)” while noting unequal distribution.

4. Public-debt adjustment to the household perspective
   1. Begin with measured household net worth: \(56.8\) trillion dollars.
   2. Subtract federal debt as a future tax burden: \(14.286\) trillion dollars.
   3. Subtract state and local debt: about \(3\) trillion dollars.
   4. Arrive at an informal adjusted perspective of roughly \(40\) trillion dollars or less.
   5. Do not present this as an official accounting identity.

5. Human-capital capitalization
   1. Take current annual U.S. national income: \(Y_0 = 13\) trillion dollars.
   2. Assume constant real growth \(g = 0.03\).
   3. Assume discount rate \(r = 0.05\).
   4. Apply the Gordon-growth capitalization formula \(W = \dfrac{Y_0}{r-g}\).
   5. Compute \(W = \dfrac{13}{0.02} = 650\) trillion dollars if interpreted literally.
   6. Preserve the board’s written result \(260\) trillion dollars as lecture evidence, while flagging the mismatch.

6. World-wealth extension
   1. Take world GDP or world income proxy: \(62\) trillion dollars.
   2. Reuse the same capitalization logic.
   3. The literal Gordon calculation with \(r-g=0.02\) gives about \(3.1\) quadrillion dollars.
   4. The spoken lecture gives about \(1.2\) quadrillion dollars.
   5. Preserve both the method and the inconsistency.

7. Defined benefit versus defined contribution logic
   1. In a defined benefit plan, the retirement payout is specified first.
   2. The manager must therefore choose assets and current funding to hit a future liability target.
   3. This creates an asset-liability matching and funding problem.
   4. In a defined contribution plan, the current contribution is specified instead.
   5. The future retirement income becomes whatever the accumulated portfolio can support.
   6. This removes the manager’s obligation to hit a fixed payout target.

8. Family-office threshold illustration
   1. Take family assets of \(100\) million dollars.
   2. Apply a \(5\%\) annual return assumption.
   3. Compute annual income of \(5\) million dollars.
   4. Use that figure to motivate hiring dedicated portfolio staff.
