2. Infer that during high-\(M\), high-\(W\) periods, firms could retain people even with weak \(C\).
3. Introduce \(T\): when conditions become turbulent, the masking effect of \(M\) and \(W\) weakens.
4. Represent that change by \(E \uparrow\): people begin reevaluating their jobs.
5. Use \(T_{\text{COVID}}\) as the lecture’s specific trigger for that reevaluation.
6. Encode reevaluation by \(\mathcal{Q}\): workers ask whether the work is meaningful, likable, and satisfying.
7. Test the perks hypothesis: firms offer \(P\) and expect retention.
8. Reject that hypothesis with \(P \not\Rightarrow R\): perks are not the main retention driver in the speaker’s account.
9. Replace it with the final condition \(C \uparrow \land G \uparrow \Rightarrow R \uparrow\).
