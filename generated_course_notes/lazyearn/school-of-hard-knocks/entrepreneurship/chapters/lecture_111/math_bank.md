## Definitions And Objects

- [transcript-backed] \(E_0\): starting daily energy reserve.

- [transcript-backed] \(E_{\mathrm{remain}}\): remaining usable energy after interactions and decisions.

- [transcript-backed] \(c_i\): energy cost of one communication, interaction, or decision.

- [transcript-backed] \(C_j\): cost of communication \(j\).

- [transcript-backed] \(I_k\): cost of interaction \(k\).

- [transcript-backed] \(D_\ell\): cost of decision \(\ell\).

- [transcript-backed] \(r\): a request, conversation, sales interaction, or demand on attention.

- [transcript-backed] \(V(r)\): value added by request \(r\) to the person, business, or team.

- [standard reconstruction] \(c(r)\): energy/time/emotional cost of engaging with request \(r\).

## Derivation Steps

1. The speaker begins with the claim that burnout follows from failing to control energy and emotion.

2. Treat a day as beginning with finite energy \(E_0\).

3. Each communication, interaction, or decision consumes a small cost \(c_i\).

4. Remaining energy is modeled as \(E_{\mathrm{remain}} = E_0 - \sum_i c_i\).

5. In entrepreneurial roles, many interactions are effectively sales interactions.

6. Each interaction should be evaluated by whether it is worth the time and energy it consumes.

7. If \(V(r) > c(r)\), the interaction may be worth engaging.

8. If \(V(r) \leq c(r)\), saying no preserves energy for higher-value work.

## Notation Choices

- Use \(E_0\) for starting daily energy; it is compact and clear.

- Use \(E_{\mathrm{remain}}\) rather than \(E_t\) because the transcript does not imply a time-indexed model.

- Use lowercase \(c_i\) for generic energy drains.

- Use uppercase \(C_j\), \(I_k\), and \(D_\ell\) only if separating communications, interactions, and decisions improves clarity.

- Use \(r\) for an incoming request or interaction.

- Use \(V(r)\) for value added; keep the value criterion tied to “you, your business, and your team.”

- Avoid probability notation, clinical burnout scales, or optimization jargon unless explicitly labeled as an analogy.
