## Definitions And Objects

- \(R_{\text{company}}\): company revenue.

- \(R_{\text{solar}}\): Mitt Group solar-company revenue; main operating engine in the interview.

- \(I_{\text{employee}}\), \(I_{\text{business owner}}\): average income or net revenue comparison as spoken by Mitt; units uncertain.

- \(F_{\text{TikTok}}\), \(F_{\text{Instagram}}\): follower counts used as evidence for media leverage.

- \(N_{\text{states}}\): number of states where Mitt Group is closing solar deals.

- Entrepreneur: person who owns or starts the operating venture and bears direct downside.

- Intrapreneur: person building wealth and responsibility inside a strong company that creates internal opportunity.

- Diversification: allocation into other ventures or assets after the primary engine has already produced meaningful surplus.

- Scaling: “doing the little things right at scale,” mainly through people who can execute without constant founder oversight.

## Derivation Steps

1. Starting a business is not automatically rational.
2. Mitt gives base-rate claims: \(86\%\) break even or lose money; \(0.5\%\) exceed \(\$10\) million in revenue.
3. Therefore the first decision is not “business or no business” as identity, but “entrepreneur, intrapreneur, or employee” as a payoff and fit question.
4. If the operator chooses entrepreneurship, the next condition is concentrated execution, not early diversification.
5. The core business must create the “big pile” first: in Mitt’s case, solar revenue above \(\$10\) million.
6. Only after that does allocation into other buckets make sense.
7. Media leverage then supports the core business by recruiting, credibility, and connection rather than replacing operations.
8. Scaling requires repeatable fundamentals executed by people; tools may add \(10\%\) to \(20\%\), but people drive order-of-magnitude growth.
9. Burnout control follows the same resource logic: energy is finite, interactions drain it, and saying no protects operating capacity.
