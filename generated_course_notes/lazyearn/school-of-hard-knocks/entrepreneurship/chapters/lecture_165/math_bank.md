3. Admit exceptional cases where balanced allocation fails locally: entrepreneurship pushes and newborn care.
4. Restrict the balance system to ordinary life, about \(80\text{--}85\%\) of the speaker’s framing.
5. Diagnose imbalance by asking what is causing stress and what is currently being prioritized.
6. Treat burnout as the failure mode: imbalance reduces desire, creativity, and work capacity.
7. Introduce time blocking as a control mechanism for the workday.
8. Use shorter task windows to reduce drift and wasted time.
9. Reduce environmental interruptions by keeping needed objects close.
10. Restore energy through hobbies and close relationships, feeding back into next-day motivation.
