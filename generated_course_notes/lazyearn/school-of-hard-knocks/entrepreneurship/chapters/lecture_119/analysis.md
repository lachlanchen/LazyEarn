# Chapter Plan

## Lecture Arc

The lecture begins as a direct interview question about the biggest risk taken and the role of risk in entrepreneurship. The answer does not start with a theory of risk; it starts with a household constraint: a newborn on the way and roughly the last $1,000 in the family bank account.

The speaker then pivots from pressure to action: he quits, begins writing his first product, and converts the only available asset, a motorcycle, into a short extension of runway. The final transition is from solitary exposure to shared commitment: a future business partner adds personal money, the two continue together, and the story closes by restating the magnitude of the risk as “putting it all on the line.”

3. The Decision Point: Quit and Build the First Product

A standalone `Question & Answer` subsection belongs here: “Was this calculated risk or recklessness?” The answer should stay grounded in the transcript: the risk was extreme, but the speaker immediately describes actions that created temporary runway.

4. Converting Assets Into Time  
Use the motorcycle sale as the central mechanism: an asset is turned into another month or two of operating time. This is the main commercial reasoning beat of the chapter.

5. Partner Capital and Shared Risk  
Describe the entry of the future business partner, who adds personal money. The rhythm shifts from individual sacrifice to joint commitment.

6. Retrospective Claim: “All on the Line”  
Close with the speaker’s own summary: calling it “all on the line” is not an understatement. Tie this back to the Entrepreneurship book’s larger themes of risk, ownership, capital, and judgment.
