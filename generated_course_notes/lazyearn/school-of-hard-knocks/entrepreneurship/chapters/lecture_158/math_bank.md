## Definitions And Objects

- \(P_0\): initial property value or contract-controlled asset value.

- \(g\): asset-level appreciation rate over the period being discussed.

- \(\Delta P\): paper increase in property value, before financing costs, transaction costs, repairs, taxes, vacancy, and selling costs.

- \(E_0\): initial cash equity or down payment.

- \(R_E\): equity return before costs, computed against initial cash equity.

- \(M\): long-horizon growth multiple for the $11,000-to-$3 million anecdote.

- \(S_{\text{gross}}\): gross wholesaling spread before assignment costs, diligence costs, financing costs, and execution risk.

- “Below value”: transcript-backed deal criterion; should mean purchase or contract price below perceived market or end-buyer value, but the transcript does not define appraisal method.

- “First right of refusal”: right to buy the office building if another offer appears; not an equation, but an ownership-option object.

- “The machine”: lead-generation and client-routing system: inbound/outbound leads, agent connection, possible credit repair, lending partner, financing path.

- “Media leverage”: ability to distribute one message beyond a live room; the transcript contrasts 10 people with hundreds of thousands, millions, and the aspiration to speak to 10 million.

## Derivation Steps

1. Leveraged paper return example:
   1. Start with controlled asset value: \(P_0 = \$300{,}000\).
   2. Apply appreciation: \(g = 10\% = 0.10\).
   3. Compute asset gain: \(\Delta P = gP_0 = \$30{,}000\).
   4. Compare gain with cash down payment: \(E_0 = \$10{,}000\).
   5. Compute paper gain multiple: \(\Delta P/E_0 = 3\).
   6. State explicitly: before costs and before realized sale proceeds.

2. Long-horizon anecdotal multiple:
   1. Initial purchase amount: \(P_{\text{buy}} = \$11{,}000\).
   2. Later offer: \(P_{\text{offer}} = \$3{,}000{,}000\).
   3. Compute multiple only if useful: \(M = P_{\text{offer}}/P_{\text{buy}}\).
   4. Optional reconstruction: annualize over 60 years with \(r = M^{1/60}-1\).
   5. Label as anecdote, not a stated expected return.

3. Deal-spread model for land wholesaling:
   1. Find land believed to be underpriced.
   2. Put it under contract at \(P_{\text{contract}}\).
   3. Find developer or end buyer willing to pay \(P_{\text{end buyer}}\).
   4. Gross spread: \(S_{\text{gross}} = P_{\text{end buyer}} - P_{\text{contract}}\).
   5. Keep costs and execution risk outside the simple formula.

4. Media leverage comparison:
   1. Live class audience: \(A_{\text{room}} \approx 10\).
   2. Social audience: \(A_{\text{social}}\) may be hundreds of thousands or millions.
   3. Reach multiple: \(M_{\text{reach}} = A_{\text{social}}/A_{\text{room}}\).
   4. Treat as attention distribution, not automatic revenue.

- Use \(P\) for property or price, \(E\) for equity, \(g\) for growth/appreciation rate, \(R_E\) for equity return.

- Use “paper gain” for \(\Delta P\) unless the transcript gives realized sale proceeds.

- Use “before costs” in every leverage equation involving return.

- Use \(P_{\text{contract}}\) and \(P_{\text{end buyer}}\) for wholesaling to avoid implying ownership transfer details not stated.
