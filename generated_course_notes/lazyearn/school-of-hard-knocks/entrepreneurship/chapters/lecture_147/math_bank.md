## Derivation Steps
1. Validation-before-build chain  
   1. Identify a need in an industry or space.  
   2. Interview as many potential clients as possible.  
   3. Talk specifically to decision-makers.  
   4. Ask what challenges they face and what solving them would mean.  
   5. Seek an early pilot commitment.  
   6. Only then build the MVP.

2. Idea-quality test  
   1. A problem may be substantial.  
   2. That alone does not make the venture a great business.  
   3. Test whether customers can actually be acquired.  
   4. If acquisition is not scalable or repeatable, keep the judgment provisional.  
   5. If acquisition becomes scalable, the idea clears a higher bar.

3. Capital-readiness chain  
   1. Build and test the product as cheaply as possible.  
   2. Try to generate some revenue first.  
   3. Reinvest into broader traction.  
   4. Show potential plus repeatable acquisition plus a plan.  
   5. Ideally show rising revenue and rising MRR.  
   6. Then take the business out for investment.

4. Scaling-leverage rule  
   1. Estimate the founder’s effective hourly value \(r_F\).  
   2. Estimate the outside cost \(c_{\text{task}}\) of the task.  
   3. If \(c_{\text{task}} < r_F\), outsource.  
   4. If the work requires permanent staff, put it into the forecast and budget.

5. Exit-readiness chain  
   1. Keep building revenue.  
   2. Keep moving the bottom line efficiently.  
   3. Efficient operating progress attracts attention.  
   4. That attention can appear as investment or acquisition interest.
