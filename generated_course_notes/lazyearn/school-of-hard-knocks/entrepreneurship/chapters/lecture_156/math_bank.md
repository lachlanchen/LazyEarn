## Derivation Steps

1. Volume And Expected Outcomes

1. Let \(N\) be the number of qualified sales attempts.
2. Let \(p\) be the close probability per attempt, held fixed for a simple editorial model.
3. Expected closes scale as \(\mathbb{E}[\text{closed deals}] \approx Np\).
4. Moving from \(100\) attempts to \(200\) attempts doubles expected opportunities under the fixed-\(p\) assumption.
5. Jacob’s reported \(200\text{--}400\) dials/day therefore functions as a controllable lever, not a guarantee.

2. Gym Membership Challenge

1. Prospect says they want to double gym membership in two years.
2. Jacob’s example asks whether word of mouth can produce \(300\) members in that period.
3. Convert the target to a monthly burden: \(300/24 \approx 12.5\) members/month.
4. Use the arithmetic to expose whether the stated growth method matches the stated goal.
5. Do not claim the lecture explicitly performs this monthly calculation; it is a standard reconstruction.

3. Commission As Performance Exposure

1. Salary is presented as not truly guaranteed because poor performance can still lead to firing.
2. Commission links pay directly to production.
3. Sales therefore resembles business ownership in one narrow sense: outcomes are more directly tied to performance.
4. Keep this as a conceptual mechanism, not a universal employment claim.

4. Listen Before Pitch

1. Ask questions and listen.
2. Identify needs, wants, and reasons for buying.
3. Build trust and rapport through accurate diagnosis.
4. Keep the pitch short, less than one minute.
5. Ask for the order once the pitch is aligned with the diagnosis.
