## Definitions And Objects

- \(Y\): annual earned income or salary.

- \(s\): savings rate, especially the transcript’s 10 to 20 percent recommendation.

- \(S\): annual savings converted from income into investable capital.

- \(I\): initial investment amount, especially the claimed \(I=\$20{,}000\) Snapchat seed investment.

- \(E\): exposure ratio, investment divided by annual salary.

- \(P\): portfolio value, distinct from annual income, dividends, or realized cash.

- \(V\): claimed investment or portfolio outcome value.

- \(M\): return multiple, useful as bookkeeping but risky if the underlying outcome combines several investments.

- \(H\): weekly work hours, used for the industrial real estate “60 hours a week” claim.

- “Income,” “portfolio value,” “realized gain,” “business value,” and “tax benefit” must remain separate objects.

## Derivation Steps

1. Savings conversion:
   1. Start with annual income \(Y\).
   2. Apply savings rate \(s\).
   3. Compute annual savings \(S=sY\).
   4. Treat \(S\) as investable capital, not as wealth by itself.

2. Salary-risk exposure:
   1. Transcript gives salary \(Y=\$80{,}000\).
   2. Transcript gives investment \(I=\$20{,}000\).
   3. Compute \(E=I/Y=0.25\).
   4. Interpret as concentrated personal exposure.

3. Portfolio-value distinction:
   1. Host asks for most money made in a year.
   2. Interviewee rejects dividend/income framing.
   3. Interviewee reframes answer as portfolio range.
   4. Notes should label the number as portfolio value, not annual cash income.

4. Return multiple caution:
   1. Define \(M=V/I\).
   2. Transcript gives \(I=\$20{,}000\).
   3. Transcript gives \(V\approx \$72{,}000{,}000\), but says Snap plus other portfolio companies.
   4. Use the ratio only as a conceptual asymmetry measure.
