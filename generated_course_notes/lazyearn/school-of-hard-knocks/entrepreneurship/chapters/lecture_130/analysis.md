## Lecture Arc

The segment opens as a direct qualification question: what makes a company worth investing in? The answer first establishes the speaker’s investing context, not abstract theory: a created investment group with about $50 million available, operating as venture capital and therefore looking at very early rounds.

From there, the speaker pivots from identity to screen: interesting ideas, thoughtful management teams, and large target markets. The next move gives scale discipline through numbers: typical investments of roughly $500,000 to $2 million or $3 million, aimed at companies probably under $2 million in revenue.

   Close with the post-investment mechanism: help the company organize, hire strong talent, and focus. Preserve the speaker’s final motivation: young CEOs have too many incoming demands and too few people to handle them.
