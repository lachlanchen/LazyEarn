## Definitions And Objects

- \(n\): total number of material risk variables in a decision. Standard reconstruction.

- \(c\): number of material risk variables the entrepreneur can control, mitigate, or fix. Standard reconstruction.

- \(u=n-c\): uncontrollable variables. Standard reconstruction; useful only if it keeps the diagram compact.

- \(\rho=c/n\): controllability ratio. Standard reconstruction; descriptive, not predictive.

- Risk variable: a concrete source of uncertainty that affects the outcome of the venture or decision.

- Mitigable risk: a risk the entrepreneur can reduce, repair, or influence through action.

- Uncontrollable risk: a risk “way out of my control”; the speaker treats this as a reason not to take the bet.

- Flyer: a loose bet taken despite too many uncontrolled variables.

- Risk tolerance: the person-specific willingness to accept remaining uncertainty after controllability has been assessed.

- Concentrated bet: placing a large share of available capital or effort into one opportunity; illustrated by the PayPal-to-Tesla anecdote.

## Derivation Steps

1. Start with a risky business decision.
2. Identify the important variables that could determine the outcome.
3. Separate variables into controllable or mitigable versus outside control.
4. If too many variables are outside control, classify the decision as a flyer.
5. If enough key variables are controllable, the decision may be viable.
6. Apply personal risk tolerance to decide whether the viable risk is acceptable.
7. Choose bet size according to tolerance and desired upside.
8. Use the Musk anecdote as an example of high tolerance plus large allocation, not as a universal rule.
