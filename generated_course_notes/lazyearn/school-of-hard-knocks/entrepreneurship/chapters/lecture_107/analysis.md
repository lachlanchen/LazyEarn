# Chapter Plan

## Lecture Arc

The lecture opens with the interviewer posing diversification as the problem: whether entrepreneurs should spread beyond one or two activities. The answer immediately reverses the premise. Instead of treating diversification as mature strategy, the speaker frames early diversification as a way to soften failure when the founder has not yet become excellent at a single thing.

The pivot is from general advice to personal sequence. He cites, with uncertainty, a Mark Cuban-style line that “diversification” is for people who are not strong enough in one domain, then grounds the claim in his own chronology: he did not begin by diversifying; first, his solar company reached more than $10 million in revenue. The motivational transition is therefore not “diversify or do not diversify” in the abstract, but “earn the right to diversify by proving one engine first.”
