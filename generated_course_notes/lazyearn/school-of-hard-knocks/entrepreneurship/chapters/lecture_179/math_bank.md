  \[

## Definitions And Objects

- \(C_t\): customers at time \(t\).
- \(N_t\): new customers acquired during period \(t\).
- \(L_t\): customers lost during period \(t\), including churn or turnover.
- \(W_t\): wage or job income used to support the founder.
- \(B_t\): available business cash or startup funds.
- \(R_t\): rent expense.
- \(F_t\): food expense.
- \(P_t\): other personal living costs.
- “Why”: the founder’s motivating reason for continuing through bad days and low-feedback periods.
- Sales: the ability to persuade customers to buy the product or service.
- Accounting: the basic numerical system that shows where the business actually stands.
- Onboarding: the post-purchase process that reduces buyer’s remorse, sets expectations, and clarifies next steps.
- Referral leverage: using useful content, free value, and relationships to create future introductions.

## Derivation Steps

1. Founder survival logic:
   1. A new business may not yet provide enough income.
   2. The founder still needs rent, food, and living costs covered.
   3. A job can provide runway and capital injection.
   4. Therefore working while starting is a financial constraint solution, not a contradiction.

2. Sales and churn logic:
   1. A business needs paying customers.
   2. Customer turnover is real.
   3. Existing customers alone may decline over time.
   4. The business must continually market and sell.
   5. Growth requires acquisition to exceed churn.

3. Sales practice logic:
   1. The speaker entered demos without practicing.
   2. Mentor analogy: professional athletes practice and watch film.
   3. Sales should be trained through role play, pitch practice, and call review.
   4. Skill improves through feedback loops, not just live attempts.

4. Onboarding logic:
   1. The customer buys.
   2. Doubt or buyer’s remorse can appear afterward.
   3. Poor expectation-setting can create chargebacks or future conflict.
   4. Onboarding should explain expectations, communication, relationship structure, and next steps.

5. Referral/social media logic:
   1. Attention is on social media.
   2. Useful or meaningful content can reach first-degree connections.
   3. A referral incentive can convert attention into introductions.
   4. The Austin Smith anecdote claims a $10,000 giveaway helped close about $100,000 in business.
