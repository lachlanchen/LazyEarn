## Derivation Steps

1. First-deal value multiple:
   1. Take the transcript-backed initial cash figure: \(C_0 \approx \$28{,}000\).
   2. Take the transcript-backed later value estimate: \(V_{\text{today}} \approx \$400{,}000\).
   3. Compute the rough ratio: \(V_{\text{today}}/C_0 \approx 14.3\).
   4. State cautiously: this is not full ROI because purchase price, debt, expenses, taxes, renovations, and timing are unknown.

2. Ownership-to-wealth mechanism:
   1. Initial trust enables first acquisition.
   2. Property is retained rather than immediately sold.
   3. Asset value rises according to Alex’s estimate.
   4. Some equity or value is extracted: \(E_{\text{pulled out}} \approx \$150{,}000\).
   5. Ownership remains, so rent cash flow continues.
   6. Mechanism: cash invested becomes retained asset plus extracted capital plus monthly cash flow.

3. Multi-lane real estate model:
   1. Traditional brokerage training narrows the agent into one path.
   2. A market shift can weaken that path.
   3. TRE’s model broadens the skill set across residential, commercial, investing, wholesaling, listing, and buying.
   4. More channels create more ways to survive and earn when one channel weakens.

4. Risk argument:
   1. Taking risk exposes capital to downside.
   2. Avoiding risk reduces that specific downside.
   3. But avoiding the deal also forfeits possible upside.
   4. Therefore, in Alex’s framing, inaction is also a risk.

5. Partnership mechanism:
   1. Trust exists before the first deal because Alex and Matt have known each other since childhood.
   2. They start by doing deals, not by theorizing a permanent partnership.
   3. Shared values reduce conflict.
   4. Different skills divide labor.
   5. The partnership becomes a scaling structure.
