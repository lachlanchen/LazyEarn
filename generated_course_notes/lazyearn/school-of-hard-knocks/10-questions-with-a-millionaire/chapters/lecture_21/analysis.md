# Chapter Plan

## Lecture Arc

The interview opens with Doug Williams's career credential: former COO of HMS Holdings, a healthcare company sold for $3.5 billion, and current investor and board member. The first movement is investment selection: a $50 million venture pool, early rounds, thoughtful teams, large markets, and companies still under roughly $2 million in revenue.

The lecture then pivots from capital allocation to operating judgment. Williams describes coaching CEOs who face too many choices with too few people, then narrows the lesson into message discipline: sell through the buyer's fear of failure, add credibility, and make the differentiation clear.

From there the interviewer turns to sales, and Williams recasts selling as diagnosis rather than demonstration: no one likes being sold to, but people like buying when a real need is understood. The next pivot is scale, where he moves from valuation language, revenue or EBITDA multiples, into a visual metaphor of a folding paper: plan the organization, metrics, alignment, relationships, and channels at each doubling before the company gets there.

The middle of the interview broadens from enterprise mechanics into career and life design. The book discussion emphasizes skills, mentors, and people over titles. Work-life balance then becomes a practical rule about being fully at work when away and fully home when home, followed by mental health as the discipline of sharpening the axe through exercise, hobbies, and more than one focus.

The close returns to origin story, financial decisions, risk, and advice. Computer science leads to field problem-solving, then Arthur Andersen, healthcare consulting, HMS, and the $3.5 billion sale. The final lessons are about knowing the people behind an investment, accepting that flawless plans do not exist, matching risk to personal profile, and asking how to help rather than how to get ahead.

7. **Risk, Trust, And Character Under Stress**
