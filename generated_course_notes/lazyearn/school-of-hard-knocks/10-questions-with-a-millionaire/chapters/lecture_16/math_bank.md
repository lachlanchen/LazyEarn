## Derivation Steps

1. **Pivot Mechanism**
   1. Start with local network audit software.
   2. Observe that the internet makes the whole world networked.
   3. Reuse the traffic-analysis engine.
   4. Replace “security violation / fraud” as the target with “anonymous user interest.”
   5. Replace security reports with marketing reports.
   6. Validate by early customer demand and rapid sales growth.

2. **Funding Readiness**
   1. An idea alone is difficult to fund.
   2. A demonstrable product lowers investor uncertainty.
   3. Early adoption shows that people actually use it.
   4. Usage evidence creates a plausible path to investment.

3. **Risk Runway**
   1. Boyd quits job to build first product.
   2. Cash falls to about `$1{,}000`.
   3. He sells a motorcycle to extend runway.
   4. Partner contributes personal money.
   5. Worst-case model remains: get a job if it fails.

4. **VC Rejection To Ownership**
   1. Company grows fast and is profitable.
   2. Founders seek VC to expand faster.
   3. VC process consumes attention and ends in rejection.
   4. No VC cushion forces stricter expense control.
   5. Founders retain high ownership.
   6. At IPO, Boyd and partner own `95\%`.

5. **Scaling Capacity**
   1. Employee is hired for a role at current scale.
   2. Company grows quickly.
   3. Same employee’s span increases from `4\text{--}10` reports to `20\text{--}30`.
   4. Capacity limit appears, like running out of oxygen at altitude.
   5. Management response: stop adding responsibility, reduce load, or hire above current scale.
