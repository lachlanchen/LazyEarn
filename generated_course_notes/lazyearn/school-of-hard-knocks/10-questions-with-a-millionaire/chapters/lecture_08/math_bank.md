## Definitions And Objects

- **Revenue claim:** Stated business revenue of \(149\,\mathrm{M}\) last year, with expectation to roughly double to \(300\,\mathrm{M}\). Treat as interview testimony.

- **W2 employees:** Direct employees, stated as more than 600.

- **Sales reps:** Salespeople selling through the company, stated as more than 3,000.

- **Seven-figure business:** In Zane’s ladder, a business primarily driven by a good idea and strong sales/marketing.

- **Eight-figure business:** In Zane’s ladder, a business that requires the right people and a strong team.

- **Nine-figure business:** In Zane’s ladder, a business that requires systems, SOPs, team, product, marketing, and sales.

- **SOP:** Standard operating procedure; a documented work function that lets another person execute a task without relying on the founder.

- **Founder dependence:** The condition where the business still relies on the CEO’s personal knowledge or direct action.

- **Machine/system:** Zane’s term for a company whose documented functions continue operating even if the CEO is absent.

- **Proximity to money:** The claimed advantage of moving into a city or environment with more capital, customers, operators, and high-value networks.

- **Idle cash:** Money sitting in a bank account without producing value or strengthening the business.

- **Deployed capital:** Money put into businesses, private equity, stocks, real estate, or other assets; for Zane, this marks the wealthy distinction.

- **Conviction:** Belief in the product; described as the dominant sales variable.

## Derivation Steps

1. **Revenue trajectory**
   1. Start with the stated last-year revenue: \(149\,\mathrm{M}\).
   2. Zane says the business should do double this year.
   3. Doubling gives \(298\,\mathrm{M}\).
   4. The host rounds this to about \(300\,\mathrm{M}\).
   5. Treat the final number as projected revenue, not audited fact.

2. **Scaling ladder**
   1. Seven figures: a good idea plus sales and marketing can create initial scale.
   2. Eight figures: the business needs a team because the founder cannot carry all functions.
   3. Nine figures: the company needs systems and SOPs because even team knowledge must be standardized.
   4. Documentation makes a function transferable.
   5. Transferability reduces reliance on the CEO.
   6. Reduced founder dependence lets the business act more like a machine.

3. **Environment/proximity mechanism**
   1. A poor local environment is described as low in money, access, motivation, and useful connections.
   2. The recommended first move is to build skill and save enough to leave.
   3. Moving to a larger city increases proximity to capital and ambitious operators.
   4. Higher opportunity density raises the chance of useful meetings, customers, and motivation.
   5. This remains a qualitative mechanism, not a measured probability model.

4. **Save versus spend resolution**
   1. Early-stage advice: save enough money to move and invest in skills.
   2. Later-stage advice: do not hoard excess business cash.
   3. The apparent contradiction resolves by distinguishing escape capital from idle capital.
   4. Once expenses, operating costs, and payroll are covered, excess cash should be put to work.
   5. Reinvestment can build the business asset and force continued operating discipline.

5. **Yacht constraint**
   1. Consider \(100\,\mathrm{M}\) net worth.
   2. A yacht with operating cost \(12\,\mathrm{M}\) per year consumes \(12\%\) of that net worth annually.
   3. Zane’s point: a luxury asset can materially stress a \(100\,\mathrm{M}\) balance sheet.
   4. With \(1\,\mathrm{B}\), the same scale of spending is less likely to change the overall financial position.
   5. Keep this as his illustrative reasoning, not a general financial planning rule.

6. **Sales conviction model**
   1. Scripts, objection handling, and role play are acknowledged as important.
   2. Zane assigns tactics only \(10\%\) of the weight.
   3. Conviction receives \(90\%\) of the weight.
   4. Buyer response is described as sensitive to enthusiasm, excitement, and belief.
   5. The chapter should present this as a sales doctrine from the interview, not a statistical model.

- Use “seven-figure,” “eight-figure,” and “nine-figure” in prose; use \(7\)-figure, \(8\)-figure, \(9\)-figure only in compact diagrams or equations.
- Use \(\Rightarrow\) for qualitative implication in diagrams, not for mathematically proven necessity.

- Use “deployed assets” rather than “net worth” when describing Zane’s strict definition of wealth, because he emphasizes money working in assets.
