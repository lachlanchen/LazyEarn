   1. State claimed income: \(I_{2023}\approx \$7.1\text{M}\).

2. **Short-term rental paper-loss mechanism**
   1. Start with W-2 or 1099 income.
   2. Acquire or operate a rental as a short-term rental.
   3. Require tenant stays of \(7\) days or less.
   4. Manage the property for \(100\) hours.
   5. Use cost segregation to accelerate depreciation.
   6. Apply the paper loss against W-2/1099 income as the example claims.

3. **Beginner entity-structure move**
   1. Begin as sole proprietor or single-member LLC.
   2. Cross roughly \(\$50{,}000\text{--}\$60{,}000\).
   3. Consider S corporation election.
   4. Pay salary from the business.
   5. Apply \(15.3\%\) self-employment tax only to salary/amount taken as described.

4. **Vehicle depreciation arithmetic**
   1. Assume business owner income: \(\$200{,}000\).

   5. Compute retained delta: \(\$50{,}000-\$20{,}000=\$30{,}000\).
   6. Reinvest delta into cash-flowing asset or business.

5. **Whiteboard STR example**
   1. Start with \(I=\$100{,}000\), W-2/1099 or entrepreneur income.
   2. Property value: \(P=\$500{,}000\).
   3. Cash saved/down payment: \(D=\$100{,}000\).

   6. Cost segregation estimate: \(20\%\) of purchase price in year one.

6. **Augusta rule mechanism**
   1. Rent primary residence for up to \(14\) days.
   2. Charge rent for business use/meetings as described.
   3. Business deducts rent; individual receives tax-free rental income as claimed.
   4. Keep this as a claimed tactic, not a full statutory derivation.

## Notation Choices

- Use \(I\) for income, \(T\) for tax, \(P\) for property value, \(D\) for down payment, \(L\) for loan.
- Use \(\mathrm{STR}\) for short-term rental.
- Use “paper loss” instead of “real loss” or “cash loss” to preserve Carlton’s distinction.
- Treat “offset” as arithmetic reduction in the example, not as guaranteed legal conclusion.
- Write “Section 179” and “Section 168(k)” in prose; use \(\S 179\) and \(\S 168(k)\) only if the chapter style supports legal notation.
