- Diversification: late-stage allocation of money into other buckets after one core engine has produced enough capital.

## Derivation Steps

1. **Entrepreneurship base-rate filter**
   1. Mitt first says most people should be cautious about starting a business.
   2. He introduces the \(86\%\) break-even-or-loss claim.
   3. He adds the \(0.5\%\) over-\(\$10\) million revenue claim.
   4. He compares owner/operator income against employee income.
   5. Conclusion: entrepreneurship is not automatically the higher-probability economic path.

2. **Why Mitt still started**
   1. People depended on him.
   2. He had time, resources, and prepared capital.
   3. He accepted the possibility of losing every penny.
   4. He could not accept staying two to three years on a path he believed was wrong.
   5. Conclusion: the decision was necessity plus readiness, not generic optimism.

3. **Diversification sequence**
   1. Common belief: millionaires have seven streams of income.
   2. Mitt’s correction: the wealth usually came first from one main thing.
   3. His example: solar passed \(\$10\) million before he diversified.
   4. Later allocation happens only after the “big pile” exists.
   5. Conclusion: concentration creates the base; diversification allocates the surplus.

4. **Social media as distribution**
   1. Audience size is stated: TikTok \(145{,}000\), Instagram \(25{,}000\).
   2. New hires report discovering him through TikTok.
   3. Social presence creates credibility and media opportunities.
   4. Conclusion: attention functions as recruiting, trust, and opportunity flow.

5. **Risk from repeatable skill**
   1. Mitt values taking risk while young enough to enjoy and use the upside.
   2. He claims he could rebuild faster because he now knows the process.
   3. Sales experience supplies the repeated exposure: rejection, cold calls, door knocking, varied customers.
   4. Conclusion: risk becomes more rational when the operator trusts a repeatable skill stack.

6. **Scaling rule**
   1. Scaling is defined as doing small fundamentals right at scale.
   2. Expansion before dominating one current market is treated as premature.
   3. CRM, technology, or one smart hire may improve performance by \(10\%\) to \(20\%\).
   4. Real scale requires many people who can operate without founder dependency.
   5. Conclusion: people plus fundamentals, not tools alone, create scale.

7. **Burnout model**
   1. Daily energy is finite.
   2. Each interaction, decision, and communication consumes energy.
   3. Emotional carryover from one failed appointment damages the next appointment.
   4. Saying no protects energy.
   5. Conclusion: burnout is framed as failed energy and emotion management.
