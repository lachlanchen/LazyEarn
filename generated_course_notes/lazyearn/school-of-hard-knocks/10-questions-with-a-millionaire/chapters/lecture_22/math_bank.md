## Derivation Steps

1. Time-zone side-hustle mechanism:
   1. Worker has a normal day job.
   2. Local selling stops around 8 p.m.
   3. West Coast licensing creates a later reachable market.
   4. The time-zone offset adds roughly three hours.
   5. Extra evening calls create a second-career runway before leaving the full-time job.

2. Goal-size mechanism:
   1. Many people set \(\$100{,}000\) as the goal.
   2. Perrin claims the small goal limits the plan.
   3. A \(\$500{,}000\) plan forces a different operating design.
   4. Even falling short of the larger plan may land near \(\$100{,}000\).
   5. State as a planning heuristic, not a guarantee.

3. Replication test:
   1. Ask whether the business would want ten copies of the current operator.
   2. If yes, the operator’s habits are probably scalable.
   3. If no, fix reliability, accountability, or value creation before scaling.

4. Money threshold:
   1. Perrin rejects “a million dollars would make me happy” as too simple.
   2. He shifts the question to management ability.
   3. If a person cannot manage \(\$1{,}000\), he says they cannot manage \(\$1{,}000{,}000\).
   4. Money can buy freedom only when paired with discipline.
