## Definitions And Objects

- Audience: the accumulated people who know, watch, buy, or may invest; the main substitute for institutional capital in Cardone’s account.

- Offer: the product, event, service, or investment invitation presented to the audience.

- Funnel: free attention \(\rightarrow\) known audience \(\rightarrow\) interested leads \(\rightarrow\) buyers or investors \(\rightarrow\) repeat buyers.

- Free content: the claimed 80% of output that costs the audience nothing but brings people into the funnel.

- Paid conversion: the claimed 20% or smaller subset that pays, invests, attends, or buys.

- Rich: high income or visible financial comfort that can stop when the income producer stops.

- Wealthy: durable ownership, brand, assets, or structures that continue beyond the individual’s labor or lifetime.

- One-unit fragility: an asset structure where a single vacancy can reduce that asset’s rent to zero.

- Multi-unit resilience: an asset structure where many rent sources reduce dependence on one tenant.

- LLC / apartment complex: the borrowing and asset-owning entity in Cardone’s explanation of real estate lending.

- Commitment: not a numeric object, but a claimed prerequisite that changes whether money and credit are treated as obstacles.

- Scale: enough customers, employees, assets, or attention that losing one customer, employee, tenant, or deal does not control the owner.

## Derivation Steps

1. Million-dollar drawdown:
   1. Start with \(\$1{,}000{,}000\).
   2. Assume the person is 21 and lives to 80.
   3. Remaining years: \(80-21=59\).
   4. Remaining months: \(59\times 12=708\).
   5. Monthly draw: \(\$1{,}000{,}000/708\approx \$1{,}412\).
   6. Round to about \(\$1{,}400\) per month.
   7. State the transcript’s assumption: no inflation and no further income.

2. Million-to-billion repetition:
   1. A billion dollars equals one thousand million-dollar units.
   2. Cardone asks how many times one must “make a million.”
   3. Bare arithmetic gives \(1{,}000\).
   4. Taxes, employees, retained margin, and losses mean the practical burden is higher.
   5. Use this as scale arithmetic, not as a full wealth model.

3. Undercover Billionaire “go to zero”:
   1. Starting cash: \(\$100\).
   2. Target: \(\$10{,}000{,}000\) business in 90 days.
   3. Scale factor: \(100{,}000\).
   4. Cardone rejects managing the \(\$100\).
   5. He returns the money and reframes the problem as people, relationships, and reinvestment.
   6. Treat “go to zero” as rhetorical operating doctrine, not general financial advice.

4. One unit versus 48 units:
   1. Single-family rental has one tenant.
   2. Tenant leaves.
   3. Rent from that asset goes to zero.
   4. Cardone identifies this as non-scaling fragility.
   5. Move to 48 units.
   6. At \(\$1{,}000\) per unit, gross rent is \(\$48{,}000\) per month.
   7. This supports the claim that the bank can lend against asset income and the entity.

5. Real estate deal extrapolation:
   1. Cardone claims a \(\$350{,}000\) investment/raise.
   2. He claims a \(\$1.9\text{ million}\) note.
   3. He claims \(\$3.7\text{ million}\) net profit.
   4. He mentally repeats the deal 10 times.
   5. Result: \(10\times \$3.7\text{ million}=\$37\text{ million}\).

6. Funding funnel:
   1. Start with claimed online audience: \(16\text{ million}\).
   2. Product buyers: \(154{,}000\).

## Notation Choices

- Use dollars explicitly: \(\$1{,}000{,}000\), \(\$1.1\text{ billion}\), \(\$80\text{ trillion}\).

- Use \(N\) for counts when needed: \(N_{\mathrm{audience}}\), \(N_{\mathrm{buyers}}\), \(N_{\mathrm{investors}}\).

- Use \(M\) for money stocks or amounts only when helpful: \(M_{\mathrm{USD}}\approx \$80\text{ trillion}\).

- Use \(U\) for units in real estate examples: \(U=1\), \(U=48\).

- Use \(R_{\mathrm{unit}}\) for rent per unit only in compact calculations: \(48R_{\mathrm{unit}}\) with \(R_{\mathrm{unit}}=\$1{,}000/\text{month}\).

- Treat arrows as conceptual flow, not formal state-transition mathematics.

- Avoid physics/operator notation entirely. There are no operators, commutators, state vectors, or board equations in this source.
