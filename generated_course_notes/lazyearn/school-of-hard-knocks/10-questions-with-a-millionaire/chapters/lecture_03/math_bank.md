## Derivation Steps

1. **Hourly income cap**
   1. Start with income tied to hours: \(I_{\text{labor}} = w \cdot h\).
   2. \(h\) is finite because a person has limited working hours.
   3. Therefore income is capped even if \(w\) is high.
   4. TJ’s conclusion: shift toward systems, investment, ownership, or passive income.

2. **Buying a cash-flowing business**
   1. Find a business already producing monthly cash flow \(C_{\text{monthly}}\).
   2. Finance the acquisition so monthly debt service is \(D_{\text{monthly}}\).
   3. Compare cash flow to debt service.
   4. If \(C_{\text{monthly}} > D_{\text{monthly}}\), there is positive monthly surplus.
   5. In the transcript’s example: \(20{,}000 - 10{,}000 = 10{,}000\).

3. **Why acquisition can be faster than starting**
   1. Starting from zero requires creating demand, process, and proof.
   2. Buying an existing business means the “playbook” is already operating.
   3. Visible cash flow gives a basis for financing and decision-making.
   4. Seller exit pressure may create opportunity.

4. **Seller supply mechanism**
   1. Some owners want or need to leave.
   2. Reasons named: age, sickness, fatigue, divorce, need for cash, lack of succession.
   3. Buyers can find them through portals, brokers, or direct outreach.
   4. This creates a market for acquiring operating businesses.
