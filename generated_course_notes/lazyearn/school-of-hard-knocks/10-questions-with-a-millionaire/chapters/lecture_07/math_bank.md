## Derivation Steps

1. Scaling from influence to action:
   1. Scaling requires good people.
   2. Good people have options.
   3. They join when they believe in the leader and the size of the dream.
   4. The leader must take a first step before full resources are present.
   5. Example: buy the minivan before the team exists.

2. Necessity level:
   1. Commit money before the team or result is fully secured.
   2. The commitment raises pressure.
   3. Pressure raises urgency.
   4. Urgency changes behavior.
   5. Edwin summarizes this as low stress producing low performance.

3. Focus then diversify:
   1. Start with one business.
   2. Become strong enough that it produces constant cash flow.
   3. Use that base to take risks in adjacent or new businesses.
   4. Security took 17 years to reach $40 million in one year.
   5. Solar reached the same annual level in one year because prior discipline transferred.

4. Three-way check split:
   1. Receive income \(I\).
   2. Spend only about \(I/3\) on living.
   3. Assign about \(I/3\) to IRS/savings.
   4. Assign about \(I/3\) to reserve.
   5. Treat reserve as nonexistent for normal spending or requests.

5. Mother’s house target:
   1. Promise mother a house within 90 days.
   2. Identify concrete requirements: $12,000 down and $1,400 per month.
   3. Convert 90 days into roughly 12 weeks.
   4. Translate the goal into eight to ten sales each week.
   5. Use the promise to keep selling late when quitting is tempting.

6. Abundance formula:
   1. Begin with becoming: hope, faith, belief, self-worth.
   2. Act before having all resources.
   3. Break the goal down until it feels attainable.
   4. Put a date on the target.
   5. Expect a major test near the goal.
   6. Continue forward and focus on what is controllable.
