## Definitions And Objects

- \(A_t\): action or attempt at time \(t\), usually a sales action, business attempt, or learning repetition.
- \(R_t\): result of the attempt, including failure, call outcome, or market response.
- \(L_t\): lesson extracted from the result after reducing emotional attachment to failure.
- \(\text{Report}_t\): end-of-day sales review containing activity, outcomes, what worked, what failed, and the salesperson’s state.
- Context: who the salesperson is “being” behind the words; Johnny treats it as distinct from content.
- Content: the literal script, words, or sales process.
- Pattern recognition: ability to notice repeated stimulus-response structures and model people who already have the desired result.
- Stimulus-response: Johnny’s basic model for sales interaction and skill improvement.
- Anchor client: first credible client/case study used to sell adjacent prospects.
- Demographics: age, background, origin, family context, and other identity descriptors of the prospect.
- Psychographics: interests, desires, behavior, psychology, and decision-making patterns.
- Transfer of certainty: Johnny’s definition of sales as helping a buyer believe the bridge from current state to desired state is real.

## Derivation Steps

1. Failure loop:
   1. Take action.
   2. Receive a result or failure.
   3. Remove the identity-level significance of the failure.
   4. Extract the lesson.
   5. Take the next action faster.

2. Feedback-loop discipline:
   1. Make sales calls or take daily actions.
   2. Record who was contacted and what happened.
   3. Record what worked and what did not.
   4. Include the salesperson’s state/context, not only the spoken content.
   5. Adjust the next day’s behavior.

3. Same-script puzzle:
   1. Give two salespeople the same script.
   2. Observe different outcomes.
   3. Conclude that content alone does not determine result.
   4. Add context, identity, confidence, and state to the model.

4. Pattern-recognition model:
   1. Find people with the desired result.
   2. Pay or work near them.
   3. Observe their stimulus-response patterns.
   4. Model those patterns instead of reinventing the process.

5. Ownership-risk decomposition:
   1. Johnny succeeds in a commission sales role.
   2. Paul points out that the role excludes marketing, delivery risk, hiring, operations, and finance.
   3. The hidden business system is larger than the closing skill.
   4. Ego causes underestimation of the missing functions.

6. Scaling chain:
   1. A good product supports a credible vision.
   2. Vision attracts leadership.
   3. Leadership supports fundraising and recruiting.
   4. Compensation attracts talent.
   5. Talent enables scale.

7. Rebuild-from-zero sequence:
   1. Sell the most expensive offer available to regain cash.
   2. Cover food and shelter.
   3. Add value for free.
   4. Convert free value into testimonials and case studies.
   5. Use an anchor client to sell adjacent prospects.
   6. Build a sales team and expand lead sources.
