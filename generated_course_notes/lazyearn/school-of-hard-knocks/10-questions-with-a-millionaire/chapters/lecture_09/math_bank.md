## Derivation Steps

1. **Capacity limit of owner-only work**
   1. Owner does all tasks personally.
   2. Owner has finite hours.
   3. Therefore business capacity is capped by owner time.
   4. To scale, some owner tasks must be replaced by other people.

2. **Scaling mechanism**
   1. Identify repeatable work.
   2. Write it down as SOPs.
   3. Select people capable of performing parts of the work.
   4. Train those people.
   5. Supervise as owner or CEO.
   6. Keep managers inspired and equipped.
   7. Result: business can operate beyond the owner’s direct labor.

3. **Why SOPs are necessary but insufficient**
   1. Written procedures standardize behavior.
   2. But people do not run the company exactly like the founder.
   3. Leadership drift can occur even with procedures.
   4. Supervision, culture, benefits, tools, and information are still required.

4. **Risk decision logic**
   1. Save capital.
   2. Calculate downside.
   3. Be prepared to lose.
   4. Keep a backup plan.
   5. Remove ego.
   6. Work to keep the “ship” from sinking.
   7. Win or lose, the attempt builds the skill set for future growth.

5. **Health versus wealth priority reversal**
   1. Person has money.
   2. Health becomes threatened.
   3. Money loses practical priority.
   4. Health becomes the dominant good.
   5. Conclusion: wealth pursuit must not erase health and family.

6. **Entrepreneurial stage model**
   1. Early stage: get knowledge and money.
   2. Character stage: become someone who will not squander opportunity.
   3. Growth stage: make larger bets and calculate bigger risks.
   4. Success stage: decide whether money is enough and how to balance life.
