## Derivation Steps

1. Delegation leverage:
   1. Transcript gives $300M/year with delegated organization.
   2. Transcript gives about $3M/year if Louis did it all himself.
   3. Compute rough ratio: \(300/3 = 100\).
   4. State as managerial leverage, not a precise causal proof.

2. Working-capital gap:
   1. First deal is about $9M annualized.
   2. Customer needs 30-day pay terms.
   3. Louis adds the fifth week of expenses going out before payment arrives.
   4. Treat the operating cash gap as roughly five weeks.
   5. Louis estimates the needed buffer at about $1M.

3. Invoice factoring:
   1. Company presents weekly invoices.
   2. Factoring company buys or advances against invoices.
   3. Advance is stated as around 90% of invoice value.
   4. All-in cost is stated as about 21%.
   5. Mechanism gives cash before customer payment, but at expensive cost.

4. Retained earnings reducing borrowing:
   1. Business generates money each week.
   2. Louis does not take the cash out personally.
   3. Cash remains in the company.
   4. Next week’s factoring/borrowing need falls.
   5. Balance sheet strengthens over time.

5. Seed money versus cash-flowed proof:
   1. Founder with only an idea asks for capital.
   2. Investor bears financial downside.
   3. Investor therefore wants control over spending.
   4. Control means majority equity or a 70/30-style split.
   5. If founder has revenue/proof first, outside money funds scale rather than raw risk.

6. Results-based negotiation:
   1. Buyer pressures current price or margin.
   2. Seller gives ground on today’s price if necessary.
   3. Seller ties future upside to measurable results.
   4. Customer pays more only after savings, growth, or bottom-line improvement.
   5. Relationship shifts from transaction price to long-term value.
