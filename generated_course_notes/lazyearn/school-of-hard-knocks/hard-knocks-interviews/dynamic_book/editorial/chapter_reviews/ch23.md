# Chapter 23 Review

## Decision

Accepted for the working manuscript. The chapter preserves the interviews'
incompatible accounts of happiness, faith, challenge, family, service, and
contentment while drawing a firm distinction between the material floor money
can secure and the judgments no balance can make. Its final enough statement
resolves the book through stewardship rather than withdrawal or accumulation.

## Narrative Role

- Opens with four different answers to the same happiness question rather than
  declaring one wealthy life exemplary.
- Protects the seriousness of money under scarcity through Glenn Boyd's
  material threshold and Edwin Arroyave's plan to buy his mother a home.
- Separates a financial target from the human job it is meant to perform.
- Distinguishes pleasure, contentment, challenge, meaning, and transient
  emotion without diagnosing the speakers.
- Presents religious testimony as testimony and preserves equally serious
  secular routes to meaning and duty.
- Adds ethical and civic boundaries to the material definition of enough.
- Produces a one-page enough statement covering floor, freedom, claims, work,
  risk, and what money may never purchase.
- Ends with stewardship of the wealth-producing machine and the allocation of
  time, risk, authority, and reward.

## Source Fidelity

- 3, 02:48–05:06: a veteran investor's relation among leadership,
  resilience, purpose, responsibility, and faith, including his admission of
  wrestling with belief. The chapter treats this as personal interpretation,
  not evidence that faith causes wealth.
- 3, 07:24–11:15: a healthcare founder's poverty, salary pressure, reported
  sale, ownership, financial freedom, stronger circles, and service. Deal
  magnitude and personal proceeds are unnecessary to the chapter and excluded.
- 3, 13:23–17:02: Tim Tebow's Christian argument, mission, warning about
  succeeding at things that do not matter, and inheritance-versus-legacy
  distinction. Unstable anti-trafficking prevalence figures are excluded.
- 3, 17:32–18:48: a post-exit couple's trust, common purpose, freedom without
  purpose, and preference over people, work, and time. The transaction remains
  generic because its size and identities add nothing to the argument.
- 4, 03:00–04:24: the beverage founder's continuing work, family, team,
  happiness, limitation of money, and reported shared sale upside. The chapter
  does not infer distributional fairness from the interview.
- 4, 07:47–11:19: John's modest upbringing, challenge-based account of
  happiness, lack of contentment, truthful sales, tax and civic judgment, and
  desire that his children leave the world better and be happy. His tax view is
  identified as a position rather than law or a complete theory of public duty.
- 4, 14:54–16:22: Scooter Braun's former ten-million-dollar target, emptiness
  after passing it, friends, generosity, entrepreneurship, adventure, money as
  burden or freedom, emotional ebb and flow, and inward work. The chapter does
  not repeat later extreme-persistence rhetoric.
- 90, 04:49–06:05: Edwin Arroyave's promise and ninety-day plan to buy his
  mother a house, including the translation of cost into weekly sales.
- 90, 07:20–10:23: scarcity, hoarding, action, attention, and faith. Abundance
  language is bounded by actual resources and does not imply that belief
  produces capital or events.
- 90, 11:20–13:27: religious struggle, outward success with persistent
  emptiness, pleasure versus happiness, gratitude, and reframing.
- 90, 13:39–15:28: the selective-attention account is retained only as a
  practical description of noticing and action, not settled neuroscience or
  a claim that thought controls external outcomes.
- 130, 00:00–00:36: Glenn Boyd's poverty-to-paid-bills threshold returns once
  to establish the nonlinear material role of money. It is treated as one
  account with his overlapping interviews, not independent corroboration.

All company values, proceeds, income, biographies, spiritual beliefs, and
life judgments remain attributed. The chapter makes no causal claim from one
speaker's wealth to happiness, faith, purpose, or civic virtue.

## Contradictions Resolved

- Money can be decisive under scarcity and yield diminishing direct benefit
  after security without becoming trivial at either stage.
- A target can focus effort while failing as an identity or reason for living.
- Pleasure can enrich life while remaining insufficient as a durable account
  of happiness.
- Challenge can support growth or become compulsion; its aftermath and human
  cost distinguish the two.
- Faith can organize a speaker's meaning without becoming proof, prescription,
  or explanation of financial success.
- Gratitude can alter attention without curing illness, erasing injustice, or
  causing external events.
- Private success uses public and social infrastructure; legal retention alone
  does not exhaust questions of duty.
- Enough can change across life stages while still imposing present financial
  and ethical boundaries.

## Figures

No figure is accepted. Sources 3, 4, and 90 contain interview staging, luxury
objects, generic host shots, and speaker portraits rather than visual evidence
needed to understand the argument. A decorative closing portrait would imply
that one speaker embodies the book's plural answer. The enough statement is
more useful and legible as text.
