# Chapter 22 Review

## Decision

Accepted for the working manuscript. The chapter completes the move from
freedom as a financial threshold to freedom as usable agency. It allows rest,
leisure, and departure to be valid outcomes, while showing why post-exit work
can also be meaningful when its process, terms, and burdens remain chosen.

## Narrative Role

- Opens with Glenn Boyd's retirement, family travel, boredom, and ordinary
  return to startup work rather than another display of exit wealth.
- Separates freedom from a pressure from freedom to live differently.
- Makes recovery a legitimate first use without prescribing permanent leisure.
- Uses Mike Kessner's later-career coaching, family-time practice, solitude,
  home, and retirement idea to show several non-equivalent uses of agency.
- Tests chosen work through the ordinary-Tuesday and multi-year-process tests.
- Keeps presence, care, and reciprocal consent visible in family, friendship,
  employment, investment, and service.
- Produces a freedom portfolio across rest, health, people, learning, making,
  service, place, and chosen obligations.
- Turns to Chapter 23 by treating freedom as room whose contents still require
  a judgment about enough.

## Contradictions Resolved

- Financial security reduces real coercion while no balance can choose a
  purpose.
- Leaving can be a valid exercise of agency or an unexamined escape from
  difficulty, grief, or identity.
- Rest can restore a life while endless leisure can become empty for a
  particular person.
- Boredom can reveal a wanted activity without proving that everyone needs
  paid work, a company, or constant production.
- Chosen work can remain demanding while preserving the rights to alter scope,
  share authority, refuse growth, and stop.
- A family home can perform poorly as an investment and well as a home.
- Travel can support markets, learning, family memory, or status; the purchase
  alone does not determine its use.
- Service can express responsibility while also becoming identity theater or
  control over the recipient.
- Freedom includes the ability to accept some obligations, provided the people
  affected can question, refuse, and receive fair treatment.

## Figures

No figure is accepted. Sources 83, 123, and 133 have no validated visual
evidence, equation, object, or process that would deepen the chapter beyond the
verified transcript. A yacht, private jet, interview pose, coffee routine, or
generic family still would turn context into decoration. The freedom portfolio
is more legible as a structured textual exercise than as an ornamental diagram.
