## Contradiction And Usefulness

- Control is separated into legal ownership, economic participation, and
  decision power; the three can diverge.
- Bottlenecks and centralization can improve cost, speed, quality, and learning
  while concentrating outages and enabling unfair extraction.
- Integration is evaluated as avoided margin, coordination, control, learning,
  capital, fixed cost, management complexity, and concentration risk. A visible
  supplier margin is not presumed available to an owner.
- Founder control can protect a long promise or entrench power. Entity form can
  organize risk but cannot erase conduct, guarantees, duties, or jurisdiction.
- An acquisition transfers obligations and failure modes alongside access.
  Employee knowledge, customers, permissions, licenses, accounts, and founder
  trust may not survive closing.
- The nine-field control map ends with a choice among ownership, contract,
  diversification, backup, redesign, and explicit acceptance of dependence.

## Figure Decision

- Sources 6, 11, 22, 25, 27, 31, 47, 51, and 55 have no validated documentary
  asset that materially clarifies the rights-and-control argument.
- Source 53's available candidate is a generic interview frame carrying a
  twenty-five-million-dollar text claim. It neither documents the acquisition
  mechanism nor improves the surrounding analysis and is rejected.
- Chapter 14 is deliberately figure-free; no figure-ledger entry is created for
  rejected or absent assets.
