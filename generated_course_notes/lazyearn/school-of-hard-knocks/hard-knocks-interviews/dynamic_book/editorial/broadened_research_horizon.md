# Broadened Research Horizon

This pass tested local philosophy, literature, and physics collections against
the accepted book. The interview corpus remains the narrative and evidentiary
spine. External material enters only when it supplies a missing distinction or
mechanism.

## Accepted Connections

| Source | Verified idea | Book use | Why it earns space |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Laozi*, chapters 32, 33, 44, and 46 | Sufficiency and stopping are related disciplines; unchecked desire can make enough recede. | Chapter 23, opening definition of enough | Distinguishes recognizing sufficiency from protecting it through a stopping practice. |
| *Mencius*, *King Hui of Liang I* and *Teng Wen Gong I* | A stable livelihood supports steadiness of conduct; survival pressure narrows practical moral agency. | Chapter 23, material floor | Prevents contentment from becoming a moral defense of deprivation. |
| Statistical-mechanics notes, entropy and reversibility sections | A system's microscopic reversibility does not make ordinary macroscopic loss practically recoverable; constraints and lost information matter. | Chapter 21, reversible and irreversible costs | Produces a concrete risk test without importing a forced physics metaphor into reader-facing prose. |

## Editorial Rule

Future breadth passes should identify the exact unresolved question before
opening an outside source. If the source cannot alter a decision, reveal a
boundary, or explain a mechanism more clearly than the interviews already do,
it belongs in research memory rather than the manuscript.
