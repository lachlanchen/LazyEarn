## Focused Research Synthesis

The accepted survival-before-return card supplies the distinction among loss
capacity, willingness, and necessity; the four capital jobs; the household's
nonfinancial balance sheet; hidden correlations; and the written burden for a
concentrated exception.

Focused readings of Benjamin Graham, John Bogle, and J. L. Collins contribute
price-versus-claim discipline, aggregate cost arithmetic, broad ownership,
selection limits, margin for error, and a policy that the owner can actually
hold. Their anecdotes, slogans, named products, fixed allocations, withdrawal
rules, and United States-specific account instructions are not imported.

The statistical-mechanics reading sharpens one epistemic boundary: an expected
distribution is not the path one household receives. No physical law,
thermodynamic term, or decorative physics analogy appears in the reader text.

## V3 Explanatory Synthesis

The chapter moves from the household floor to four capital jobs: continuity,
compounding, creation, and opportunity. These are functions rather than
products or percentages. Broad low-cost productive ownership is the default
for suitable long-horizon household capital; concentration requires written
evidence, disconfirmation, a maximum exposure, and a survivable complete-loss
path.

The industrial-property success, Austin marked loss, and New York margin call
form one three-path test. They distinguish informed operating concentration,
willingness to remain exposed, capacity to bear loss, and a creditor's power
to end the waiting period. Anne Mahlum's intended securities-backed borrowing
shows the same forced-sale mechanism before realization.

Record 4 receives one household risk map covering people and purpose, dates and
currencies, capital jobs, capacity/willingness/necessity, correlations,
concentration exceptions, early/delayed/inflation/permanent-loss paths, and
operating rules. It is an educational decision record, not personalized
investment, tax, legal, insurance, pension, or product advice.
