## Focused Research Synthesis

The survival-before-return card supplies the boundary inherited from Chapter
18: a household must fund the path it receives, not merely prefer the expected
result. Chapter 19 narrows that idea to dated obligations and resources that are
actually controlled. It preserves cash's inflation and opportunity cost while
keeping conditional credit, illiquid value, and uncertain receipts outside
cash-on-demand.

No current product, tax, legal, banking, insurance, or fixed reserve rule is
introduced. Reserve duration follows dated claims, income dependence,
collection delay, care, health, seasonality, and the time required to reduce
cost without breaking promises.

## V3 Explanatory Synthesis

The chapter treats interruption as two clocks: obligations continue while the
person or company diagnoses the shock. Four reserve jobs assign controlled cash
to household continuity, operating runway, committed projects, or opportunity;
they are functions rather than mandatory accounts or percentages, and one
dollar cannot perform two jobs at once.

The long-cycle career bridge now has both a cash test and a future-capability
test. Review evidence, maximum duration, health, relationships, skill, trust,
and judgment determine whether the arrangement extends, changes, or ends.
Record 4 receives one interruption map covering dated claims, controlled and
incoming cash, reserve jobs, conditional access, shock paths, recovery
capacity, replenishment, and release. Record 1 changes only when the protected
floor or capability plan genuinely changes.

## Figure Decision

Chapter 19 remains figure-free. The accepted visual reviews contain talking
heads, property glamour, cash graphics, and generic crisis imagery rather than
documentary evidence of account control, credit conditions, payroll duties,
bridge work, or reserve assignment. A decorative frame would weaken rather
than clarify the argument.
