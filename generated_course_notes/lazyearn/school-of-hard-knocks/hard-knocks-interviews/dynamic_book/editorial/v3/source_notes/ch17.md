## Current Primary-Source Boundary

The chapter names no current rate, limit, down payment, or eligibility rule.
Its narrower statements were checked on 2026-08-26 against official sources:

- HUD identifies the Single Family Housing Policy Handbook 4000.1 as its
  consolidated current FHA policy source and maintains dated revisions:
  https://www.hud.gov/hud-partners/single-family-handbook-4000-1
- The current VA Lender's Handbook requires owner occupancy for a multiunit
  property and applies documented income, reserve, and management conditions:
  https://www.benefits.va.gov/WARMS/docs/admin26/m26-07/Lender_Handbook_VA_Pamphlet_Complete.pdf
- SBA describes 7(a) guarantees as protection available to participating
  lenders after borrower default, while loan terms, collateral, servicing, and
  liquidation remain operative:
  https://www.sba.gov/sba-lenders/
- SBA Form 148 states that qualifying owners may be required to give an
  unlimited personal guaranty:
  https://www.sba.gov/document/sba-form-148-unconditional-guarantee

These sources support only the chapter's boundary: a public insurance or
guarantee program changes a financing stack but does not erase occupancy,
underwriting, repayment, collateral, reserve, fee, or guarantee questions.
Readers must review the current official program and qualified local advice for
a real transaction.

## V3 Explanatory Synthesis

The chapter joins Chapter 16's number address to three clocks: operation from
delivery to collection, contract from payment through maturity, and recovery
through repair, refinance, or sale. Collected cash, reserve, and collateral are
kept as distinct protections.

The simplified NOI, owner-cash, stress-coverage, uses-and-sources, and refinance
expressions are educational decision bridges. They are not accounting
definitions, appraisals, legal opinions, tax advice, or personalized lending
recommendations. Record 4 receives one integrated leverage decision covering
the right and operation, cash clock, capital stack, stress path, recovery clock,
protected boundaries, and the choice to buy, change terms, or walk away.

The chapter deliberately includes reasons not to borrow. Slower growth,
rental, leasing, prepayment, retained earnings, shared ownership, and no
transaction remain valid alternatives when leverage would consume freedom.
