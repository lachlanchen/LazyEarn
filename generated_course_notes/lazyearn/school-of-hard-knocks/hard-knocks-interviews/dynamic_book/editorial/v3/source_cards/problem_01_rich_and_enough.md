# Problem 01: What Rich Means, and How Enough Becomes Operational

Status: focused close read accepted; Chapters 1, 22, and 23 implementation
accepted for V3.

## Question

How can the book treat wealth as materially serious without making a bank
balance answer questions of freedom, fulfillment, happiness, or moral worth?
What practice can keep `enough` from becoming either a slogan for deprivation
or a finish line that moves whenever the comparison group changes?

## Synthesis

The sources converge on three different jobs that are often hidden inside the
word `rich`.

1. **Material sufficiency** protects food, shelter, health, care, and the
   ability to survive a shock. Boyd's testimony and Mencius make it impossible
   to romanticize scarcity.
2. **Practical freedom** is stored capacity to wait, refuse, recover, learn,
   care, or choose work. It depends on liquidity, low enough fixed claims,
   capabilities, relationships, and control over some time, not only net worth.
3. **Governed desire** prevents every gain from creating a larger compulsory
   life. Housel diagnoses the moving comparison group; the *Daodejing*
   separates knowing sufficiency from stopping; Krishnamurti examines the
   conversion of an enjoyed moment into a demand for repetition.

This supports the existing abundance, fulfillment, and contentment triad, but
adds an operational loop. A one-page definition of enough is necessary and
insufficient. The reader also needs evidence from ordinary spending and time:

- What did this expense or ambition actually contribute after novelty faded?
- How many hours of earning, recovery, maintenance, and attention did it cost?
- Did it support a named relationship, capability, purpose, or source of joy?
- Would the demand shrink if the work required to finance or compensate for it
  were no longer compulsory?
- Is the next increment serving a new job, or merely renewing comparison and a
  remembered feeling?

The loop should be observational rather than punitive. Its purpose is to expose
the full exchange, not to make austerity morally superior.

## Contradictions and Limits

- Boyd gives a truthful personal distinction, not a universal income threshold.
  Health, dependents, geography, institutions, safety, and volatility alter the
  material floor.
- Housel's cases explain mechanisms but remain selected narratives. His
  strongest contribution here is the risk boundary and comparison mechanism,
  not a formula for happiness.
- *Your Money or Your Life* can understate how care work, disability, low wages,
  and structural constraints limit an individual's ability to redesign work.
  Its exercises must be adapted, not copied as a complete program.
- Mencius addresses rulers and agrarian livelihood. The durable insight is that
  material conditions affect available agency, not that ancient policy maps
  directly onto a modern household.
- Krishnamurti's claim that thought necessarily turns enjoyment into pleasure
  is philosophically stronger than this book can establish. Use the narrower
  observable mechanism: remembered rewards and comparison can create repeated
  pursuit even when experienced value declines.
- Contentment must never be used by an employer, government, creditor, or
  wealthy observer to tell a materially insecure person to accept less.

## V3 Decisions

1. Keep Boyd and the conflicting interview testimony as the narrative center.
2. Preserve the five-part architecture and current triad; no new philosophy
   chapter is justified.
3. Add a compact recurring `enough audit` to Chapters 1 and 23, adapted in an
   original form from the close reading above.
4. Let Chapter 21 use the stronger boundary: never expose a needed,
   irreplaceable good to ruin for an optional increment.
5. Add chapter-specific source notes in V3 so these influences are traceable
   without burdening the narrative with authority names.
6. Do not insert the Gupta, Madoff, or literary party anecdotes from Housel;
   the interview corpus already supplies the human spine and the mechanism can
   be explained without replacing it.

## Continuity Test

This card strengthens the book's opening contract rather than changing its
destination: define the life and material floor first, build useful capability
and ownership next, protect the resulting freedom from ruin, then decide what
the surplus is for. The next cards must test whether the book's practical route
to that freedom is economically and operationally credible.
