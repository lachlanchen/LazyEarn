# Problem 07: Who Bears the Residual Loss

Status: focused close read accepted for V3 architecture; manuscript change
deferred until the human-cost card is reconciled with Chapter 20.

## Question

Which losses should a household or company refuse, prevent, retain, fund,
insure, allocate by contract, or contain within an entity? When a transaction
is lawful and profitable, what further questions reveal whether its cost has
merely been moved to a worker, customer, family, community, counterparty, or
future self who had less information or power?

## Synthesis

Wealth creation organizes both an upside and a downside. The upside usually has
an owner, a price, a celebration, and a visible account. The downside is easier
to scatter. It can appear as a worker's unsafe visit, a customer's hidden risk,
a spouse's guarantee, a community's unpaid cost, an insurer's exclusion, a
supplier's delayed invoice, a future tax bill, or a founder's lost health. A
good shell does not make these losses disappear. It makes them harder to hide
and harder to assign to a person who did not knowingly accept them.

There are five different responses to a material risk:

1. **Refuse it** when the activity violates a right or creates a harm no price,
   waiver, or expected return makes acceptable.
2. **Prevent or reduce it** by changing the product, site, procedure, timing,
   training, authority, staffing, information, or promise.
3. **Retain and fund it** when the exposed party can understand, control, and
   absorb the remaining loss without violating another duty.
4. **Transfer a defined part** through a contract or insurance policy whose
   trigger, limit, exclusions, counterparty, and enforcement are understood.
5. **Separate and contain it** in an entity with coherent ownership, accounts,
   authority, capital, records, and operations where law permits.

Order matters. A company should not insure a preventable hazard and call the
work safe. It should not place an abusive promise in a separate entity and call
the conduct responsible. Transfer and containment begin only after the
activity, prevention, disclosure, and consent survive scrutiny.

Every consequential structure must pass three tests that cannot substitute for
one another:

- **Legal permissibility** asks what current law, regulation, contract, policy,
  duty, jurisdiction, date, and facts allow or require.
- **Economic soundness** asks what cash enters and leaves, when, under what
  uncertainty, with what opportunity cost, and whether the structure still
  works when the favorable treatment or outcome fails.
- **Ethical legitimacy** asks who gains, who decides, who bears the loss, who
  knew, who consented, who could refuse or stop, which rights are non-tradable,
  and what repair is available.

A lawful choice can be economically foolish. A profitable choice can be
unlawful. A lawful and profitable choice can still depend on concealed harm or
unequal power. Conversely, an emotionally attractive decision can waste
resources needed for duties it claims to honor. The three tests should meet in
one file without being collapsed into one score.

This is the value of consequence tracing. Follow the visible cash and the use
that no longer occurs. Follow the immediate beneficiary and the later claimant.
Follow every group affected, including the one absent from the negotiation.
Hazlitt's seen-and-unseen discipline is useful, but not sufficient: an unseen
cost may be an opportunity forgone, and it may also be a right violated, a
voice excluded, a public institution depleted, or a risk placed on someone who
cannot bargain. Long-run aggregate benefit does not excuse callousness toward
the identifiable person who is harmed now.

Quantification remains necessary. Prices, probabilities, reserves, limits,
deductibles, claims, wages, taxes, and remediation costs constrain wishful
thinking. Refusing all calculation can conceal a choice just as effectively as
bad calculation. But a spreadsheet does not decide which values belong in one
sum. Dignity, informed consent, basic safety, and the distinction between
persons establish floors. Above those floors, transparent tradeoffs can be
compared. Below them, a favorable aggregate does not purchase permission.

The Austin field story makes the sequence concrete. A pregnant person should
not be sent into a volatile property encounter merely because the company has
liability insurance or because the visit is common in that line of work. The
first questions concern screening, remote contact, paired visits, local
support, stop authority, escalation, and whether the visit should occur. Only
after prevention comes a policy's actual response to a defined remaining loss.
The person exposed to the hazard needs both information and the power to stop.

An entity can then perform valuable containment. It names the person that made
the promise, holds the asset, employs the worker, pays the tax, maintains the
records, and bears a specified liability. Separate accounts and authority make
truth inspectable. Adequate capital gives the promise substance. None of this
turns an entity into a moral or legal force field. A shell cannot erase one's
own wrongdoing, an unwaivable duty, a personal guarantee, a fraudulent
transfer, or harm created by operating the entity as an empty name. Exact
consequences vary with jurisdiction and fact.

A contract has the same limitation. It should state scope, evidence,
acceptance, price, timing, change, safety, confidentiality, intellectual
property, insurance, indemnity, liability, termination, transition, dispute,
and remedy in proportion to the deal. That makes the promise and its failure
legible. It does not make every allocation fair. Consent is weak when one side
lacks the information, language, time, alternative, or power to refuse. Nor can
an indemnity collect from a counterparty that has no assets, coverage, or
survival when the loss arrives.

Insurance transfers only the loss described by the policy. A real protection
decision begins with the person or capability that must survive and the amount
and duration of the need. Product comes later. Record the insured, trigger,
limit, retention, exclusions, conditions, territory, reporting rule, defense
cost, insurer, premium path, lapse or surrender consequence, guarantees, and
the loss that remains. If a product mixes protection, saving, investment, tax,
and borrowing, evaluate each function and each cost separately. A sincere
seller can still have an incentive, and a market need does not establish one
product's suitability.

Tax belongs inside the same discipline. A deduction can change the after-tax
cost or the period in which cost is recognized; it does not make spending free.
Depreciation for tax and decline in market value are different records. A paper
loss does not pay interest, repairs, insurance, or principal. An entity election
can alter employment-tax treatment and also add payroll, filing, compensation,
state, eligibility, and administrative requirements. A foundation can direct
resources to public work and cannot treat charitable assets as the founder's
personal account.

The interview contrasts cannot be resolved by applauding the smallest tax
number. Dennis's claims concern a network of income, investments, entities,
deductions, and gifts. Harpin's reported payment concerns a company sale after
decades of building. The UAE operator describes residence and a different
fiscal system. Law asks what was due. Economics asks how a rule changes action
and who funds the surrounding system. Ethics asks what people owe institutions
and one another after accounting for the value they created and the conditions
they inherited. These are linked arguments, not one calculation.

The fair-start discussion changes the tone of “self-made.” Agency, work,
judgment, and courage are real. So are family, education, health, peace,
infrastructure, customers, workers, law, currency, timing, natural ability, and
luck. A high reward can be useful because it encourages valuable activity; it
does not prove that the recipient authored every condition that made the
reward possible. This supports neither contempt for success nor guilt about
wealth. It supports gratitude, accurate attribution, fair dealing, and a duty
not to privatize every gain while exporting every loss.

The *Mozi* readings press the same question from another direction: would we
accept the arrangement if another household's food, clothing, labor, or safety
were as visible to us as our own? That is a powerful test against luxury
financed by hidden extraction. It is not a command to abolish beauty, play,
craft, celebration, or unequal reward. Those goods can contribute to a rich
life. The test is whether their cost is honestly paid, their labor dignified,
and their pursuit compatible with the sufficiency and agency of others.

Before accepting a material risk, write a residual-loss map:

1. Name the activity, promised value, upside recipient, and decision owner.
2. Describe each plausible loss in money, time, health, safety, dignity,
   privacy, relationship, environment, and lost alternative.
3. Identify every exposed person, including people outside the contract, and
   record what each knew, chose, controlled, and could refuse.
4. State the right, duty, or safety floor that cannot be traded for expected
   return.
5. Record how the risk will be refused, prevented, detected, stopped, and
   repaired before considering transfer.
6. Write the entity, contract, policy, guarantee, collateral, jurisdiction,
   evidence, and adviser on which any transfer depends.
7. Name the residual bearer after every limit, exclusion, insolvency, delay,
   denial, or failed assumption.
8. Fund that residual with capital and capability that remain available when
   correlated losses occur.
9. Give an exposed person a route to report, pause, appeal, and obtain remedy
   without retaliation.
10. Re-run the three tests when facts, law, ownership, scale, location, or
    bargaining power changes.

This is not a promise that every harm can be anticipated or insured. It is a
refusal to let “the company,” “the market,” “the policy,” or “the contract”
become a grammatical device that hides the person left with the loss. The best
shell makes responsibility harder to evade.

## Contradictions and Limits

- Prevention can be expensive and imperfect. A zero-risk requirement can
  eliminate valuable work, mobility, medicine, experimentation, or access. The
  decision still needs proportionate evidence, a rights floor, and a named
  residual bearer.
- Cost-benefit analysis can expose hidden tradeoffs and flatten incomparable
  goods into a number. Neither automatic maximization nor refusal to calculate
  is an adequate decision rule.
- Consent does not legitimate fraud, coercion, non-waivable harm, or a bargain
  made without material information. It also does not require equal outcomes
  or eliminate every difficult voluntary trade.
- Legal compliance is a floor, not proof of wisdom or fairness. Ethical
  intuition is not a substitute for current law where another person's rights,
  tax, insurance, employment, safety, or regulated claim is involved.
- A separate entity can contain specified liabilities and can be expensive,
  badly operated, undercapitalized, ignored by law, or used to obscure control.
- A contract can allocate a risk and cannot guarantee performance,
  collectability, informed consent, or justice. More pages do not repair a bad
  bargain or an unsafe product.
- Insurance protects against defined uncertainty and introduces premium,
  exclusion, lapse, insurer, claims-handling, inflation, and suitability risk.
  No interview establishes a product recommendation.
- A tax deduction can be lawful and still motivate unnecessary spending. A
  high tax payment can support public institutions and does not by itself prove
  civic virtue, legal compliance, or an efficient policy.
- Residence, source, entity, ownership, transaction, and date can change tax
  treatment. Interview headlines and historical publications cannot establish
  a reader's current result.
- Unequal starts and luck limit moral-desert claims without making effort,
  skill, contribution, incentive, or responsibility imaginary.
- Impartial concern can expose hidden exploitation and become coercive when an
  authority claims to define everyone's benefit. Modern rights, plural goods,
  and voice constrain the *Mozi* utility test.
- Long-run and all-group analysis can reveal displaced costs and become an
  excuse to sacrifice a visible minority for a speculative aggregate gain.
- Public goods, externalities, monopoly, information asymmetry, unpaid care,
  and power cannot always be inferred from a private transaction's price.
- Duties differ among owner, director, officer, adviser, professional,
  employer, insurer, lender, parent, and citizen. The map identifies questions;
  qualified current advice resolves the applicable rule.

## V3 Decisions

1. Keep Chapter 20, “The Shell Around the Risk,” and its tax-story contrast.
   It already refuses both tax-hack imitation and moral grandstanding.
2. Make the chapter explicitly distinguish legal permissibility, economic
   soundness, and ethical legitimacy. Do not let any one test claim the work of
   the other two.
3. Preserve the deduction arithmetic, documentation case, entity job,
   contract fields, adviser disclosure, and insurance definition. Verify every
   current U.S. tax statement against primary sources before V3 publication.
4. Retain Dennis's examples only as attributed claims with missing facts. Fix
   any mistaken statutory shorthand rather than repeating it, and do not
   publish thresholds, rates, or product instructions that can age silently.
5. Keep Harpin and the UAE operator as contrasting civic positions, not heroes
   and villains. Separate tax due, economic policy, community contribution,
   and ethical judgment.
6. Add the sequence refuse, prevent, retain, transfer, contain to the existing
   shell framework. Do not duplicate the chapter with a second long checklist;
   fold missing consent, stop, residual-bearer, capital, and repair fields into
   the final exercise.
7. Use the insurance seller to expose the difference among protection need,
   product mechanics, and seller incentive. Do not resolve term versus
   permanent insurance for the reader.
8. Cross-reference the Austin incident as a prevention failure. Do not repeat
   the spectacle, include an image, or suggest that insurance made the visit
   acceptable.
9. Use consequence tracing, impartial concern, rights, and fair-start reasoning
   as invisible intellectual structure. Avoid ornamental philosopher names,
   historical case figures, slogans, and an ideological policy detour.
10. Let Chapter 21 inherit the nonfinancial residuals: health, finite time,
    trust, relationship, dignity, and meaning. Chapter 20 should establish that
    these goods cannot be repaired merely by moving money after the loss.

## Continuity Test

The preceding card refused to expose a necessary life to an optional return.
This card asks what happens when risk leaves the spreadsheet and reaches a
person. A responsible shell prevents what it can, makes every promise and
transfer inspectable, preserves consent and stop rights, and names the person
who still bears the loss. The next card must examine losses no entity can fully
contain: health, love, finite attention, dignity, and the years ambition cannot
buy back.
