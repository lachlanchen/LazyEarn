## Controlling Evidence

The drafting order is governed by four linked records:

1. The accepted V2 `chapter_contracts.json` and `source_coverage.csv` retain the
   complete interview allocation, source anchors, qualifications, audience
   questions, and explicit rejections.
2. The nine V3 problem cards and curriculum audit define what must become
   deeper, more accurate, or more useful.
3. `decision_placement.csv` assigns every accepted V3 decision to prose,
   source notes, current-claims review, figures, exclusions, or a global gate.
4. `recurring_case_map.csv` controls when a person may return and what new work
   that return must perform.

No outside book displaces an interview opening merely because its language is
cleaner. Outside sources may explain a mechanism, reveal a contradiction, or
set a boundary. Distinctive influence is traced in source notes; ornamental
names, borrowed anecdotes, quotation mosaics, and forced physics metaphors do
not enter the narrative.

## Governing Movement

The book follows one practical argument:

1. Define the life and material floor that money is meant to support.
2. Find and prove a useful change another person will voluntarily choose.
3. Turn repeated value into an accountable operating and ownership system.
4. Give capital enough margin to survive time, error, interruption, and loss.
5. Use and transfer freedom without allowing accumulation to choose the life.

Abundance, fulfillment, and contentment are not three closing themes. They are
three tests applied throughout the sequence:

- **Abundance / 富足:** Does this decision enlarge real capability, resilience,
  time, relationship, and choice beyond chronic scarcity?
- **Fulfillment / 满足:** Does the work serve a purpose worth its full effort and
  cost?
- **Contentment / 知足:** Can the person still recognize enough, revise the game,
  or stop without losing identity?

Rich remains an explicit material question. The book explains income, value,
ownership, capital, and preservation seriously before asking what wealth is
for. It is neither anti-wealth nor a promise that one visible route will work
for every reader.

## Voice and Continuity

- Write in one candid authorial voice. Interviewees remain people reporting
  experience, not mascots, authorities, or a sequence of profiles.
- Introduce a recurring person once. A later appearance carries only the new
  decision named in `recurring_case_map.csv`.
- Preserve uncertainty and contradiction beside the attractive claim. Ask
  what worked, for whom, under what conditions, at whose cost, and where the
  lesson stops.
- Explain a mechanism before giving it a label or equation. Define every term
  and keep reported company value, revenue, profit, cash, equity, and personal
  proceeds separate.
- Use questions and exercises as decisions within the argument, not as a
  workbook appended to otherwise finished prose.
- End each chapter by changing the next question. Do not end with a mechanical
  summary, slogan stack, or preview paragraph.
- Keep research operations, file paths, model language, and editorial
  workflow out of reader-facing prose.

## Chapter Contracts

### Part I: What Money Is For

### 1. A Number Cannot Tell You What Rich Means

**Governing question.** What must money continue to make possible before a
large number can count as wealth?

**Narrative work.** Let Kenn Ricci's spending ratio meet Glenn Boyd's account
of poverty relief and diminishing luxury. Tony Stephens's finite ribbon makes
time part of the balance without becoming a mortality forecast.

**V3 work.** Separate income, revenue, profit, company value, equity,
liquidity, security, autonomy, status, and happiness. Keep Boyd central but
attribute the company transaction rather than implying personal proceeds.
Open a compact enough audit expressed as capabilities, obligations, protected
goods, and time. No universal threshold or imported wealth anecdote survives.

**Notebook update.** Record 1 opens with the material floor, current claims on
money, protected goods, and the first enough statement.

**Handoff.** A definition of enough is useful only after the reader sees the
starting position from which it must be built.

### 2. The Unequal Starting Line

**Governing question.** What can agency mean when access, safety, health,
family, institutions, timing, and luck are unequal?

**Narrative work.** Preserve the accepted V2 opportunity cases. Tom Brady's
mentors and deliberate practice enter only as one example of effort working
through access, not as proof that access was irrelevant.

**V3 work.** Keep geography, migration, education, identity, family labor,
credit, peace, and institutions visible beside resourcefulness. Distinguish a
controllable move from a constraint that requires allies, policy, protection,
or time. Do not convert winner testimony into a moral ranking.

**Notebook update.** Record 1 adds an opportunity inventory: usable resources,
missing access, constraints, allies, and one bounded next move.

**Handoff.** The most portable response to an unequal start is capability that
can produce evidence beyond one employer or place.

### 3. Skills, Work, and the First Margin

**Governing question.** How does practiced capability become enough income and
margin to create a real choice?

**Narrative work.** Retain the accepted work, apprenticeship, tool, and
household cases. Use the Chicago great-grandchild horizon and high-income
overspending example only to show that horizon and spending matter; reject its
employment hierarchy, gendered luxury framing, and unaudited arithmetic.

**V3 work.** Sequence a stable floor, useful skill, buyer feedback, income,
high-cost debt, reserve, and first surplus. Saving cannot repair inadequate
income, and course or equipment consumption is not skill until it changes
demonstrable output.

**Notebook update.** Record 1 names one capability, the evidence it must
produce, the buyer of that output, the first-margin target, and the household
agreement needed to protect it.

**Handoff.** Margin creates optionality; Chapter 4 decides which option belongs
to the desired life.

### 4. Choose the Life Before the Income

**Governing question.** What ordinary life should the income machine preserve?

**Narrative work.** Mike Kessner's financially unremarkable family home and
quantity of time make nonfinancial value concrete. Glenn Boyd may return only
to test the daily life created by the company, not to repeat his sale story.

**V3 work.** Compare present output with future capability while refusing to
call a person or relationship an asset. Replace no-Plan-B doctrine with
bounded attention, protected floors, seasons, and revision dates. Marriage
accounts show aligned aspirations only; they do not prescribe a household
form or turn intimacy into an instrument.

**Notebook update.** Record 1 defines the desired ordinary week, protected
relationships and health, tolerable uncertainty, capability investment, and a
date at which the life may change its mind.

**Handoff.** With purpose and constraints visible, the book can ask what is
worth making for someone else.

### Part II: Make Something Valuable

### 5. Find the Expensive Problem

**Governing question.** Which burden is painful, frequent, urgent, or costly
enough for a reachable decision-maker to change behavior?

**Narrative work.** Keep John Abraham, Chris Meroff, Hill, and the accepted V2
problem cases as the human spine. Meroff's losing revival supplies a warning:
devotion can deepen a weak premise.

**V3 work.** Open one strategy-choice record: chosen customer and need,
alternative, promised difference, non-goals, capabilities, operating
requirements, economic logic, and evidence that would change the choice.
Define position through a particular buyer, actual reference alternative,
promised change, and credible proof. Label user, payer, approver, champion,
retailer, and regulator by the decision each controls.

**Notebook update.** Record 2 receives the problem, roles, alternative,
promise, proof burden, non-goals, and first disconfirming observation.

**Handoff.** A chosen problem is still a claim; observation and commitment
must decide whether it survives.

### 6. Listen Before You Build

**Governing question.** What evidence should be allowed to change the solution
before delivery becomes expensive?

**Narrative work.** Abraham's decision-maker interviews and pilot request
advance the evidence chain. Interview cases remain primary; startup books
provide only conversation and experiment discipline.

**V3 work.** Use one evidence ladder: specific past behavior, workaround,
costly commitment, bounded pilot, viable delivery, repeated exchange. Add an
unbranded experiment contract with claim, prediction, boundary, observation,
disconfirmation, and next decision. No role validates a decision held by a
different role, and customers reveal evidence without choosing company
strategy.

**Notebook update.** Record 2 updates the evidence rung, role-specific result,
what failed, and the strategy field that changed.

**Handoff.** Evidence that a problem exists does not yet establish what the
change is worth or what promise can be defended.

### 7. Price the Outcome

**Governing question.** What change is the buyer choosing, compared with which
real alternative, and under what uncertainty?

**Narrative work.** Preserve the accepted value and regulatory-pathway cases.
Use outcomes, avoided loss, time, fit, and capability without pretending that
all value can be measured exactly.

**V3 work.** Carry positioning into price through buyer, reference
alternative, promised change, and proof. Open one reciprocal exchange record
that separates the decision, evidence step, commercial terms, and consent.
State duties created by the price; uncertainty and buyer difference remain
visible.

**Notebook update.** Record 2 adds value range, assumptions, buyer-specific
terms, proof, duties, and the smallest mutually useful next step.

**Handoff.** A value case becomes knowledge only when another person can
accept, question, or refuse it.

### 8. Sales Is a Test of Reality

**Governing question.** What does a voluntary exchange prove, and what does it
leave unknown?

**Narrative work.** Return to Meroff only to qualify the claim that sales are
the sole indicator. Keep accepted cases of direct asking, rejection,
adaptation, truthful explanation, and stopping after a clear no.

**V3 work.** Complete the reciprocal exchange record. A sale validates only
the decision, person, terms, evidence, and delivery reproduced in that sale.
It does not prove scale, margin, collected cash, retention, safety, or
durability. Preparation never creates presumed consent, and urgency may state
real consequences but may not manufacture fear.

**Notebook update.** Record 2 records the exchange, consent, objections,
disconfirming result, delivery obligation, and next evidence step. It absorbs
the old sales review rather than adding a second checklist.

**Handoff.** One exchange creates an obligation; the next question is how
attention, delivery, collection, return, and repair become a system.

### 9. Distribution Opens the Door; Trust Keeps It Open

**Governing question.** How does attention travel through a complete promise
and return as remembered trust?

**Narrative work.** Keep the accepted channel, service, reputation, pivot, and
recovery cases. The story follows the work performed by each channel, not a
parade of platforms.

**V3 work.** Update position from actual response. Draw one attention-to-cash
operating flow: attention, qualified decision, exchange, delivery, acceptance,
collection, return, referral, complaint, and repair. Name the constraint,
waiting time, and person carrying each delay. A bottleneck limits the whole
promise; it is not permission to maximize one local activity.

**Notebook update.** Record 2 closes with channel and trust evidence, then
hands the operating-flow identifier and unresolved constraint to Record 3.

**Handoff.** Once value repeats, the reader must ask who owns the claim and who
inherits its duties.

### Part III: Own the Machine

### 10. From Skill to Ownership

**Governing question.** Which precise claim can extend participation beyond
the next hour worked without erasing the labor and duties beneath it?

**Narrative work.** Retain the Miami hand surgeon opening. Surgical wages,
reported operating income, property, equity, control, liquidity, and business
burden remain separate.

**V3 work.** Define ownership through economics, rights, authority,
liquidity, duties, and downside. Merge the rights, legal, economic, and control
taxonomies into one ledger. Knowledge, accountability, tools, systems, and
capital can extend output; none establishes a hierarchy of human worth or a
guarantee of freedom.

**Notebook update.** Record 3 opens with the operating flow and one ownership
claim, naming rights, return, control, transfer, obligations, liquidity, and
loss.

**Handoff.** A claim that depends on the founder's constant presence remains a
job with an additional risk label.

### 11. A Business That Can Leave the Room

**Governing question.** Can the organization deliver expected work and respond
to an unexpected condition when the founder is absent?

**Narrative work.** Keep the accepted five-company opening. Shaquille O'Neal's
reported franchise scale appears only to make physical presence impossible;
it does not prove holdings arithmetic or delegation quality. Purchase-order,
staged-growth, and Boyd cases remain distinct capacity counterweights.

**V3 work.** Apply a two-part absence test. Expected work needs an invariant,
reason, interface, owner, measure, and inspection. Unexpected conditions need
boundaries, escalation, expiring authority, support, and review. Link
delegation to the evidence ladder, operating flow, growth permission, and
customer contact. Documentation preserves judgment rather than pretending to
remove it.

**Notebook update.** Record 3 adds accountable outcomes, authority, resources,
exceptions, inspection, customer feedback, founder dependence, and permission
to grow.

**Handoff.** A system can deliver and still fail if demand creates a cash gap
larger than the organization can carry.

### 12. Working Capital Is the Price of Growth

**Governing question.** Who finances the delay between demand, delivery,
acceptance, collection, payroll, and repair?

**Narrative work.** Open with the labor-force contract that produced demand
and near-insolvency at the same time. The apparel purchase order, staged
industrial growth, and Boyd's expense warning each reveal a different
constraint. The Arizona founder and Dallas banker enter as unresolved capital
counterexamples.

**V3 work.** Distinguish stock from flow before presenting the weekly cash
calendar and peak-carry equations. Couple demand, contribution, capacity,
cash, duties, and recoverability without dumping the six tests repeatedly.
Quality, safety, fair work, privacy, maintenance, and repair remain real costs
even when they lack attributed revenue. Compare present output with future
capability, then defer the final debt-versus-equity decision to capital fit.

**Notebook update.** Record 3 adds timing, peak carry, capacity, duties,
stress path, stop condition, and the capital-fit question. Record 1 receives
only the changed capability or protected-floor implication.

**Handoff.** The cash carrier is a person or institution with rights; growth
therefore becomes a governance decision.

### 13. The Company Is Other People

**Governing question.** How should contribution, authority, information,
upside, and residual risk be shared among the people carrying the company?

**Narrative work.** Use the Austin partners for staged trust and complementary
roles. Keep the dangerous field incident sober and brief. O'Neal does not
return here; celebrity scale is not the human architecture.

**V3 work.** Add bounded responsibility to the governance charter: outcome,
authority, resources, limits, information, support, review, and remedy. Keep
decision ownership, expiring emergency authority, truthful crisis
communication, dissent, safety, and leader support. Durable shareholder
return is legitimate; automatic shareholder primacy is not. Trace every
materially exposed group without turning leadership into consensus theater.

**Notebook update.** Record 3 names contributions, tests before equity,
decision closure, dissent, emergency authority, safety invariants, invisible
risk, leader support, and repair.

**Handoff.** Distributed judgment raises the next ownership question: which
rights and critical links must remain controlled for the promise to survive?

### 14. Own the Rights and Critical Links

**Governing question.** Which rights, interfaces, bottlenecks, and capital
terms protect the promise, and which merely collect control?

**Narrative work.** Russ's retained masters require precise distinctions among
recording, composition, license, advance, recoupment, and transfer. Return to
the Arizona founder and Dallas banker only to resolve their capital
contradiction through business-specific timing and control.

**V3 work.** Complete the rights ledger and capital-fit record across need,
timing, contribution, rights surrendered, control, guarantees, reporting,
failure, and exit. Integration is justified by coordination and duty, not by
the prestige of owning another business. Bootstrap control and outside capital
remain contingent choices.

**Notebook update.** Record 3 updates rights, critical links, ownership and
control, financing terms, guarantees, information duties, and capital fit.

**Handoff.** Rights and systems are durable only if judgment and authority can
pass to a willing, capable next steward.

### 15. Build Something That Can Outlive You

**Governing question.** What should continue, who may choose to carry it, and
what should be allowed to end?

**Narrative work.** TJ Millionaire opens the possibility that children do not
want the business and an outside buyer may preserve useful work. Jody Lorenzo
opens a planning question, not a trust design. Tim Tebow's distinction between
resources left for someone and capacity cultivated in someone keeps provision
and capability together.

**V3 work.** Separate continuity, succession, transfer, sale, kinship,
ownership, employment, and authority. Use willing capability rather than
blood as the succession test. A family title never substitutes for consent,
competence, accountable authority, or a removal path. Fold the family wealth
compact into existing transferability fields: purpose, protected people,
roles, capabilities, rights, succession paths, support, boundaries, review,
and exit. Current legal design remains outside the chapter without primary
jurisdictional review.

**Notebook update.** Record 3 completes transferability and succession.
Record 5 opens only the family roles, purpose, protected people, and freedom to
decline an inherited occupation.

**Handoff.** Transfer claims depend on numbers; Part IV begins by asking what
each reported number actually measures.

### Part IV: Capital, Risk, and Time

### 16. Know What the Number Means

**Governing question.** What does this number include, exclude, entitle its
holder to, and leave exposed?

**Narrative work.** Preserve the accepted reported-number cases. Introduce the
industrial-property success, Austin paper loss, and New York margin call only
as differently addressed numbers; the risk judgment waits for Chapter 18.

**V3 work.** Give every number an entity, period, category, gross/net status,
ownership share, liquidity, conditions, source, and confidence. Reconcile the
attention-to-cash operating flow to revenue, contribution, working capital,
available cash, debt, and owner distribution. A valuation remains a
proposition, not spendable money.

**Notebook update.** Record 4 opens with the number address and reconciliation
to Record 3's operating flow.

**Handoff.** Once the claim is correctly named, debt can be judged by the cash
and asset expected to carry it.

### 17. Debt Must Be Carried by an Asset

**Governing question.** Can the asset and complete capital stack carry the
obligation through the path received rather than only the expected path?

**Narrative work.** Retain accepted property, acquisition, equipment, rent,
and refinancing cases. Speaker heuristics remain testimony rather than
underwriting instructions.

**V3 work.** Match debt to cash flow, collateral, maturity, rate path,
covenants, guarantees, vacancy or demand loss, maintenance, refinancing, and
recovery. The lender may change while the obligation remains. Every precise
regulated or current claim receives date, jurisdiction, and primary-source
review.

**Notebook update.** Record 4 writes the capital stack, timing, bearer,
guarantees, stress paths, maturity, recovery, and reasons not to borrow.

**Handoff.** An asset may carry debt and still expose too much of the household
to one thesis; Chapter 18 sets the survival boundary.

### 18. Compounding Is Slow; Risk Is Fast

**Governing question.** Which capital can wait, which risk can be survived, and
what evidence could justify an exception to broad ownership?

**Narrative work.** Retain the retired dentist's quiet compounding opening.
Return to the industrial-property winner, Austin concentrated loss, and New
York margin call as a three-path test rather than a contest between confidence
and fear. Jameis Winston's partition remains athlete-specific testimony.

**V3 work.** Let capacity, willingness, and necessity govern four roles:
continuity capital, broad long-horizon ownership, concentrated operating or
expert claims, and explicitly lossable optional bets. State a broad, low-cost
default for suitable long-horizon household capital and the written burden for
concentration. Add human-capital, guarantee, currency, location, platform,
customer, and forced-sale correlations. No named product, fixed allocation,
return promise, withdrawal rule, or thermodynamic metaphor appears.

**Notebook update.** Record 4 assigns each capital role, loss capacity,
willingness, necessity, correlations, concentration exception, disconfirming
evidence, maximum exposure, and permanent-loss path.

**Handoff.** A sound long-horizon claim still fails if short-horizon cash is
needed before the thesis has time to recover.

### 19. Cash Buys the Right to Survive

**Governing question.** Which liquidity protects continuity, recovery,
bargaining power, and future choice?

**Narrative work.** Keep accepted household, operating, project, and
opportunity cases separate. Credit remains conditional capacity, never cash.

**V3 work.** Preserve four reserve roles: household continuity, operating
runway, committed project cash, and opportunity capital. Assign each a job,
owner, trigger, replenishment rule, and boundary. Briefly retest present output
against future capability so reserves do not preserve a system that destroys
the person required to run it.

**Notebook update.** Record 4 assigns reserve roles, access, duration,
replenishment, and interruption paths. Record 1 changes only if the protected
floor or capability plan changes.

**Handoff.** Some losses should not be retained in cash; they must be refused,
prevented, transferred, or contained around the activity.

### 20. The Shell Around the Risk

**Governing question.** Which loss should be refused, prevented, retained,
transferred, or contained, and who bears what remains?

**Narrative work.** Carlton Dennis's tax board opens the file rather than a tax
hack. Richard Harpin and the UAE operator remain contrasting civic positions,
not heroes or villains. The Austin field incident returns only to show that
insurance, an entity, or a contract cannot repair missing prevention.

**V3 work.** Separate legal permissibility, economic soundness, and ethical
legitimacy. Keep arithmetic, records, entities, contracts, adviser incentives,
and insurance definitions, but verify every current tax, legal, employment,
insurance, and regulated claim through primary sources with date and
jurisdiction. The risk-shell record names consent, prevention, bearer,
capital, transfer, limits, repair, stop, and residual loss. Lorenzo may open a
planning question but cannot supply current trust instructions.

**Notebook update.** Record 4 completes the risk response and current-claims
record. Record 5 receives only materially exposed people and harms money cannot
fully repair.

**Handoff.** A financial shell can transfer money; it cannot restore health,
time, trust, dignity, identity, or meaning as interchangeable units.

### Part V: Enough

### 21. The Cost of the Game

**Governing question.** Which costs of ambition are reversible, which are
path-dependent, and which must be protected before optional gain?

**Narrative work.** Open with emergency physician AK's stage-dependent
question while explicitly refusing to attribute cancer to ambition. Tom Brady
returns beside mentorship, recovery, body care, integrity, and accountability.
Josh Terry supplies bounded segments; Mark White supplies creditor
accountability and small promises. Tony Stephens's ribbon returns in prose,
not as a second figure.

**V3 work.** Distinguish reversible, irreversible, non-substitutable, and
path-dependent costs while recognizing that money can fund care, repair, and
restitution. Meaning never justifies avoidable harm; removal and prevention
precede endurance. Extend the cost review with season, renewal date, protected
floor, stop authority, and the people carrying the burden. Current health
language remains concise, global, nonclinical, and bounded by authoritative
guidance. No diagnosis, medication binary, diet, breathwork, panic,
stimulation, or trauma-cause claim enters.

**Notebook update.** Record 5 opens the cost account, protected goods, season,
renewal, stop authority, repair, and accountability. Record 1 changes only
where the material or capability floor changes.

**Handoff.** Protection answers what freedom must not destroy; Chapter 22 asks
what freedom is positively for.

### 22. Freedom Needs a Use

**Governing question.** Which chosen obligations make freedom worth having
after compulsion recedes?

**Narrative work.** Glenn Boyd returns only for the post-exit difference
between release and purpose. Mike Kessner's move from office to home keeps
family time and plural work visible. The post-exit couple shows freedom without
a ready purpose and preserves each person's belief and role autonomy.

**V3 work.** Distinguish freedom from compulsion, recovery, chosen work,
relationship, service, mastery, play, and contribution. Chosen work remains
chosen only when exit, rest, and revision are credible. Marriage, parenthood,
faith, and continued work remain personal forms rather than a universal answer.

**Notebook update.** Record 5 builds a freedom portfolio: recovery, people,
work, learning, service, play, chosen obligations, boundaries, and review.

**Handoff.** Freedom becomes durable when the reader can name what is enough
to keep, enough to share, and enough to stop accumulating.

### 23. Enough

**Governing question.** How should wealth be used and transferred so that it
enlarges capability without conscripting identity or becoming another form of
captivity?

**Narrative work.** Glenn Boyd returns to close the poverty-to-luxury arc.
Kessner's home keeps memory outside financial return. Tebow's provision and
capacity distinction, Winston's income partition, and the Chicago
great-grandchild horizon appear as bounded intergenerational cases rather than
a final montage of advice. Harpin and the UAE operator return only when private
stewardship meets contribution beyond the household.

**V3 work.** Revise the Chapter 1 enough audit. Separate material provision,
capability, ownership, work, authority, belonging, and choice. Wealth may
offer security, education, time, health support, relationships, opportunity,
and a willing claim; it may not purchase another person's career, marriage,
gratitude, identity, or family harmony. A qualified non-family steward is a
legitimate outcome. Preserve plural lives and current-law boundaries. Let
abundance, fulfillment, and contentment constrain one another without
collapsing into a slogan.

**Notebook update.** Record 5 completes the family wealth compact, contribution
boundary, resources and capability transferred, succession alternatives,
revised enough statement, and permission to stop.

**Handoff.** The final movement returns to a finite day and leaves the reader
with agency and duty, not a guaranteed wealth formula.
