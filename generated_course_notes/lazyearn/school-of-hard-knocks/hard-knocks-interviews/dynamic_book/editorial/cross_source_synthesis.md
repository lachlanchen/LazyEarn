# Cross-Source Synthesis

This file records the corpus-wide argument before chapter prose is accepted. It
prevents a frequent claim from becoming a law merely because many edited
interviews repeat it.

## Money and Freedom

**Recurring claim.** More money creates freedom, security, time, and the power
to leave.

**Counterevidence.** Speakers disagree about the number, report emptiness after
reaching it, describe new obligations, or return to work after retirement.
Scarcity makes money intensely important; beyond security, its relationship to
happiness weakens and becomes dependent on use.

**Editorial resolution.** Define money as infrastructure. Measure financial
progress through capabilities and resilience before status. Chapters 1, 4, 22,
and 23 carry the full arc.

## Agency, Luck, and Starting Conditions

**Recurring claim.** Resourcefulness, hunger, self-belief, discipline, and
persistence can overcome a poor start.

**Counterevidence.** The stories also depend on migration rights, geography,
health, family labor, mentors, education, institutional access, market timing,
capital, and luck. Survivors are overrepresented.

**Editorial resolution.** Preserve agency without turning outcomes into moral
rank. Ask what action is available from a real starting position and what
constraint requires collective or institutional change. Chapters 2 and 3.

## Education and Skill

**Recurring claim.** Formal education is unnecessary; practical experience and
specialization matter more.

**Counterevidence.** Medicine, law, engineering, finance, appraisal, insurance,
and other paths require or reward credentials. Apprenticeship can be powerful,
but unstructured experience can repeat bad habits. Paid education can teach a
skill or become expensive confidence.

**Editorial resolution.** Choose the least-cost route that produces the needed
capability, signal, license, and network. Judge education by verified output and
path requirements, not ideology. Chapters 2, 3, and 10.

## Value and the Customer

**Recurring claim.** Solve a real problem, listen to customers, and value will
pull the product into the market.

**Counterevidence.** Customers do not always articulate the best design, large
markets contain fierce competition, and sellers can mistake attention or one
sale for fit. Urgency can invite exploitation.

**Editorial resolution.** Treat the problem as a testable hypothesis. Observe
costly behavior, current substitutes, willingness to act, and disconfirming
evidence. Chapters 5 through 7.

## Sales, Attention, and Trust

**Recurring claim.** Ask boldly, persist through rejection, become visible, and
build a personal brand.

**Counterevidence.** A clear no deserves respect. Pressure can conceal poor fit;
platform attention is rented; recognition can amplify weak products and false
claims. Employees should not endure abuse as a lesson.

**Editorial resolution.** Sales is reciprocal reality testing. Distribution is
a path from attention to delivery and return; reputation is counterparty memory
built by kept promises. Chapters 8 and 9.

## Ownership and Scale

**Recurring claim.** Salary pays once; equity, systems, recurring revenue, and
rights keep participating.

**Counterevidence.** Equity can be worthless, recurring contracts can hide
churn, founders can own exhausting jobs, and control can concentrate harm as
well as value. Professional employment may be the rational route.

**Editorial resolution.** Move up the ownership ladder only when rights,
economics, responsibility, and downside are understood. Build transferability
without treating customers and workers as components. Chapters 10, 11, 14, and
15.

## Teams, Partners, and Leadership

**Recurring claim.** Hire people smarter than you, find complementary partners,
delegate, and lead with confidence.

**Counterevidence.** Partnership failures, hidden pressure, capital-call
disputes, unsafe work, culture strain, and role confusion recur. Confidence can
become concealment; collaboration can become indecision.

**Editorial resolution.** Test partnership through reversible work, define
decision and information rights, share downside explicitly, protect dissent and
safety, and treat leadership as stewardship. Chapters 12 and 13.

## Growth and Cash

**Recurring claim.** Move quickly, reinvest, borrow, and scale before the market
closes.

**Counterevidence.** Growth consumes working capital, degrades delivery, changes
roles, and exposes collection delays. Revenue and valuation frequently appear
where cash should be discussed.

**Editorial resolution.** Label every number, model the operating cycle, stage
capacity, preserve reserves, and specify stop conditions before accelerating.
Chapters 12, 16, and 19.

## Debt and Property

**Recurring claim.** Use leverage to acquire productive property or businesses;
let the asset repay the debt.

**Counterevidence.** Rates reset, tenants leave, demand falls, maintenance
arrives, covenants bind, and refinancing can disappear. Appreciation is local,
leveraged, and unrealized. Seller finance changes the lender, not the risk.

**Editorial resolution.** Debt is useful only inside a survivable capital
structure tested against adverse cash flow and personal collateral exposure.
Chapter 17.

## Concentration and Diversification

**Recurring claim.** Concentrate to build a fortune; stay inside a domain you
understand.

**Counterevidence.** The corpus contains concentrated winners because they are
visible, but also severe paper losses, collapses, and fragile positions.
Diversification preserves options but can become indiscriminate ownership.

**Editorial resolution.** Distinguish the operating concentration used to build
capability from the household concentration that can erase freedom. Tie every
portfolio to horizon, liquidity, loss capacity, knowledge, and sale rules.
Chapter 18.

## Tax, Entities, Insurance, and Advice

**Recurring claim.** Structure determines what an owner keeps and protects.

**Counterevidence.** A deduction still costs money; an entity is not immunity;
jurisdictions and rules change; sellers and advisers have conflicts; insurance
products combine protection and finance in ways that are easy to oversimplify.

**Editorial resolution.** Explain purpose and risk, not hacks. State date,
jurisdiction, fees, commissions, guarantees, exclusions, conflicts, and the
need for qualified review. Chapter 20.

## Persistence and Stopping

**Recurring claim.** Never quit, ignore rejection, and outwork everyone.

**Counterevidence.** Speakers report health events, damaged relationships,
bankruptcy, burnout, and years spent defending bad positions. Continued effort
can raise encounter probability or deepen loss.

**Editorial resolution.** Pair persistence with evidence, review dates, health
limits, ethical limits, and explicit stopping rules. Chapters 8, 18, and 21.

## Enough and Stewardship

**Recurring claim.** Family, service, faith, health, purpose, and impact matter
more than the final number.

**Counterevidence.** Speakers define these goods differently, and retrospective
wisdom can soften the sacrifices that made wealth possible. Wealth can enlarge
care or enlarge power without accountability.

**Editorial resolution.** Do not impose one metaphysics. End with plural sources
of meaning and a common duty: people are not inputs, freedom needs a use, and
position creates obligations. Chapters 21 through 23.

## Recurring-Case Rule

A person may recur only when the later appearance changes the argument. A
biography is introduced once. Later chapters restate only the minimum needed to
understand a new decision. Overlapping edits or follow-up interviews are one
evidence family, not independent corroboration.

## Coverage Rule

Each source has one primary chapter in `chapter_contracts.json`. Secondary use
is allowed wherever its mechanism or contradiction is necessary. A source is
marked `accepted` only after every substantive item in its ledger row is either
present in checked prose or explicitly classified as repetition, promotion,
corruption, sensitive material, unsupported claim, or detail outside the
book's scope with no loss of meaning.
