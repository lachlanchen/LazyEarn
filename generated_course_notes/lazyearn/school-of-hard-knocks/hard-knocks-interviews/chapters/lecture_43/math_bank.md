## Notation Choices
- Use \(R\) only for annual revenue; use \(S\) for discrete sale/exit values.
- Use \(N\) for counts of people, firms, billionaires, or competitors.
- Use \(T\) for durations or effective time intervals.
- Use \(a\) for age variables, with explicit subscripts `chronological` and `biological`.
- Use \(F\) for follower count; keep it approximate.
- Use \(t_{\mathrm{job}}, t_{\mathrm{career}}\) for fractional attention allocation.
- Use \(\alpha\) for capital-allocation share.
- Use \(\mathcal{P}\) and \(\mathcal{T}\) for qualitative sets or partitions.
- Use roman-text subscripts inside math for semantic clarity:
  \(R_{\mathrm{smoothie,last}}\), \(N_{\mathrm{TX,billionaires}}\), \(T_{\mathrm{sales}}\).
- Use \(\approx\) for rounded, self-reported, or time-varying numbers.
- Use \(>\) for “over” or “in excess of” when that is exactly how the lecture phrases it.
- Use interval notation only when the transcript provides a genuine range:
  \(R_{\mathrm{smoothie,this}} \in [\$32,\$35]\times 10^{6}\).
- Use \(\to\) only for schematic business logic, not as a formal theorem arrow.
- Keep dollar values inside math as \(\$\) and scale with \(10^6\), \(10^9\) when compactness helps.
- Do not invent operator notation for faith, identity, or responsibility claims; keep those in prose unless they are being represented as a simple schematic chain.
