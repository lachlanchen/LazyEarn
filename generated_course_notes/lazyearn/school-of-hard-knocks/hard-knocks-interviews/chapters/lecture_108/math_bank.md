## Derivation Steps
1. Failure-compounding schema
   1. Start with a failed attempt.
   2. Remove excessive meaning or ego attachment from the failure.
   3. Extract what was learned.
   4. Repeat over time.
   5. Treat improvement as compounded learning, not as one-off inspiration.

2. Imagination-to-result schema
   1. Begin with an imagined outcome not yet present in material reality.
   2. Hold that image stably in mind.
   3. Let the imagined form guide thought and action.
   4. Repeat daily through meditation and internal reconditioning.
   5. Use action to move the imagined outcome toward material realization.

3. Feedback-loop schema
   1. Take consistent action.
   2. Record who was contacted, what happened, and what worked or failed.
   3. Compare repeated attempts rather than isolated moments.
   4. Distinguish surface script from underlying context.
   5. Use repeated review to sharpen pattern recognition.

4. Pattern-recognition-through-modeling schema
   1. Identify people who already have the result sought.
   2. Pay for access to their methods and environment.
   3. Observe recurring structures in how they act and decide.
   4. Model those structures instead of inventing from scratch.
   5. Convert borrowed patterns into faster execution.

5. Scaling schema
   1. Start with a strong product.
   2. A strong product attracts good leadership.
   3. Good leadership supports recruiting and fundraising.
   4. Recruiting and fundraising supply talent and cash.
   5. Talent plus cash make larger-scale growth possible.

6. Zero-to-rebuild schema
   1. Sell the most expensive viable offer available to restore cash flow.
   2. Secure food and shelter first.
   3. Add value for free to create testimonials and case studies.
   4. Use those proofs to land an anchor client.
   5. Sell adjacent clients in the same niche.
   6. Build a team around the validated offer.
   7. Add ads and more lead sources only after the offer is working.

7. Sales-certainty schema
   1. Identify the prospect’s current situation.
   2. Identify the prospect’s desired situation.
   3. Understand the prospect’s demographics and psychographics.
   4. Frame the offer as the bridge across that gap.
   5. Preempt objections upstream rather than rescuing the close at the end.
   6. Define sales as transfer of certainty, not mere pressure.
