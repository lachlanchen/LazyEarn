## Derivation Steps
1. Turnaround value contrast
   1. Start from a distressed operating position: \(E_0 \approx -\$20\,\mathrm{M}\).
   2. Build and scale a hospitality machine.
   3. Sell the company to Hilton.
   4. Record the outcome as \(EV_{\mathrm{exit}} \approx \$3.3\,\mathrm{B}\).
   5. Use the pair \((E_0,EV_{\mathrm{exit}})\) as the lecture’s core turnaround contrast, not as a full derivation of personal net worth.

2. Enterprise value clarification
   1. Transcript first says “worth \$2.2 billion, \$3.3 billion equity value.”
   2. Speaker immediately corrects to “enterprise value.”
   3. Therefore use \(EV\) as the safer financial object.
   4. If needed, explain with \(EV=\text{Equity Value}+\text{Debt}-\text{Cash}\).
   5. Do not infer exact debt/cash numbers.

3. Brand accumulation and collapse
   1. Brand is said to take decades to build.
   2. Brand is said to take minutes to kill.
   3. Model this asymmetry with \(B_{t+1}=B_t+\Delta B_t\).
   4. Positive increments are small and cumulative under integrity.
   5. Negative shocks can be abrupt under trust violation.

4. Top-salesperson removal and growth
   1. A top salesperson is described as creating “cancer” in the organization.
   2. Leadership chooses brand/culture protection over short-run dependence.
   3. After removal, speaker reports growth of about \(20\%\).
   4. Record as \(g_{\mathrm{post\text{-}removal}}\approx 20\%\).
   5. Keep causality speaker-attributed, not proven econometrically.

5. 9/11 liquidity shock
   1. Flights are grounded.
   2. Hotel guest flow collapses.
   3. Cash inflow effectively goes to zero: \(F_t\approx 0\).
   4. Obligations continue: \(O_t>0\).
   5. Daily bank communication occurs.
   6. Additional borrowing is required: \(L_t>0\) or \(B_t>0\) in a rough recurrence.
   7. Survival can be summarized by \(C_{t+1}=C_t+F_t+L_t-O_t\).

6. Bank trust / three C’s
   1. Banks repeatedly say he is “good for the money.”
   2. Reason given: capacity, character, credit.
   3. Transparency and early repayment reinforce trust.
   4. Model as a structured triad, not a numerical formula.
   5. In polished notes, treat the three C’s as the condition for repeated capital access.

7. Negotiation silence
   1. Ask for the deal / place the pen.
   2. Do not speak past the close.
   3. Wait through anxiety.
   4. Counterparty eventually calls back.
   5. Preserve as a sequence:
      \[
      \text{close attempt} \to \text{silence} \to \text{counterparty movement}
      \]

8. California as operating-scale object
   1. Speaker says California is not merely a state but a country-scale economy.
   2. Quantitative anchor: \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathrm{GDP}}(\mathrm{California})=5\).
   3. Qualitative failures: no customer communication, no payroll experience, excess regulation, failed execution.
   4. Inputs gathered from \(N_{\mathrm{meetings}}>300\) conversations.
   5. Final structure is a state-as-business analogy, not literal national-accounting derivation.
