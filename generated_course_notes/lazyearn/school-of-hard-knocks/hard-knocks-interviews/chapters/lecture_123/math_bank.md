## Derivation Steps
1. Long-game career compounding
   1. Start from an uneven early career path rather than a clean launch.
   2. Replace immediate mastery with a selection rule: stay near authentic, high-energy people.
   3. Treat those relationships as opportunity-generating over time.
   4. Add repeated execution.
   5. Interpret later success as compounded career capital rather than a single breakthrough.

2. Industry-choice logic
   1. Ask which field matters “in today’s day and age.”
   2. Mike names data science / analytics.
   3. Motivate that choice by a firm-level use case: analysts detect what is trending.
   4. Send those signals to decision-makers.
   5. Conclude that industry attractiveness is tied to information leverage, not just prestige.

3. Team-specialization sales mechanism
   1. Start with the claim that sales is lonely when done on an island.
   2. Introduce a team structure to reduce isolation and improve learning.
   3. Add vertical specialization so sellers know the client’s industry.
   4. Convert sector familiarity into better conversations and more creative solutions.
   5. Conclude that team-based specialist selling dominates lone generalist selling.

4. Habit-to-performance chain
   1. Start with the daily routine: espresso, meditation, gratitude.
   2. Treat the routine as a repeated stabilizer rather than mystical insight.
   3. Use the routine to produce a better state for work.
   4. Repeat over long horizons.
   5. Interpret the value as cumulative rather than one-off.

5. Success ordering
   1. Ask for a definition of success.
   2. Mike explicitly places happy family life first.
   3. He places financial freedom next.
   4. He then adds a handful of good friends.
   5. Use a ranked ordering, not a flat list.

6. Alone-time strategic value
   1. Pose the tension: many people fear being alone.
   2. Reframe alone time as decompression from phone/computer noise.
   3. Use that time for reflection and planning.
   4. Allow for random thoughts / non-obvious solutions to surface.
   5. Conclude that solitude functions as leadership operating space.

7. Restart-from-zero wealth logic
   1. Assume the wealth stock is wiped out.
   2. Choose the fastest reliable income engine: full-time sales.
   3. Generate commission checks quickly through hustle and work rate.
   4. Redirect cash flow into scalable asset buckets.
   5. Rebuild wealth by moving from income engine to wealth engine.

8. Legacy-phase motivation
   1. Distinguish early motivation from later motivation.
   2. Early phase: make a living, then make more money for family.
   3. Later phase: legacy and influence over younger operators.
   4. Connect this to current firm growth and mentoring.
   5. Interpret “second mountain” as a phase change in the objective function.
