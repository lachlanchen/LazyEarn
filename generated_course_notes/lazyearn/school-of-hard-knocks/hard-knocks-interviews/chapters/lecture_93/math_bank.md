## Derivation Steps
Real-estate scaling loop
1. A project closes with positive profit \(\Pi_n\).
2. Profit is not mainly consumed; it is rolled into the next build.
3. That raises deployable capital from \(K_n\) to \(K_{n+1}\).
4. Higher \(K_{n+1}\) permits a larger subsequent project.
5. Repetition of this loop explains the move from smaller figures to larger figures.
6. The named 1031 exchange is introduced as a way to reduce immediate tax drag on the rollover step.

Faith-as-action loop
1. Do not wait until \(r_t \ge r^\ast\).
2. Take a concrete action \(a_t\) first.
3. That action converts intention into commitment.
4. Commitment raises urgency/necessity.
5. Higher urgency forces recruitment, execution, and follow-through.
6. Resources arrive after commitment rather than before it.

Sales-certainty loop
1. Gather validating evidence about the product or business.
2. Validation raises internal certainty \(c\).
3. Higher certainty increases persuasive force \(\mathcal{I}(A \to B)\).
4. The prospect need not first believe the proposition itself.
5. The prospect first registers that the speaker believes it.
6. Sales is therefore modeled as transfer of belief rather than mere information transfer.

Three-bucket discipline
1. Start with an incoming check \(C\).
2. Split \(C\) immediately into three roughly equal parts.
3. Use one part for living.
4. Route one part to IRS/savings.
5. Route one part to reserve.
6. Treat the reserve as unavailable for ordinary consumption so it can absorb future shocks.

Persistence-and-decision heuristic
1. A failure event or weak-sales event occurs.
2. Do not promote that local failure into a permanent self-judgment.
3. Continue forward motion instead of freezing.
4. Make the next decision quickly.
5. Decision speed reduces overthinking delay.
6. Repeated fast decisions and continued effort preserve entrepreneurial longevity.

## Notation Choices
- Use \(\Pi\) for project-level profit and \(R\) for company-level annual revenue/volume; do not blur them.
- Use \(K_n\) for next-build capital in the real-estate case; keep the recurrence discrete because the speaker describes repeated projects, not continuous growth.
- Use \(C\) for incoming checks in the personal-finance section; use subscripts \(\text{live}\), \(\text{tax/sav}\), and \(\text{reserve}\) rather than introducing extra symbols.
- Use \(c\) for certainty and \(\mathcal{I}(A \to B)\) for influence; this keeps the sales section compact and directional.
- Use \(a_t\), \(r_t\), and \(r^\ast\) for the “faith before resources” discussion; this is the cleanest way to distinguish action from readiness.
- Use \(\to\) for process flow and \(\Rightarrow\) for claimed strategic implication; avoid presenting either as formal proof.
- Use \(\approx\) and \(>\) rather than exact equalities whenever the speaker gives only qualitative or approximate operational claims.
- Keep verbal labels such as “faith,” “necessity level,” and “transfer of belief” in roman text or quotation marks; they are named ideas, not formal operators.
