## Definitions And Objects

- \(W_t\): cumulative wealth or enterprise value at time \(t\).
- \(W_T\): long-horizon wealth outcome over a career horizon.
- \(\Pi_{\mathrm{year}}\): annual profit, earnings, or business generation when the interview speaks in yearly terms.
- \(\pi_{\text{single}}\): payoff from a single transaction or single investment.
- \(L_t\): realized loss or downside event at time \(t\).
- \(A\): risk aversion; use smaller \(A\) for greater willingness to bear downside.
- \(P_t\): playbook at time \(t\); a learned decision policy, not a literal written document.
- \(V\): value in the sense of idea/product quality or economic usefulness.
- \(D\): distribution capacity; partners, networks, streamers, or channels that move value into the market.
- \(I_M\): reinvestment into advertising, marketing, and branding.
- \(V_F^{\mathrm{redeployed}}\): value created when the founder stops doing low-leverage tasks and reallocates time to sales, strategy, or relationships.
- \(C_H\): cost of a high-quality hire.
- \(K_{\mathrm{rep}}\): reputation capital; trust, honor, word, clean dealing.
- \(s_0 \to s_1 \to s_2 \to \cdots\): apprenticeship-state ladder; useful for the Peter Marco trajectory.
- \(O_t\): opportunity encountered at time \(t\); a break, opening, or access point that can be seized or ignored.

## Derivation Steps

1. Risk-aversion mechanism: ask what separates ordinary earners from major wealth builders; identify \(A\) as the key variable; invoke the attributed 83% / negative-\$3 million claim; conclude that high-wealth paths tolerate deeper early downside than ordinary paths.
2. Playbook mechanism: reject \(\pi_{\text{single}}\) as the main target; treat early years as repeated trials; record losses \(L_t\); update \(P_t\); improve later decision quality; raise expected \(W_T\).
3. Opportunity mechanism: an opportunity \(O_t\) appears; the entrepreneur acts rather than hesitates; sustained work increases exposure to further opportunities; compounding comes from repeated capture rather than one perfect choice.
4. Numeracy mechanism: study finance/law to read contracts, operations, and incentives; use numbers to “figure everything out”; numerical literacy becomes a control system for business decisions.
5. Apprenticeship mechanism: start in a low-trust state \(s_0\) (cleaning bathrooms); move to messenger; move to skilled trade work; move to handling valuable inventory globally; accumulate trust and competence; end in ownership and scale.
6. Reputation mechanism: transact repeatedly with high-value goods and clients; preserve \(K_{\mathrm{rep}}\) through keeping one’s word; \(K_{\mathrm{rep}}\) stabilizes future earning power even if specific assets disappear.
7. Delegation mechanism: founder initially does everything under capital constraint; growth raises the opportunity cost of founder time; compare \(V_F^{\mathrm{redeployed}}\) with \(C_H\); delegate once the inequality flips; organizational speed improves.
8. Partner-selection mechanism: use a small trait filter rather than a vague people test; humble, smart, driven act as state variables for partner quality; passing the filter supports delegation and scale.
9. Distribution mechanism: a strong product/value proposition alone is insufficient; add partners and channels \(D\); enlarge reach; revenue and deal flow increase with accessible distribution.
10. Branding mechanism: once profit appears, allocate \(I_M\); branding raises memorability and inbound demand; competence without brand leaves the business invisible.

## Notation Choices

- Use \(W\) for wealth and cumulative value, not for yearly income.
- Use \(\Pi_{\mathrm{year}}\) for annual money made and \(\pi_{\text{single}}\) for one-shot deal payoff.
- Use \(A\) for risk aversion, since that is the lecture’s own term; avoid switching between “risk aversion” and “risk appetite” unless explicitly translating via prose.
- Use \(L_t\) only for realized downside or losses; do not use it for generic bad outcomes.
- Use \(P_t\) for playbook; interpret it as a learned policy updated over repeated attempts.
- Use \(V\) and \(D\) for value and distribution in the media/business-scaling sections.
- Use \(I_M\) for marketing/brand reinvestment; keep advertising and branding under the same symbol unless a later draft truly needs separation.
- Use \(K_{\mathrm{rep}}\) for reputation capital in the jewelry and trust-based commerce discussion.
- Use state arrows \(s_0 \to s_1 \to s_2\) for apprenticeship ladders or career progression; do not turn them into probabilistic Markov notation unless a later prompt explicitly asks for that level of formalization.
