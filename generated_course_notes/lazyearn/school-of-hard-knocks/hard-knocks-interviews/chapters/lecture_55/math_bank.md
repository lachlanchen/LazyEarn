## Notation Choices
- Use \(F_{\bullet}\) for follower counts and \(V_{\bullet}\) for view counts or sale values only when the context makes that distinction obvious.
- Use \(R_{\bullet}\) for revenue, business volume, or “business did” figures.
- Use \(\Pi_{\bullet}\) for profit.
- Use \(Y_{\bullet}\) for “most amount of money made in a single year” style claims.
- Use \(N_{\bullet}\) for location counts and \(n_{\bullet}\) for people counts or room-of-100 examples.
- Use \(p_{\bullet}\) for proportions or rhetorical probabilities stated in the lecture.
- Keep currencies explicit: dollars, dirhams, and percentages should never be silently mixed.
- Prefer \(\to\) for operator identities, heuristics, and commercial mechanisms.
- Reserve \(\propto\) for the explicit “direct proportion” problem-size claim and for cautious proportional heuristics only.
- Use \(\uparrow,\downarrow\) only when capturing directional monotonic claims like walk-away power and leverage.
- Treat verbal slogans such as “macro patience + micro urgency” as compact operator formulas, not as literal algebra.
- Avoid strong equality for soft doctrines when a heuristic arrow or approximation is more faithful.
