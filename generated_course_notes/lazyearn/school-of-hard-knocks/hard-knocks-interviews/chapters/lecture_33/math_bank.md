## Definitions And Objects

- \(M_{\mathrm{peak}}\): maximum money event associated with a specific year; in Herjavec’s case, a year dominated by business sale proceeds and capital gains.
- \(Y\): annual income flow, salary-like or operating-income-like money earned during a year.
- \(G_{\mathrm{exit}}\): realized gain from sale of a business or ownership stake.
- \(W\): wealth stock; not just one year’s income, but durable ownership value and retained capital.
- \(A\): owned asset or business that can be sold.
- \(V(A)\): realizable value of the owned asset \(A\).
- \(S_t\): life/business state at time \(t\).
- \(a_t\): action level or decision vector at time \(t\); used only to formalize the “nothing changes unless you change” claim.
- \(I_{\mathrm{people}}, I_{\mathrm{processes}}, I_{\mathrm{profitability}}\): indicator variables for the education investor’s three-part screen.
- \(K_0\): starting capital available to the operator.
- \(H_{\mathrm{RE}}\): real-estate holding horizon, measured in years.
- \(\Pi_{\mathrm{RE}}\): realized real-estate profit.
- \(N\): discovered customer need.
- \(P\): sales pitch or offer presentation.
- \(p_{\mathrm{close}}\): probability of closing the sale.
- \(C_t\): connection capital / access capital / the stock of people who know us and may open doors.
- \(V_t\): value provided to others at time \(t\), used to build \(C_t\).
- \(n_{\mathrm{competitors}}\): number or density of competitors in a market.
- \(D_{\mathrm{market}}\): revealed demand in a market.

## Derivation Steps

Rich-versus-wealthy split
1. Start from the lecture’s largest annual number: \(M_{\mathrm{peak}} \approx \$500\text{M}\).
2. Note Herjavec immediately qualifies it: the number included sale proceeds and capital gains.
3. Separate that event-year total into ordinary yearly income \(Y\) and sale-related gain \(G_{\mathrm{exit}}\).
4. Compare it to his stated income-only ceiling \(Y \approx \$18\text{M}\).
5. Infer the lecture’s main distinction: very high income can make us rich, but ownership and exit produce the wealth jump.

Trend-creation logic
1. Begin with Herjavec’s contrast between following and creating trends.
2. Treat following as monetizing participation in an existing wave.
3. Treat creating as controlling or owning the wave early enough to capture outsized value.
4. Use the contrast to justify why ownership \(A\) and sale value \(V(A)\) matter more than income alone.

Change axiom
1. Take the spoken line “nothing changes if nothing changes.”
2. Represent current condition by \(S_t\).
3. Let \(\Delta a_t\) denote whether behavior/choices change.
4. If \(\Delta a_t=0\), then the next state stays effectively the same: \(S_{t+1}\approx S_t\).
5. Use this only as a schematic compression of the lecture’s spoken logic.

Investment screen
1. Introduce the three screening dimensions: people, processes, profitability.
2. Represent each dimension by a binary indicator \(I_\bullet\in\{0,1\}\).
3. Encode the investor’s claim that most companies have only two of the three.
4. The underwriting problem becomes identifying the missing leg and asking whether the investor can add it.

Real-estate hold-horizon rule
1. Introduce holding horizon \(H_{\mathrm{RE}}\).
2. State Herjavec’s rule in attributed form: \(H_{\mathrm{RE}}\ge 10\) years is usually enough to make money.
3. Identify the enemy not as property ownership itself but as forced sale from liquidity stress.
4. Write the failure mode as short horizon plus liquidity pressure, not as ordinary market fluctuation.

Need-first sales logic
1. Begin with Herjavec’s claim that the biggest sales mistake is applying one pitch to everybody.
2. Represent need discovery by \(N\) and pitch by \(P\).
3. Order them: first identify \(N\), then deploy \(P\).
4. Summarize the close process as \(p_{\mathrm{close}} = p(N,P)\), with need match carrying priority.

Network-through-value logic
1. Start from Herjavec’s rejection of “it’s who you know” as passive received wisdom.
2. Introduce connection capital \(C_t\) as something that can be built.
3. Let \(V_t\) denote value provided to other people.
4. Model access growth as \(C_{t+1}=f(C_t,V_t)\).
5. Interpret “become the person other people want to know” as increasing \(V_t\), not begging for access.

Competition and differentiation
1. Use the gas-station analogy to treat heavy competition as proof of demand rather than automatic discouragement.
2. Represent this as \(n_{\mathrm{competitors}}\uparrow \Rightarrow D_{\mathrm{market}}\uparrow\).
3. Then add the promoter’s second rule: competition alone is not enough; the offer must still be differentiated.
4. Thus crowded market plus differentiated offer is the intended operating setup.

Operator-over-idea priority
1. Start from Herjavec’s stated preference: he invests in people over ideas.
2. Compare a strong operator with a weak idea against a weak operator with a strong idea.
3. Rank the former above the latter because execution can improve a mediocre concept, while bad execution can destroy a strong business.
4. Keep the relation ordinal rather than numeric.

## Notation Choices

- Use \(Y\) for annual income and avoid \(I\) for income, since \(I_\bullet\) is already reserved for screening indicators.
- Use \(G_{\mathrm{exit}}\) for exit-related realized gain rather than generic “capital gains” everywhere; it keeps the lecture tied to business sale events.
- Use \(M_{\mathrm{peak}}\) only for the largest year/event, not for ordinary recurring earnings.
- Use \(W\) for wealth stock and keep it conceptually distinct from income flow \(Y\).
- Use \(A\) for owned asset/business and \(V(A)\) for its realizable sale value.
- Use \(S_t\) and \(a_t\) only for the “nothing changes” schematic; do not extend them into a full dynamic model.
- Use \(K_0\) for initial capital because the lecture explicitly branches advice by whether we start with money or without it.
- Use \(H_{\mathrm{RE}}\) in years and \(\Pi_{\mathrm{RE}}\) for realized real-estate profit.
- Use \(N\), \(P\), and \(p_{\mathrm{close}}\) for the sales block: need, pitch, close probability.
- Use \(C_t\) for connection/access capital and \(V_t\) for value provided to others.
- Use \(n_{\mathrm{competitors}}\) and \(D_{\mathrm{market}}\) if the competition-demand heuristic is formalized.
- Prefer \(\approx\) over \(=\) for almost all dollar figures unless the transcript is explicitly exact.
- Prefer \(\to\) for directional lecture claims and \(\Rightarrow\) only for stronger speaker-stated implications.
- Mark speaker-specific rules in prose nearby when they might otherwise read like general theorems.
