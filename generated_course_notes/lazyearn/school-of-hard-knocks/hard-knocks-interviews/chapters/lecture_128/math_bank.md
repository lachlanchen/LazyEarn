## Definitions And Objects
- \(Y\): single-year income; only bounded by the “seven digits” statement.
- \(C_s\): conspicuous or status consumption; the Porsche example is the model case.
- \(\mathcal{M}\): chosen local market or submarket.
- \(B(\mathcal{M})\): set of buildings in the chosen market.
- \(L_t \subseteq B(\mathcal{M})\): buildings or lobbies physically covered by time \(t\).
- \(A_t\): activity level; field presence, visits, calls, and follow-up intensity.
- \(K_t\): accumulated local knowledge of buildings, tenants, activity, and change.
- \(R_t\): relationship stock generated by repeated contact plus market familiarity.
- \(O_t\): opportunity flow; leads, openings, introductions, deals, or actionable situations.
- \(N_t\): refusal or “no” event in the search / relationship process.

## Derivation Steps
1. Entry mechanism
   1. Start from the transcript claim that commercial real estate is relationship-based.
   2. Treat relationships as something accumulated, not granted at entry.
   3. The speaker specifies the accumulation process: know every building and be in every lobby.
   4. Repeated coverage produces local state information about what is happening inside buildings.
   5. Information plus repeated presence increases relationship stock.
   6. Relationship stock and knowledge together increase opportunity flow.

2. Discipline and anti-dissipation mechanism
   1. The lecture first frames the problem as poor self-management.
   2. It then gives concrete leakage channels: women, beer, flashy car purchases.
   3. Therefore high income is not sufficient evidence of durable wealth-building.
   4. In note form, treat discipline as preserving both capital and attention for later compounding.

3. Persistence mechanism
   1. Active market coverage creates contact attempts and exposure.
   2. Some exposure returns rejection.
   3. The speaker explicitly denies that “no” should terminate the process.
   4. Therefore a refusal event updates tactics, not the decision to remain active.

## Notation Choices
- Use \(\to\) for causal or operational progression, not \(=\), unless a quantity is actually being defined.
- Use \(t\) only as a sequence index for accumulation; do not imply measured continuous time.
- Use \(\mathcal{M}\) for the relevant submarket so that “every building” is interpreted locally rather than globally.
- Use \(B(\mathcal{M})\) for buildings, \(K_t\) for knowledge, \(R_t\) for relationships, \(A_t\) for activity, and \(O_t\) for opportunity throughout.
- Use \(C_s\) for status consumption; avoid utility-maximization notation unless a later prompt explicitly wants it.
- Treat \(f\) and \(g\) as unspecified monotone maps, not estimated models.
- Keep inequalities only where the transcript supports them directly, as with \(10^6 \le Y < 10^7\).
- Avoid heavy finance notation, stochastic-process notation, or optimization language unless later prompts require tighter formalization.
