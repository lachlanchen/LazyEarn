## Derivation Steps
Delegation and scale:
1. The lecture gives the scale fact \(N_{\text{FG}}=155\).
2. Shaq says he cannot be in \(155\) places at once.
3. Direct single-person supervision therefore fails at that scale.
4. The lecture resolves the constraint with one word: delegation.
5. Editorially, the implied operator identity is parallel management capacity rather than personal omnipresence.

Selective imitation:
1. Shaq answers the success question with the provocation “I was a thief.”
2. He defines the theft as taking methods from better performers.
3. The named source set is \(\mathcal{E}=\{\text{Jordan},\text{Magic},\text{Kareem},\text{Ali}\}\).
4. He says he put those elements “inside” his brain.
5. The resulting object is not a copy of one exemplar but a constructed self.

Wealth preservation asymmetry:
1. The host introduces the statistic \(p_{\text{broke,NFL}} \approx \frac13\) over \(t_{\text{post-career}}\approx 3\text{--}5\) years.
2. Shaq answers with the asymmetry “easy to make, hard to keep.”
3. The lecture then names `annuity` as a preservation keyword.
4. Later autobiography supplies the cautionary evidence: \(\$1{,}000{,}000\) spent in about \(30\) minutes.
5. The preserved lesson is that income arrival and wealth retention are distinct problems.

Saving rule:
1. Shaq attributes a mentor rule: save \(75\), enjoy \(25\).
2. Let income be \(Y\).
3. Then \(s=0.75Y\).
4. And \(c=0.25Y\).
5. The split exhausts income via \(s+c=Y\).

Negotiation ladder:
1. Shaq advises not to talk first and to start high.
2. He gives the opening ask \(a_0=200\).
3. The counter comes back at \(a_1=110\).
4. The next asks move to \(a_2=140\) and \(a_3=125\).
5. The lecture treats \(125\) as the deal point.
6. The numerical lesson is that anchoring high preserves bargaining room.

Negotiation loss warning:
1. Shaq imagines the other side’s budget as \(B=200\).
2. He imagines an opening ask of \(50\).
3. Lost room is computed as \(B-a_0 = 200-50\).
4. The result is \(150\).
5. The lecture’s claim is that under-anchoring destroys negotiable surplus.

Business-starting heuristic:
1. Shaq distinguishes inventing something from copying something already working.
2. He pairs that with the heuristic \(p_{\text{firm failure}}\approx \frac{8}{10}\) over \(t_{\text{firm failure}}\approx 5\) years.
3. The lecture uses the statistic to make imitation of proven demand look commercially rational.
4. The supporting social rule is to hire people smarter than oneself.
5. The combined logic is that uncertainty should be reduced by pattern recognition and better talent.

## Notation Choices
- Use \(N\) for counts of units, stores, or businesses.
- Use \(p\) for speaker-side probabilities or frequencies.
- Use \(t\) for time windows in years.
- Use \(Y\) for income or monetary inflow over a period.
- Use \(s\) and \(c\) for saved and consumed shares of \(Y\).
- Use \(B\) for counterparty budget in negotiation.
- Use \(a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3\) for the negotiation-offer sequence.
- Use \(\mathcal{E}\) for the exemplar set in the “thief” explanation.
- Use \(\to\) for procedural or operator flow, not strict algebraic implication.
- Use \(\Rightarrow\) only for the lecture’s stronger causal-operational claims, such as delegation enabling scale.
- Use \(\approx\) for heuristics, quoted stats, and approximate counts.
- Use \(\sim\) for rhetorical scale markers like “around a thousand businesses.”
- Do not invent formal annuity notation; the lecture does not define it.
- Do not assign precise symbols to “motivation,” “luck,” or “faith” unless later writing specifically needs an editorial schematic.
