## Definitions And Objects
- \(t\): business time index, best read in years or long career intervals, not daily market time.
- \(Y_t\): total annual income or yearly economic take.
- \(Y_t^{\text{labor}}\): compensation from labor, salary, fees, or executive pay.
- \(Y_t^{\text{own}}\): ownership-derived income from practices, centers, brands, equity stakes, or business distributions.
- \(\Delta A_t\): change in value or realized gain from productive assets, especially real estate or owned business pieces.
- \(W_t\): investable wealth or net worth available for compounding.
- \(r_t\): return on invested wealth.
- \(S_t\): new savings/investment contribution out of current income.
- \(C_t\): personal consumption.
- \(C_t^{\text{needs}}\), \(C_t^{\text{wants}}\): basic spending versus discretionary/luxury spending.
- \(K_t\): productive business capital in a broad sense: tools, systems, team quality, and operating capacity.
- \(I_t\): reinvestment back into the business.
- \(\delta\): depreciation/drift term in the editorial capital-accumulation model.
- \(n_t\): trusted delegated operating span or effective team size.
- \(Q_t\): business output, throughput, or scalable revenue capacity.
- \(M_t\): assets under management or controlled asset base.
- \(V_t^{\text{RE}}\): long-horizon real-estate value, only if the chapter later needs a dedicated real-estate symbol.

## Derivation Steps
Ownership lift from wage income to higher annual take:
1. Start with professional or executive labor income \(Y_t^{\text{labor}}\).
2. Add ownership in the place where the labor happens.
3. Add adjacent business units or network effects.
4. Add ownership of the real estate under the operating business.
5. The annual result becomes \(Y_t^{\text{labor}} + Y_t^{\text{own}} + \Delta A_t\), not labor pay alone.

Compounding logic from the surgeon’s financial advice:
1. Keep \(S_t > 0\) by investing early rather than consuming everything.
2. Let existing wealth \(W_t\) earn return \(r_t\).
3. Roll gains back into the base instead of extracting them immediately.
4. Repeat over many \(t\)-periods.
5. Wealth growth accelerates because returns attach to a growing base.

Reinvestment logic from the contractor case:
1. Growth requires spending, not only saving.
2. Direct \(I_t\) into better tools, labor, and operating quality.
3. This raises \(K_t\).
4. Higher \(K_t\) raises \(Q_t\).
5. Higher \(Q_t\) supports more revenue, which can fund the next round of \(I_t\).

Delegation logic from the finance entrepreneur case:
1. A solo operator faces a personal time ceiling.
2. Delegation increases effective team span \(n_t\).
3. More work can be done without the founder doing everything personally.
4. The firm can support larger headcount and client volume.
5. Larger \(Q_t\) makes seven-figure and larger outcomes more plausible.

Real-estate patience logic from Todd Napola:
1. Do not treat real estate like a daily checked brokerage balance.
2. Short-run fluctuations are not the operative decision variable.
3. Hold over long horizons so ownership has time to work.
4. The relevant comparison is generational or multi-decade, not daily.
5. Patience converts ownership into durable asset accumulation.
