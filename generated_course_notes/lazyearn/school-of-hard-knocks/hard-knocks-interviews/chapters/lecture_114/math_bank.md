## Derivation Steps
Validation pipeline
1. Start from a perceived need in an industry or space.
2. Estimate product viability and \(\mathrm{TAM}\) before building.
3. Interview as many potential clients as possible.
4. Prefer decision-makers as interview subjects.
5. Extract \(P\): concrete challenges and struggles.
6. Ask the \(V\) question: what would solving this mean for the company?
7. Seek \(\Pi\): an early pilot commitment.
8. Build the \(\mathrm{MVP}\) only after that sequence.

Idea-worth test
1. A problem may be substantial.
2. That alone does not certify \(G\).
3. Ask whether customers can actually be acquired.
4. Ask whether acquisition can scale.
5. Only then treat the idea as worth deeper investment.

Perfectionism to feedback loop
1. Treat perfectionism as reluctance to expose the product.
2. Show a differentiated but imperfect version anyway.
3. Let the client help shape fit and features.
4. Use the response to course-correct.
5. Replace concealment with iterative feedback.

Fundraising readiness
1. Build and test cheaply at the beginning.
2. Reinvest early revenue if available.
3. Broaden traction.
4. Show \(U\): visible upside or potential.
5. Show \(A_{\mathrm{rep}}\): repeatable acquisition.
6. Show \(\mathcal{P}\): a concrete plan.
7. Prefer \(\Delta \mathrm{MRR} > 0\) and rising revenue month to month.
8. Then take the business out for investment.

Scaling via delegation
1. Compute or estimate \(r_{\mathrm{founder}}\).
2. Estimate \(c_{\mathrm{task}}\).
3. If \(c_{\mathrm{task}} < r_{\mathrm{founder}}\), outsource.
4. If the role must be permanent, include it in forecast and budget.
5. Continue hiring only as the model supports it.

Exit-readiness sequence
1. Keep building revenue.
2. Keep improving the bottom line and efficiency.
3. Sustain visible movement in the business.
4. Let investors or acquirers notice the operating performance.
5. Exit becomes plausible as a consequence of sustained execution, not as a separate trick.
