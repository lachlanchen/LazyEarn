## Derivation Steps

1. Monthly equivalent of the first woman billionaire’s six-month figure:
   1. Take the spoken amount \(Y_{6\text{mo}} = \$80 \times 10^6\).
   2. Treat the time interval as \(6\) months.
   3. Divide by \(6\).
   4. Obtain \(Y_{\text{month}} \approx \$13.3 \times 10^6/\text{month}\).
   5. Keep this as simple arithmetic on a spoken number, not as a lecture-derived rate law.

2. Miles Garrett annual-income lower bound:
   1. The interviewer asks whether the number is greater than \$10 million.
   2. Garrett says yes.
   3. The interviewer asks whether it is greater than \$15 million.
   4. Garrett says yes.
   5. The interviewer asks whether it is greater than \$20 million.
   6. Garrett says yes.
   7. The safest preserved statement is \(Y_{\text{Miles}} > \$20 \times 10^6\).

3. Ken Ricci/Richie richness threshold:
   1. Let annual spend be \(S\).
   2. Apply the spoken heuristic \(W^\ast = 25S\).
   3. Substitute \(S = \$1000/\text{year}\).
   4. Get \(W^\ast = \$25{,}000\).
   5. Substitute \(S = \$10 \times 10^6/\text{year}\).
   6. Get \(W^\ast = \$250 \times 10^6\).
   7. Interpret the rule as spend-relative, not prestige-relative.

4. Business-school metric filtration from the first woman billionaire:
   1. Start with headline revenue \(R\).
   2. Reject \(R\) as the decisive business number.
   3. Move to nominal profit \(\Pi\).
   4. Note that \(\Pi\) may be distorted by bad debt or bad stock.
   5. Move again to after-tax cash actually distributable into one’s pocket.
   6. Label that target \(C_{\text{net}}\).
   7. Treat \(C_{\text{net}}\) as the operational metric the chapter should emphasize.

5. Dr. Forbes Riley communication chain:
   1. Start with the ability to communicate what one wants.
   2. Convert communication into persuasion.

- Use \(R\) for revenue or sales flow; reserve \(V_{\text{sales}}\) for cumulative “sold over \$2.5 billion worth of products” type claims so that it is not confused with annual revenue.

- Use \(D\) for debt; for inherited starting debt, write \(D_0\).

- Use \(\Pi\) for nominal/accounting profit only when discussing the business-school correction.

- Use \(C_{\text{net}}\) for after-tax cash actually extractable; make clear this is note-writer notation, not a visible lecture symbol.

- Use \(N\) for counts: \(N_{\text{companies}}, N_{\text{planes}}, N_{\text{employees}}\).

- Use \(S\) for annual spend and \(W^\ast\) for the richness threshold under the \(25\times\) heuristic.

- Use \(\approx\) for rounded, heuristic, or noisy spoken figures; use \(>\) for explicit lower bounds; use \(=\) only for transcript-given exact amounts or direct arithmetic consequences.

- Use arrows \(\to\) only for schematic business-state chains such as communication \(\to\) persuasion \(\to\) yes.

- Do not introduce net-worth notation unless the transcript explicitly supports it.
