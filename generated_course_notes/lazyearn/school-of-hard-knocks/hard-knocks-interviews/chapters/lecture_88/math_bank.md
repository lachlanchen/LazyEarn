## Derivation Steps
Histogram / wealth-field reading
1. Introduce Highland Park as a high-wealth environment.
2. Read the overlaid graphic with \(x\) as income bins and \(y\) as household counts.
3. Observe that the mass is concentrated in the rightmost \(>200\) bin.
4. Treat the histogram as evidence that the interview field is unusually affluent.
5. Use that field condition to motivate why the lecture asks wealth-mechanism questions there.

Ribbon / finite-life model
1. Model a life as a strip running from \(1\) to \(100\).
2. Mark average female life at \(81\) and average male life at \(75\).
3. Mark the speaker’s age at \(57\).
4. Tear away the already-lived segment.
5. Interpret the untorn remainder as the finite stock of time left.
6. Add the lecture’s qualification: even the nominal remainder overstates usable life because illness can reduce quality before the endpoint.

Opportunity-shock business case
1. Start from a prior annual high of about \(\$1.5\) million.
2. Introduce the external shock: American Airlines files bankruptcy.
3. Note the local employee pool \(N_{\text{AA}}\approx 13{,}000\).
4. Employees fear losing benefits and rush to use hearing coverage while it is still available.
5. Demand for hearing-related services spikes.
6. The business records \(\$16.7\) million in a single year.
7. Read the case as opportunity recognition under shock, not as a generic linear trend.

Leverage and mentorship model
1. Begin with the lecture’s complaint about people working in isolation.
2. Introduce hiring as the move that creates leverage.
3. Introduce mentorship as the move that reduces mistakes and shortens path length.
4. Treat both as multiplicative mechanisms rather than mere additions to effort.
5. Use this to explain why growth is framed as scale, not just hard work.

Corporate-wealth model
1. Separate paycheck income from ownership.
2. Treat salary \(S\) as what keeps bills paid.
3. Treat equity \(E\) as the asset that compounds or appreciates.
4. Realize wealth when ownership is accumulated and then repriced or monetized.
5. Conclude that corporate wealth in the lecture comes primarily through equity participation, not salary alone.

CEO triad logic
1. Start with unavoidable change.
2. Recast change as opportunity when one responds better or sooner than others.
3. Connect effective response to preparation.
4. Convert preparation into confidence.
5. Use clarity/simplicity as the communication condition for turning strategy into action and shareholder value.
