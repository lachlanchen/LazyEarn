## Derivation Steps
Revenue doubling estimate
1. Take the stated prior-year company revenue \(R_{\text{last}}=\$149\text{M}\).
2. Take the stated projection \(R_{\text{proj}}\approx \$300\text{M}\).
3. Form the growth multiple \(g=R_{\text{proj}}/R_{\text{last}}\).
4. Report the result as approximately \(2\times\), not as an exact forecast.

Seven-to-nine-figure threshold model
1. Start with seven figures: idea plus sales and marketing.
2. Add team quality to explain the move toward eight figures.
3. Add systems and SOPs to explain the move toward nine figures.
4. Treat the nine-figure jump as a structural change, not just “more hustle.”

SOP continuity argument
1. Treat each recurring operational role as a function \(f\).
2. Document \(f\) in a standard operating procedure.
3. Allow a colleague \(c\) to execute \(f\) if the original operator \(o\) is absent.
4. Conclude that the business no longer relies on one person for continuity.
5. Rephrase the result as a move from CEO-dependent execution to machine-dependent execution.

Environment and escape-velocity heuristic
1. Compare a poor environment with low access to capital, examples, and connections.
2. Compare a major city with visible wealth concentration and active economic flow.
3. Infer higher expected access to opportunities when \(\rho_{\text{wealth}}\) is higher.
4. Therefore place early savings toward relocation and skills before passive investing.

Capital deployment argument
1. Separate operating needs from excess cash.
2. Treat excess idle cash as nonworking capital.
3. Reinvest that capital into the business or into higher-earning skill acquisition.
4. Remove the comfort cushion that makes performance slack acceptable.
5. Force the organization to recreate value repeatedly rather than sit on stored cash.

Sales conviction model
1. Distinguish product belief from technique.
2. Use the interview’s weighting: tactics are \(10\%\), conviction is \(90\%\).
3. Treat buyer response as sensitive to visible enthusiasm and credibility.
4. Conclude that lack of belief weakens conversion even when tactics are polished.

Rich-versus-wealthy burden example
1. Use the lecture’s personal definition that wealthy means billionaire scale.
2. Test the counterexample of a \( \$100\text{M} \) net worth with a \( \$50\text{M} \) yacht.
3. Use the stated annual operating burden \(>\$12\text{M}\).
4. Compute a carrying burden of roughly \(12\%\) of net worth per year.
5. Conclude that large headline wealth can still be too thin to support nonproductive luxury assets.
