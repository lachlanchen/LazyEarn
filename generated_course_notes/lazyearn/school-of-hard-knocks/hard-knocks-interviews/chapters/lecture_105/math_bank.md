## Derivation Steps
Compounding and the early 401(k) contribution:
1. Start with a small contribution \(PV\) or a sequence of contributions \(c\).
2. Leave the contribution invested over many periods.
3. Each period multiplies the balance by \(1+r\).
4. Earlier deposits receive more multiplication steps than later deposits.
5. Therefore a small early contribution can dominate a much larger late contribution in final effect.

Living below one’s means:
1. Treat income \(Y_t\) as the available resource base.
2. Keep expenditures plus debt burden below that base: \(E_t + DS_t < Y_t\).
3. The gap \(Y_t - (E_t + DS_t)\) is operating slack.
4. In bad times, this slack absorbs shocks.
5. If the inequality is reversed, bad times become financially dangerous.

Debt-paydown rule:
1. Reduce spending to \(E_{\text{needs},t}\), not lifestyle maximization.
2. Compute the remainder \(R_t = Y_t - E_{\text{needs},t}\).
3. Send that remainder to debt reduction.
4. Prioritize business debt first, per the operator’s advice.
5. As debt falls, fixed claims shrink and freedom rises.

First-facility capital formation:
1. Increase labor input early in the career.
2. Convert excess work into unusually large earned income.
3. Suppress nonessential consumption.
4. Accumulate \(K_0 = \sum (Y_t - E_t)\).
5. Use \(K_0\) to qualify for bank financing \(L\).
6. Use \(L\) to build or acquire the first facility.

Franchise scaling logic:
1. Begin with a working operating format.
2. Standardize product, process, and advertising.
3. Replicate the unit across more locations.
4. Let scale show up as rising \(N\), not as one-off invention at every site.
5. Experience and perseverance remain necessary conditions even under a strong franchise system.

Payroll-pressure leadership:
1. Weekly payroll creates a fixed obligation \(B_{\text{week}}\).
2. As \(B_{\text{week}}\) grows, more households depend on organizational success.
3. Leadership shifts from personal effort alone to coordinated team execution.
4. A rallying point or motivational focus becomes necessary.
5. Outwork still matters, but now it must be organizationally transmitted.

## Notation Choices
- Use \(E_t\) for expenses, not \(C_t\), so that \(c\) can remain available for retirement contributions.
- Use \(DS_t\) for debt-service burden and \(R_t\) for debt reduction; do not overload one symbol for both.
- Use \(K_0\) for initial equity capital before borrowing and \(L\) for the bank loan.
- Use \(N\) or \(N_t\) for count of business units; keep it discrete, since the lecture discusses store counts, not continuous scale.
- Use \(B_{\text{week}}\) for payroll burden to avoid conflict with debt-paydown notation.
- Use \(H/H_{\text{norm}}\) for work-intensity comparisons when preserving the “three times as much as a normal doctor” claim.
- Keep all notation explicitly editorial; the lecture itself does not supply symbols.
- Prefer simple scalar notation over optimization notation; there is no genuine formal model in the source.
