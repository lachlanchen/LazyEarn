## Derivation Steps
Implied e-commerce profitability
1. Take the fuller first-interview figures \(R \approx \$32\,\mathrm{M}\) and \(P \approx \$19\,\mathrm{M}\).
2. Define the implied margin by \(m:=P/R\).
3. Compute \(m \approx 19/32\).
4. Record \(m \approx 0.59\), i.e. roughly \(59\%\).
5. Flag the result as cautious because nearby montage numbers are spliced.

Seven-to-eight-figure scale transition
1. Start from the transcript claim that seven figures can often be done alone.
2. Treat founder time \(T_f\) as the binding constraint at higher scale.
3. Shift the founder’s role from task execution to idea generation \(I_f\).
4. Delegate execution to workers \(L_w\).
5. Treat \(I_f \to L_w \to O\) as the lecture’s main scaling mechanism.

Mindset and stewardship chain
1. Record the quoted heuristic \(\sim 0.8\,M + 0.2\,K\).
2. Attach \(M\) to stewardship, multiplication, and holding money rather than to motivation alone.
3. Use the lecture’s claim that a windfall without \(M\) is quickly lost.
4. Use the lecture’s counterclaim that \(M+K\) allows capital to be multiplied.

Function-versus-status comparison
1. Compare Timex and Rolex by identical timekeeping function.
2. Compare Volkswagen and Rolls Royce by identical transport function.
3. Separate core utility from prestige premium.
4. Use this as the lecture’s argument against treating visible luxury as the whole story.

Confidence accumulation
1. Make daily promises to oneself.
2. Keep those promises.
3. Repeated kept promises produce daily goal completion \(G_d\).
4. Repeated \(G_d\) builds confidence \(C\).
5. Higher \(C\) supports pursuit of larger goals \(G_{\text{larger}}\).

## Notation Choices
- Use \(R\) for revenue, \(P\) for profit, and \(m=P/R\) for margin throughout.
- Use \(T_f\) for founder time; do not invent more elaborate productivity notation unless needed later.
- Use \(M\) for mindset and \(K\) for skill set so that \(S\) remains free for scale regimes.
- Use \(S_{7}\) and \(S_{8}\) only as editorial shorthand for seven-figure and eight-figure states; the lecture itself gives no symbolic notation.
- Use \(\approx\) for quoted magnitudes and rounded ages.
- Use \(\to\) for process flow or causal sequencing, not formal proof.
- Use \(\Rightarrow\) only for strong lecture-level implication, and even then keep it heuristic.
- Keep dollar magnitudes in millions, e.g. \(\$32\,\mathrm{M}\), \(\$19\,\mathrm{M}\), \(\$10\,\mathrm{M}\).
- Treat \(\mathcal{P}\) as an editorial set of stabilizing supports, not as lecture notation.
- Avoid calculus, optimization, regression, or probability notation not warranted by the transcript.
