## Definitions And Objects

- \(V_{\mathrm{sale}}\): sale or transaction value of a company.
- \(t_{\mathrm{sale}}\): sale year attached to a named exit.
- \(T_{\mathrm{RE}}\): years spent in real estate by the Florida operator.
- \(P\): purchase price of a starter multifamily/commercial property.
- \(C_0\): initial cash/down payment available to the buyer.
- \(d\): down-payment ratio \(C_0/P\).
- \(\mathrm{LTV}\): loan-to-value ratio \(1-d\).
- \(A_n\): \(n\)-th acquired property/asset in the refinance-and-repeat cycle.
- \(E_{n+1}\): recycled equity/cash extracted to seed the next acquisition.
- \(H\): holding horizon in years; here used to distinguish long-hold investing from flipping.
- \(N_{\mathrm{people}}\): world population scale used in Morgan’s rarity comparison.
- \(N_{\mathrm{billionaires}}\): approximate billionaire count used in the same comparison.
- \(p_{\mathrm{billionaire}}\): contextual rarity ratio, not a deep model.
- \(\text{exposure}\): outward opportunity-seeking activity, e.g. lunches, conventions, networking.
- \(P(\text{opportunity})\): probability-like shorthand for the chance of meeting opportunity/luck.
- \(V_{\mathrm{company,early}}\): rough early company size in Repole’s startup anecdote.
- \(C_{\mathrm{needed}}\): rough initial capital need inferred from that anecdote.
- \(R\): revenue path or staircase across scale stages.
- \(R_{\mathrm{BodyArmor}}\): BodyArmor-specific revenue scale path.
- \(T_{\mathrm{survival}}\): first five years of entrepreneurship as the “survival years.”
- \(\text{self at }55,\ \text{self at }45,\ \text{self at }35\): temporal self-benchmarks in Repole’s me-versus-me doctrine.

## Derivation Steps

Real-estate entry arithmetic
1. Start with available cash \(C_0=\$100{,}000\).
2. Choose a target property price \(P\in[\$300{,}000,\$400{,}000]\).
3. Form the down-payment ratio \(d=C_0/P\).
4. Infer the financed share as \(\mathrm{LTV}=1-d\).
5. Use the example only as leverage arithmetic, not as a universal underwriting rule.

Refinance-and-repeat loop
1. Buy a small multifamily/commercial asset.
2. Manage it yourself.
3. Lease it yourself.
4. Repair or stabilize it.
5. Refinance once the asset is stronger.
6. Pull cash/equity out.
7. Use that extracted capital to buy the next asset.

Why “always buy” can coexist with “nobody can time the market”
1. Reject the flip frame.
2. Replace the relevant time variable with a long hold \(H\in\{10,20,30\}\).
3. Reduce the importance of short-run entry timing.
4. Treat long-horizon ownership as the resolution of market-timing anxiety.

Morgan rarity arithmetic
1. Take the approximate world population \(8\times 10^{9}\).
2. Take the approximate billionaire count \(3\times 10^{3}\).
3. Form the ratio \(p_{\mathrm{billionaire}}=N_{\mathrm{billionaires}}/N_{\mathrm{people}}\).
4. Use the result only to motivate “there is no clean secret,” not to derive a theory.

Assembled intelligence
1. Start with the question “Do you need to be smart?”
2. Replace “individual genius” with “smart enough to hire smart people.”
3. Convert the founder from sole capability source into capability assembler.
4. Conclude that organizational intelligence exceeds founder intelligence alone.

Opportunity through exposure
1. Reject the “sit at your desk and wait” posture.
2. Add lunches, conventions, happy hours, and deliberate networking.
3. Treat those actions as increasing exposure.
4. Treat luck/opportunity as something that arrives conditional on exposure, not in isolation.

Repole scale staircase
1. Begin at the small base: \(\$100{,}000\).
2. Step through the stated milestones: \(\$500{,}000,\ \$1\text{M},\ \$3\text{M},\ \$9\text{M},\ \$32\text{M},\ \$50\text{M},\ldots\).
3. Continue until the business reaches \(\$750\text{M}\).
4. Use the staircase to argue that large exits are built by passing through smaller scales in order.

Founder-ladder logic
1. Start with a tiny company, not a billion-dollar company.
2. Move from \(\$100\) to \(\$1{,}000\).
3. Then to \(\$100{,}000\).
4. Then to \(\$1\text{M}\).
5. Then to \(\$10\text{M}\).
6. Only after repeated scale transitions does the billion-dollar frame become plausible.

Failure definition
1. Ask whether failure means bankruptcy.
2. Explicitly deny that narrow definition.
3. Substitute bad months, bad days, losses, and payroll fear.
4. Recode failure as repeated contact with operating limits.
5. Preserve bankruptcy as one extreme case, not the only one.

Self-competition logic
1. Demote outside comparison to noise.
2. Compare current self to prior selves: 55 vs 45 vs 35.
3. Treat improvement as internal benchmarking rather than public ranking.
4. Distinguish being happy from becoming content.

## Notation Choices

- Use \(V\) for sale/transaction values and other large one-time monetary scale markers.
- Use \(R\) for revenue paths, staircases, and operating scale.
- Use \(T\) for durations in years, with subscripts for meaning: \(T_{\mathrm{RE}}, T_{\mathrm{survival}}\).
- Use \(P\) for property purchase price and \(C_0\) for initial cash/down payment.
- Use \(d\) for down-payment ratio and \(\mathrm{LTV}\) for leverage share.
- Use \(A_n\) for the \(n\)-th asset in the real-estate acquisition loop and \(E_{n+1}\) for extracted equity feeding the next purchase.
- Use \(H\) for investment holding horizon.
- Use \(N\) for population-scale counts when doing contextual ratios.
- Use arrows \(\to\) for process flows and stage ladders, not for equalities.
- Use \(\neq\) for strong lecture contrasts such as “failure is not only bankruptcy” and “enterprise capability is not founder capability alone.”
- Use \(\uparrow\) only for directional, monotone claims that are clearly inferential and not literal formulas, e.g. exposure versus opportunity.
- Keep large-money sequences in ordinary dollar notation with commas when the speaker gives milestone ladders verbatim.
- Reserve scientific notation for compact summary markers or derived ratios, e.g. \(\$8\times 10^9\) or \(3.75\times 10^{-7}\).
- When a lecture claim is qualitative but structurally important, prefer text inside math mode rather than forcing false precision:
  \[
  \text{market timing skill is not reliably exploitable}.
  \]
