## Derivation Steps
Partnership gain
1. Take the partnership acquisition price \(P_{\mathrm{buy}} = \$25\,\mathrm{M}\).
2. Take the later sale price \(P_{\mathrm{sell}} = \$55\,\mathrm{M}\).
3. Subtract to get gross gain: \(\Delta P = P_{\mathrm{sell}} - P_{\mathrm{buy}} = \$30\,\mathrm{M}\).
4. Divide to get gross multiple: \(M_{\mathrm{exit}} = P_{\mathrm{sell}}/P_{\mathrm{buy}} = 2.2\).
5. Keep this as gross deal arithmetic only; do not infer net personal proceeds.

Risk sizing
1. Let \(I\) be the amount committed to a trade or business risk.
2. Model the lecture’s worst case as a total wipeout of that position.
3. Write post-loss wealth as \(W_{\mathrm{after}} = W_{\mathrm{before}} - I\).
4. Impose the lecture’s rule: only choose \(I\) such that \(W_{\mathrm{after}}\) is still survivable.
5. Summarize the policy as “risk only what can go to zero without taking us down with it.”

Empathy-to-sale chain
1. Identify the other person’s actual problem rather than the product we want to push.
2. Translate that problem into the other person’s felt situation.
3. Speak to the pain point in the other person’s language.
4. Generate connection and trust.
5. Let the request for a solution arise from that trust.
6. Close the sale by solving the problem already identified.

Networking and speed
1. Start from a prior contact made at a golf tournament.
2. Call immediately while recognition is still warm.
3. Bring the contact in to see something real and valuable.
4. Convert the contact into chairman-level involvement.
5. Treat the episode as a speed-and-substance chain, not networking theater.

Time-to-market value
1. Take the buyer-side annual product revenue \(R_{\mathrm{year}} = \$12\,\mathrm{M}\).
2. Convert to monthly revenue: \(R_{\mathrm{month}} = R_{\mathrm{year}}/12 = \$1\,\mathrm{M}\).
3. Take the time saved \(\Delta t = 3\) months.
4. Multiply to get added buyer value: \(\Delta V = R_{\mathrm{month}}\Delta t = \$3\,\mathrm{M}\).
5. Compare the fee \(F = \$300\,\mathrm{k}\) to the added value.
6. Conclude that the fee is small relative to buyer gain if the launch-acceleration assumption is accepted.

Buyer surplus
1. Start from \(\Delta V = \$3.0\,\mathrm{M}\).
2. Subtract the fee \(F = \$0.3\,\mathrm{M}\).
3. Get buyer-side surplus \(S_{\mathrm{buyer}} = \$2.7\,\mathrm{M}\).
4. Compute the value-to-fee ratio \(\Delta V/F = 10\).
5. Use this to formalize the lecture’s line that the real question is not cost alone, but what the buyer gets.

Field-service margin
1. Take the reported revenue \(R_{\mathrm{field}} \approx \$30\,\mathrm{M}\).
2. Take the reported profit band \(\Pi_{\mathrm{field}} \approx \$2\text{--}3\,\mathrm{M}\).
3. Compute \(m = \Pi_{\mathrm{field}}/R_{\mathrm{field}}\).
4. Obtain an approximate range of \(6.7\%\) to \(10\%\).
5. Keep this as operating arithmetic, not as a net-worth inference.
