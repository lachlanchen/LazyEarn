## Derivation Steps
**Index-fund discipline**
1. Start with income $Y$.
2. Reserve a fixed share for the core index allocation.
3. Set that core share to $10\%$ of income.
4. Treat other investments as separate from that protected bucket.
5. Apply the rule mechanically rather than improvisationally.

**Scaling in tranches**
1. Do not treat growth as one smooth continuum.
2. Separate it into three levels, each with different demands.
3. Assume the second large jump will not be produced the same way as the third.
4. Ask what new source, capability, or structure powers the next tranche.
5. Re-estimate instead of recycling the previous recipe.

**From seven figures to eight figures**
1. Start with current success, but do not assume it is scalable.
2. Ask whether the first win came from luck, friends and family, or a one-off good idea.
3. Estimate whether the market is large enough for the next revenue level.
4. If the market is real, choose the right approach for expansion.
5. Bring in outside expertise rather than solving the next stage alone.

**Traction-first investing**
1. Observe the environment before allocating capital.
2. Filter out weak or temporary companies.
3. Identify where there is traction.
4. Invest only after the traction signal is credible.
5. Keep saving discipline in place while doing this.

**Failure-to-scale logic**
1. Remove emotional over-significance from small failures.
2. Learn from mistakes faster.
3. Shorten the iteration cycle.
4. Let those faster iterations compound over time.
5. Treat this compounding as a reason some startup ecosystems outperform others.

**Penthouse dependency chain**
1. Scaling requires cash.
2. Cash for fast scale often requires fundraising.
3. Scaling and hiring require leadership.
4. Leadership depends on visionary clarity.
5. Visionary clarity depends on having a genuinely strong product.
6. Talent acquisition further depends on compensation.
7. Compensation depends on having a product strong enough to support it.

**E-commerce hierarchy**
1. Begin with the winning product.
2. Treat marketing as amplification rather than foundation.
3. Treat sales as the next layer after marketing.
4. If the product is strong, the marketing burden falls.
5. If the marketing is strong, the sales burden falls.
6. If all three are strong together, scale becomes much easier.

**Office-building adjacency case**
1. Own the office building.
2. Meet a tenant through that ownership position.
3. Gain visibility into the tenant’s struggling operating business.
4. Acquire the business using superior logistics or operating knowledge.
5. Benefit from later equity appreciation when the company scales.

**Comparison trap**
1. Compare yourself to richer or more performatively wealthy people.
2. Internalize the comparison as insecurity.
3. Spend to keep up.
4. Damage savings and capital allocation.
5. Break the loop by returning attention to one’s own pace and position.
