## Derivation Steps
**Gel-nails unit economics**
1. Read off the selling price: \(P_{\mathrm{gel}}=\$25\).
2. Read off the cost basis: \(C_{\mathrm{gel}}=\$1\).
3. Subtract to get unit profit: \(\pi_{\mathrm{gel}}=24\).
4. Divide by price to get gross margin: \(m_{\mathrm{gel}}=24/25\approx 96\%\).
5. Use this as the lecture’s first clean mechanism for why reinvestment can dominate borrowing.

**Reinvestment scaling**
1. Start with the stated initial capital \(K_0\approx \$200\).
2. Add operating profit generated from high-margin sales.
3. Subtract only whatever is taken out personally, \(W_t\).
4. Iterate the recursion \(K_{t+1}=K_t+\Pi_t-W_t\).
5. Connect the verbal claim “all from profit” to the zero-borrowing structure.

**Frugality versus status purchase**
1. Take the stated list value of the Rolls-Royce: about \$520k.
2. Take the stated auction purchase price: about \$175k.
3. Compute the discount \(\Delta P\approx \$345k\).
4. Use the difference as evidence that wealth retention is prioritized over display consumption.

**Middleman capture**
1. Identify the chain owner \(\leftrightarrow\) dealmaker \(\leftrightarrow\) operator.
2. Note that the dealmaker is not extracting the resource directly.
3. The lecture’s claim is that transfer coordination itself can capture value.
4. Use the intermediation chain as the formal object behind the “wealth transfer” remark.

**Mafia gasoline arithmetic**
1. Take the stated flow \(q_{\mathrm{gas}}\approx 5\times 10^8\) gallons per month.
2. Take the stated spread \(s_{\mathrm{gas}}\in[0.30,0.40]\) dollars per gallon.
3. Form the implied monthly gross take \(\Pi_{\mathrm{gross,month}}=q_{\mathrm{gas}}s_{\mathrm{gas}}\).
4. Compare that implication to the separately quoted weekly figures.
5. Do not reconcile the mismatch silently; keep the arithmetic visible and flagged.

**Professional income versus billionaire-scale asset ownership**
1. Record the surgeon’s stated one-year income \(Y_{\mathrm{surgeon}}\approx \$9\) million.
2. Observe that the lecture does not present this as final-scale wealth.
3. Add a second component for industrial ownership: \(W_{\mathrm{industrial}}\).
4. Write \(W_{\mathrm{total}}=W_{\mathrm{practice}}+W_{\mathrm{industrial}}\).
5. Place the Ghana pharmaceutical plant inside the second term.

**Chipotle store-growth arithmetic**
1. Record current annual revenue \(R_{\mathrm{Chipotle}}\approx \$13\) billion.
2. Record current store count \(N_{\mathrm{Chipotle,now}}\gtrsim 3800\).
3. Record annual addition rate \(\Delta N_{\mathrm{year}}\in[315,340]\).
4. Record North America target \(N_{\mathrm{NA,target}}\approx 7000\).
5. Use \(N_{t+1}=N_t+\Delta N_t\) as the minimal growth recurrence.
6. Treat the international “double that again” line as a target extension, not a proven forecast.

**Fortress balance sheet and anti-franchising control**
1. Record cash reserves \(C_{\mathrm{cash}}\gtrsim \$2\) billion.
2. Record debt \(D_{\mathrm{debt}}=0\).
3. Infer that external franchise capital is not needed for expansion.
4. Connect internal funding capacity to brand-control arguments.

**Responsibility versus accountability**
1. Let \(T\) be one operational task.
2. Let \(\mathcal R(T)\) be the set of people sharing work on \(T\).
3. If several people are responsible but no one is designated accountable, set \(a(T)=\varnothing\).
4. Conclude that execution can stall even with shared responsibility.
5. Preserve the lecture’s managerial point: one person must own the outcome.

## Notation Choices
- Use subscripts by case rather than by speaker name when possible: `gel`, `gas`, `surgeon`, `Chipotle`.

- Use \(\pi\) for unit profit and \(\Pi\) for aggregate profit over an interval.
- Use \(m\) for margin fraction, not markup.
- Use \(K_t\) for retained operating capital through time.
- Use \(W_t\) for owner withdrawals; do not use \(D_t\) here, because debt is needed later.

- Use \(N\) for counts: countries, stores, or participants, always with an explicit subscript.
- Use \(R\) for annual revenue, not profit.
- Use \(q\) for quantity flow and \(s\) for per-unit spread in the gasoline case.
- Use \(a(T)\) for accountable owner of task \(T\), and \(\mathcal R(T)\) for the responsible set.
- Keep arrows such as \(\longrightarrow\) only for directional business logic, not for hard equalities.
