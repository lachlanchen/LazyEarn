## Derivation Steps
1. Hours and cumulative advantage
   1. Set daily commitment levels \(h_A=10\) and \(h_B=9\).
   2. Model yearly accumulation by \(H(T)=hT\) with \(T=365\).
   3. Compute \(H_A(365)-H_B(365)=365\).
   4. Conclude that even a one-hour daily edge becomes a large yearly gap.

2. Focus before diversification
   1. Begin with the observed fact that Smith has many roles.
   2. Reframe plurality through the arrow image: one sharpened tip first.
   3. Let \(x_{\mathrm{tip}}\) denote the mastered primary craft.
   4. Only after \(x_{\mathrm{tip}}\) breaks through do the later domains \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{trail}}\) follow.

3. Confidence from tolerated failure
   1. Reject “confidence” as an initial fixed possession.
   2. Start instead with willingness to look stupid in public.
   3. Add repeated rejection, failed jokes, and visible mistakes.
   4. Interpret the later social appearance of confidence as the residue of tolerated exposure.
   5. Use Smith’s explicit numeric image \(p_{\text{hit}}=3/10\) as a transcript-backed benchmark for acceptable failure.

4. Advice filter
   1. Pose the problem: from whom should we take advice?
   2. Restrict the admissible set to people who have actually done the relevant thing.
   3. Move beyond explicit advice to behavior-reading and observation.
   4. End at intuition/self-trust rather than outsourced certainty.

5. Relationship capital over time
   1. Treat present encounters as time-dependent rather than static.
   2. Index the same contact at later ages or roles: \(16 \to 24 \to 34\).
   3. Interpret \(\mathcal R(t)\) as an evolving network rather than a one-time meeting.
   4. Infer that even a small present interaction can have delayed future value.

6. Money threshold and reversal
   1. Start from the claim that money does not buy happiness.
   2. Introduce a speaker-level threshold \(m_\ast \approx \$1{,}000{,}000/\text{year}\).
   3. After \(m_\ast\), the lecture claims problems grow rather than shrink.
   4. Connect that reversal to social suspicion, friendship distortion, and “attack zone” language.

7. Clifftop and rock bottom symmetry
   1. Start from the familiar belief that change happens at rock bottom.
   2. Add the corresponding upper abyss: clifftop after material saturation.
   3. Treat both states as failures of material satisfaction.
   4. Resolve both through \(S_{\mathrm{self}}\), not through stronger external branches.

8. Bird and branch
   1. Ask whether the bird trusts the branch or its wings.
   2. Note the transcript’s empirical image: branch can fail, bird still survives.

   4. Map that analogy onto self-trust over material or situational safety.
