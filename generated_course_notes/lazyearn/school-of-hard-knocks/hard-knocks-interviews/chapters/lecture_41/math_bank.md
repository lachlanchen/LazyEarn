## Derivation Steps

Organic growth / bootstrap scaling
1. Start with no outside capital.
2. Save enough cash to purchase the first productive asset.
3. Use that asset to generate operating profit.
4. Retain profit instead of extracting it.
5. Reinvest retained profit into additional capacity.
6. Repeat until the business footprint expands from one asset to a network.

Negotiation by walking away
1. Observe an immediate offer \(S_0 = \$30\,\mathrm{M}\).
2. Refuse the offer despite its size.
3. Keep the claim active rather than closing at the table.
4. Return later under improved terms.
5. Realize a later outcome \(S_1 > \$50\,\mathrm{M}\).
6. Conclude, in this case, that patience dominated immediate acceptance.

Corporate promotion ladder
1. Complete assigned work for the day.
2. Ask for additional work from the direct boss.
3. Receive delegated pieces of the boss’s own work.
4. Learn the next layer of responsibility by doing it.
5. Become legible as the boss’s right-hand person.
6. Get selected when a larger role opens.
7. Perform at the higher level and repeat the cycle.

Pressure acclimation
1. Move into roles with incrementally higher responsibility.
2. Encounter harder problems at each stage.
3. Repeated exposure reduces overreaction.
4. Tolerance grows even though difficulty also grows.
5. Pressure therefore scales with role, but so does capacity to absorb it.

AI-to-infrastructure demand chain
1. Take the speaker’s heuristic that AI search is roughly \(10\times\) a Google search.
2. Infer higher computational load per query.
3. Translate higher computational load into higher data-center demand.
4. Translate higher data-center demand into higher standby and baseload power demand.
5. Translate higher power demand into higher demand for generators and related equipment.

Private-equity acquisition loop
1. Identify a company with assets and operating cash flow.
2. Acquire the company.
3. Borrow against the acquired asset base.
4. Use the company’s own cash flow to service and reduce debt.
5. Improve equity value as leverage declines and operations continue.
6. Reuse track record and capital base to support later deals.

Margin versus velocity of cash
1. Do not judge a business only by per-turn margin \(m\).
2. Count how many times cash turns in a period, \(v\).
3. Multiply thin margin by many turns rather than reading margin in isolation.
4. Recognize that high \(v\) can offset low \(m\).
5. Use the Walmart example as the lecture’s canonical illustration.

- Use \(m\) and \(v\) for margin and cash velocity; keep \(\Pi\) for period profit.
- Use \(\to\) for process or stage transition, not equality.
- Use \(\approx\) whenever the transcript is approximate, rounded, or colloquial.

- Treat “geographical diversity” and “sector diversity” as labeled dimensions, not as formal vectors unless a later diagram truly needs that extra structure.
