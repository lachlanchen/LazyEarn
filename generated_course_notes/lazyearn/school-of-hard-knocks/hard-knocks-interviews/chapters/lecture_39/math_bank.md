## Notation Choices

- Use \(\mapsto\) for symbolic encoding, never \(=\), when a number stands for biography rather than magnitude.
- Use \(C\) for one-time costs, \(O\) for recurring operating costs, \(I\) for disposable income, and \(W\) for net worth.
- Use \(N_{\bullet}\) for counts, \(r_{\bullet}\) for rates or shares, \(h_{\bullet}\) for time in hours, and \(t_{\bullet}\) for year markers.
- Use \(\approx\) for speaker-reported heuristics and approximate milestones.
- Use \(\gtrsim\) for practical thresholds inferred from the lecture but not stated as exact equalities.
- Use \(\Rightarrow\) only for local operational implication, not for theorem-level proof.
- Use \(F(\cdot)\) only for the startup funnel and only qualitatively; do not imply a fitted functional form.
- Keep all money in explicit USD units.
- Treat “bankability” as a state variable \(B_{\text{bankable}}\) only if the later chapter benefits from a compact symbol; otherwise keep it verbal.
- Do not introduce calculus, optimization, stochastic notation, or matrix/operator language; the source does not support it.
