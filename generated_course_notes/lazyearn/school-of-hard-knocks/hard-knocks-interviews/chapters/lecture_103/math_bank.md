## Derivation Steps
Profit split mechanism
1. Take the spoken example \(P_{\mathrm{biz}}=\$600{,}000\).
2. Impose equal ownership \(\alpha=\tfrac12\).
3. Compute each side’s share as \(\alpha P_{\mathrm{biz}}\).
4. Obtain \(\$300{,}000\) per partner.
5. Use the example to illustrate owner income without direct day-to-day execution.

Delegation threshold heuristic
1. Model the owner’s own task quality as \(q_{\mathrm{owner}}\).
2. Model a candidate operator’s task quality as \(q_{\mathrm{delegate}}\).
3. Compare the two against the spoken \(70\%\) rule.
4. Delegate when \(q_{\mathrm{delegate}} \ge 0.7\,q_{\mathrm{owner}}\).
5. Treat the released owner attention as capacity for additional acquisition or structure-building.

Concentration then diversification
1. Start with a single concentrated business focus.
2. Let that focus generate meaningful cash flow and wealth \(W\).
3. Delay diversification until \(W\) is large enough to replace the day job and support discretionary moves.
4. Reallocate surplus from concentration into preserving or adjacent assets.
5. Interpret diversification as stage two, not as a substitute for stage one.

Vertical integration loop
1. Identify repeated outflows from the operating business.
2. Name the categories explicitly mentioned: HVAC, electrical, plumbing, architecture, real estate, commissions.
3. Acquire or control some of those adjacent providers or assets.
4. Convert former external margin into internal profit \(\Pi_{\mathrm{integrated}}-\Pi_{\mathrm{core}}\).
5. Read rent-to-self as the clearest verbal example of this mechanism.

Liquidity discipline
1. Set spending near half of income: \(E \approx \tfrac12 I\).
2. Hold the remainder partly as liquid reserve \(C_{\mathrm{dry}}\).
3. Use \(C_{\mathrm{dry}}\) for people, ideas, investments, or lower-valuation opportunities.
4. Avoid the state \(C_{\mathrm{dry}}=0\), because that blocks action when opportunity appears.

Scale-to-utility chain
1. Increase people, technology, sales, marketing, and commercialization capacity.
2. Use that capacity to support more demand reliably.
3. Push adoption until not using the product creates a disadvantage.
4. Mark that threshold as \(U_{\mathrm{industry}}\).
5. Treat the tipping point as the lecture’s operative definition of real scale.

Healthcare progress count
1. Take the early-stage count as roughly \(12\text{--}15\) good oncology drugs.
2. Take the later count as \(40+\).
3. Interpret the change as evidence of sector progress, not as a precise growth model.
4. Use immune therapy as the named causal driver, but without formal quantification.
