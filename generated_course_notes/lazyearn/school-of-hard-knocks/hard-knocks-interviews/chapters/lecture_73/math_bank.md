## Definitions And Objects
- \(R\): annual revenue when the speaker explicitly says “revenue.”
- \(A\): annual reported dollar amount when the transcript does not cleanly distinguish revenue, profit, compensation, or owner draw.
- \(Y_{\text{paper}}\): income or profit recognized “on paper.”
- \(C_{\text{personal}}\): immediately available personal cash.
- \(T\): tax liability associated with reported income.
- \(r_{\text{factor}}\): effective financing rate under invoice factoring / very expensive short-term capital.
- \(r_{\text{bank}}\): later bank line-of-credit rate after balance-sheet improvement.
- \(B_t\): borrowed working capital at time \(t\).
- \(P_t\): periodic operating profit generated by the business.
- \(D_t\): distributions or owner withdrawals taken out of the business.
- \(E_t\): retained earnings / accumulated internal capital.
- \(C_i\): business expenditure or capital outlay \(i\).
- \(\Delta \Pi_i\): incremental business gain attributed to expenditure \(i\).
- \(\mathrm{ROI}_i\): return-on-investment measure attached to expenditure \(i\).
- \(V_{\text{deal}}\): value of accepting a deal.
- \(V_{\text{walk}}\): value of the outside option, i.e. walking away.
- \(N_{\text{employees}}\): headcount carried by the enterprise or practice.
- “Self-employed”: output remains bottlenecked by founder labor.
- “Scalable business”: output survives and grows beyond one person through team structure.

## Derivation Steps
Delegation and scale:
1. Start with the founder’s explicit one-person capacity limit.
2. Record the spoken comparison \(R_{\text{company}} \approx \$300\,\mathrm{M}\) versus \(R_{\text{solo}} \approx \$3\,\mathrm{M}\).
3. Treat the gap as evidence that team structure, not personal effort alone, drives scale.
4. Convert the verbal contrast into the rule “work on the business, not only in it.”

Paper income versus personal liquidity:
1. Take the reported paper income \(Y_{\text{paper}} \approx \$3\,\mathrm{M}\).
2. Keep the matching tax bill \(T \approx \$950\,\mathrm{k}\).
3. Keep the reported personal cash \(C_{\text{personal}} \approx \$10\,\mathrm{k}\).
4. Note that profits were said to remain inside the company.
5. Conclude that reported income and personal liquidity can diverge sharply.

Expensive capital to cheaper capital:
1. Begin with no starting cash.
2. Use invoice factoring / high-cost borrowing at \(r_{\text{factor}} \approx 21\%\).
3. Retain each week’s profit in the business instead of distributing it.
4. Reduce next period borrowing because internal cash now covers more operating needs.
5. Build retained earnings and a stronger balance sheet.
6. Refinance into bank credit at \(r_{\text{bank}} \approx 6\%\text{ to }8\%\).
7. Translate the rate drop into improved monthly profit \(\Delta \Pi_{\text{monthly}} \approx \$20\,\mathrm{k}\text{ to }\$25\,\mathrm{k}\).

ROI discipline:
1. Treat each business dollar as an investment candidate, not as casual spend.
2. Attach a measurable business return to that spend.
3. Reject or question outlays whose return cannot be computed.
4. Only after that discipline does personal consumption become acceptable.

Negotiation logic:
1. Do not make the deal a necessity.
2. Preserve an outside option.
3. Use the ability to walk away as the source of bargaining leverage.
4. Represent the rule compactly as \(V_{\text{deal}} \ge V_{\text{walk}}\).

Private-practice business logic:
1. Start from the doctor’s claim that medicine and the business of medicine differ.
2. Add payroll and practice-survival constraints.
3. Combine efficient care with solvency rather than treating them as opposites.
4. Conclude that business literacy is part of sustaining patient care.

## Notation Choices
- Use \(R\) only for quantities explicitly called revenue in the transcript.
- Use \(A\) for annual dollar figures when the lecture is ambiguous about revenue versus income versus owner pay.
- Use \(Y_{\text{paper}}\) for paper income and \(C_{\text{personal}}\) for personal cash; do not collapse both into a single “income” symbol.
- Use \(T\) for taxes, \(r\) for financing rates, \(B\) for borrowed working capital, \(E\) for retained earnings, \(P\) for periodic profit, and \(D\) for withdrawals.
- Use \(\Pi\) for profit-like gain inside editorial reconstructions; keep it generic.
- Set \(\mathrm{ROI}\) in upright roman type.
- Use descriptive subscripts such as \(\text{company}\), \(\text{solo}\), \(\text{factor}\), \(\text{bank}\), \(\text{personal}\), \(\text{walk}\).
- Keep spoken quantities approximate with \(\approx\); do not introduce fake precision.
- Keep rates in percent form, matching the lecture’s wording.
- Do not introduce formal finance machinery not present in the lecture: no discount factors, CAPM notation, WACC symbols, or detailed tax-accounting identities.
