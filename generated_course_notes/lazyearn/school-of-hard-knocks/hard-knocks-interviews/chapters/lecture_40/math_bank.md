## Derivation Steps
1. VC filter: start with \(N_{\mathrm{deals}}\in[600,800]\); keep only \(N_{\mathrm{yes}}\in[0,2]\); infer \(p_{\mathrm{yes}}\ll 1\); conclude that an ordinary pitch is structurally unlikely to win.
2. Burn-the-boats logic: commit before certainty; take a risk others avoid; accept possible failure; constrain failure so \(L_{\mathrm{fail}}<L_{\mathrm{ruin}}\); preserve the ability to play again.
3. Geography selection: identify the strategic game first; map Silicon Valley to software, New York to finance, Los Angeles to media; choose the arena where the chosen game has maximum surface area.
4. Network bootstrap problem: early user enters with \(V_{\mathrm{net}}(n)\approx 0\); repeated objection is “no value, no value, no value”; the core bet is that \(n\) can still be increased before immediate value is obvious; later network thickness raises \(V_{\mathrm{net}}(n)\).
5. Networking doctrine: begin with \(U_t>0\), not with a request; useful insight raises attention; repeated usefulness raises \(C_t\); higher \(C_t\) becomes real relationship rather than fake “networking.”
6. Negotiation psychology: seller-push makes the other side feel powerful; buyer-pull makes them feel they are acting on their own desire; therefore increase \(D_{\mathrm{buy}}\) instead of visibly increasing seller pressure.
7. Father’s decision rule: before decision, short-term optionality is high; choosing door \(A\) lowers \(O_{\mathrm{short}}\); concentrated execution on \(A\) raises \(O_{\mathrm{long}}\); long-run opportunity requires local closure.
8. Multi-time game: SocialNet fails; failure does not terminate the path; treat the sequence as repeated play rather than one-shot evaluation; learning from failure is retained as input to the next move.
9. Enterprise pivot at LinkedIn: initial expectation is individual subscriptions; companies begin asking to buy; outbound description of product returns inbound purchase orders; observed enterprise demand overrides initial consumer assumption.
10. Ten-year game: most actors optimize on \(H=1\); Hoffman chooses \(H=10\); compounding over \(10\) years creates separation; network growth is prioritized first; monetization is built on top later via \(M(N)\).
