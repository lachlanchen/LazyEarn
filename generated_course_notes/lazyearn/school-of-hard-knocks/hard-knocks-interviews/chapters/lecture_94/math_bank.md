## Derivation Steps
1. Mentorship multiple.
   Take the stated spend \(C_{\text{mentor}}=\$55{,}000\).
   Take the stated resulting scale \(R_{\text{post-mentor}}\gtrsim \$5{,}000{,}000\text{/yr}\).
   Form the gross multiple \(m_{\text{mentor}}=R_{\text{post-mentor}}/C_{\text{mentor}}\).
   Mark the result as a revenue multiple, not a profit multiple.

2. Learning-spend multiple.
   Take the stated learning cost \(C_{\text{learn}}=\$500{,}000\).
   Take the stated resulting revenue \(R_{\text{learn}}=\$33{,}000{,}000\).
   Compute \(m_{\text{learn}}=R_{\text{learn}}/C_{\text{learn}}=66\).
   Keep the claim attached to the speaker; do not generalize it into a law.

3. Value-before-price calculation.
   Set the bag price at \(P_{\text{bag}}=\$1{,}000\).
   Set hypothetical contents at \(V_{\text{inside}}=\$100{,}000\).
   Compute the foregone upside \(U_{\text{bag}}=V_{\text{inside}}-P_{\text{bag}}=\$99{,}000\).
   Interpret the step as the lecture’s core rule: inspect value before reacting to cost.

4. Four-vertical business addition.
   Take the explicit target \(n_{\text{verticals}}\ge 4\).
   Use the given example \(P_{\text{high-ticket}}=\$50{,}000\) with \(N=4\).
   Compute added annual revenue \(R_{\text{high-ticket}}=P_{\text{high-ticket}}N=\$200{,}000\).
   Treat this as one vertical among several, not as a full business model.

5. Savings-rate structure.
   Take the example income \(Y=\$100{,}000\).
   Use the stated savings range \(s\in[0.20,0.25]\).
   Compute \(S=sY\in[\$20{,}000,\$25{,}000]\).
   Embed this inside the broader claim that wealth depends on allocation structure, not only on headline income.

6. Wealth-structure bookkeeping.
   Start from annual savings \(S\).
   Add growth in real-estate equity \(\Delta E_{\mathrm{RE}}\).
   Add growth in investment assets \(\Delta A_{\mathrm{inv}}\).
   Add retained or reinvested cash flow \(CF_{\mathrm{ret}}\).
   Summarize cautiously as \(\Delta W\approx S+\Delta E_{\mathrm{RE}}+\Delta A_{\mathrm{inv}}+CF_{\mathrm{ret}}\).

7. Human-capital compounding.
   Record the temporary pay cut from about \(\$150{,}000\) to about \(\$30{,}000\).
   Interpret the difference as short-run income sacrificed for learning.
   Model skill accumulation with \(H_{t+1}=H_t+\Delta H_t\).
   Let earnings depend positively on skill, \(Y_t=f(H_t)\) with \(f'(H)>0\).
   Use this to motivate the transcript’s claim that later earnings can dominate earlier wage-chasing.

8. Credit-leverage chain.
   Introduce a tradeline \(T\) to the profile.
   Keep the speaker’s claim that this positively affects \(4/5\) credit metrics.
   Track the stylized score movement \(C_{\text{score}}:600\to700\).
   Map the improved profile to claimed access \(L_{\text{access}}\in[\$50{,}000,\$100{,}000]\).
   Treat the last step as a narrated financing pathway to start, grow, or scale a business.

9. Brand-consistency filter.
   Declare a set \(\mathcal{F}\) of what the brand stands for.
   Declare a set \(\mathcal{G}\) of what the brand stands against.
   Evaluate each public action or statement \(a\).
   Keep \(a\) only if \(\chi_{\text{brand}}(a)=1\), meaning it coheres with \((\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})\).
   Use this as an editorial formalization of the “everything you say, everything you do” rule.

## Notation Choices
- Use \(R\) for annual business revenue claims, never for profit.
- Use \(C\) for costs or spends, with mechanism-specific subscripts such as \(C_{\text{mentor}}\) and \(C_{\text{learn}}\).
- Use \(m\) for revenue multiple, defined only as \(R/C\); do not call it ROI unless the notes explicitly explain the abuse of terminology.
- Use \(P\) for purchase price and \(V\) for underlying value in the bag analogy.
- Use \(U\) for upside or surplus, defined by \(U=V-P\).
- Use \(Y\) for annual personal income, \(s\) for savings rate, and \(S=sY\) for annual savings flow.
- Use \(W\) for wealth stock and \(\Delta W\) for annual or period wealth change.
- Use \(E_{\mathrm{RE}}\) for real-estate equity and \(A_{\mathrm{inv}}\) for investment assets; keep insurance as an allocation bucket in prose unless a cleaner symbolic treatment is needed.
- Use \(H_t\) for human capital indexed by age or time \(t\); use \(Y_t\) for earnings indexed the same way.
- Use \(C_{\text{score}}\) for credit score and \(L_{\text{access}}\) for accessible credit capacity.
- Use \(T\) for a tradeline addition; do not introduce a full credit-scoring formula because the transcript does not specify one.
- Use \(\mathcal{F}\) and \(\mathcal{G}\) for “for” and “against” brand commitments, and \(\chi_{\text{brand}}\) as the alignment indicator.
- Use \(\approx\) for editorial reconstructions, \(\gtrsim\) for “over/about,” and interval notation such as \([a,b]\) for quoted numeric ranges.
