## Derivation Steps
1. Notional-value example from Peter’s Visa illustration.
   1. Take \(N = 1000\) shares.
   2. Use \(p \in [\$400,\$500]\) per share.
   3. Apply \(V_{\mathrm{notional}} = Np\).
   4. Obtain \(V_{\mathrm{notional}} \approx \$400{,}000\text{--}\$500{,}000\).
   5. Use this to explain why large dollar flow can arise from relatively ordinary share counts in high-price names.

2. Consumer-goods depreciation versus asset accumulation.
   1. Let \(P_0\) be the purchase price of the item.
   2. Immediately after purchase, set \(V_{\mathrm{stuff}}(t_0^+) < P_0\).
   3. Interpret closet spending as negative or non-compounding deployment of capital.
   4. Contrast this with the intended investment case \(\mathbb{E}[V_{\mathrm{stock}}(t)] > P_0\).

3. Pre-profit cash-flow discipline.
   1. Begin in the stage where profitability is not yet secured.
   2. Require \(CF_t > 0\) to keep the company alive.
   3. Pair this with positive or improving \(OM_t\).
   4. Only after that does shareholder-return logic become the dominant frame.

4. Private-to-public timing gate.
   1. First establish \(M_{\mathrm{def}}\).
   2. Then establish \(GTM\).
   3. Only then assess whether funding need \(K\) is large enough to justify public execution.
   4. This yields the lecture’s order: strategic clarity before capital-market exposure.

5. Opportunity-through-visibility logic.
   1. Peter’s income collapses so that \(Y(t)\approx 0\) for roughly two years.
   2. He continues dressing, showing up, and staying in the field.
   3. A subway encounter occurs precisely because he remains publicly reachable.
   4. That encounter restarts trading and produces a relationship lasting about \(20\) years.
   5. The implied inference is that withdrawal suppresses opportunity arrival.

6. Personal flow versus exchange-wide flow.
   1. Peter’s own daily range is \(V_{\mathrm{Peter,daily}} \in [\$0.5,\$1.0]\times 10^9\).
   2. The broader floor is described as \(N_{\mathrm{shares}} \approx 1.2\times 10^9\) shares and roughly \(\$10^{12}\) in daily value.
   3. Therefore his personal book is large but still a subset of exchange-wide throughput.

7. Technology compression of execution.
   1. Older floor structure used roughly five rooms and paper-heavy handling.
   2. Current infrastructure can send \(N_{\mathrm{orders}} \approx 1000\) orders in \(5\text{--}10\) seconds.
   3. This is the lecture’s mechanism for why the exchange can now compress far larger flow through fewer physical rooms.

8. Organic attention leverage.
   1. Start from the claim that one post may reach \(A_{\mathrm{post}}\approx 10^7\).
   2. Treat attention as a first-stage commercial input, not merely vanity.
   3. Infer that marginal creative output can have nonlinear downstream business consequences.

- Use \(Y\) for personal annual earnings or income flow.
- Use \(V\) for dollar value or notional value; use subscripts to distinguish personal trading flow, item value, and floor-wide value.
- Use \(T_{\mathrm{AI}}\) for the 12–18 month forecast horizon, not \(H\), so that happiness can be written as \(\mathcal{H}\).
- Use \(\mathcal{F}\) for freedom and \(\mathcal{H}\) for happiness in the closing money distinction.
- Use \(P_0\) for purchase price and \(t_0^+\) for the immediate post-purchase moment.
- Use \(N\) for share count and \(p\) for share price, with \(V_{\mathrm{notional}} = Np\) as the standard identity.
- Use \(CF_t\) and \(OM_t\) for cash flow and operating margin through time.
- Use \(M_{\mathrm{def}}\), \(GTM\), and \(K\) for market definition, go-to-market clarity, and capital need in the public/private decision rule.
- Keep all ranges explicit with interval notation when the speaker gives approximate bounds rather than exact values.
