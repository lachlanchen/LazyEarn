## Derivation Steps

- Ramsey anti-debt logic
  1. Debt creates fixed obligations.
  2. Fixed obligations amplify the damage of revenue interruption.
  3. Therefore more debt means more fragility.
  4. Organic growth is slower, but it avoids those fixed claims.
  5. COVID is used as the test case proving why the slower path can survive the shock.

- Ramsey cash-funded property accumulation
  1. Buy land first, even before having cash to build.
  2. Hold the dirt while the operating company grows.
  3. Save and watch the cash flow rather than borrow.
  4. Reinvest what the firm produces into physical buildout.
  5. Let the property become a productive and appreciating asset.

- Ramsey crisis buying
  1. Maintain cash rather than full leverage.
  2. Wait for a dislocation such as 2008.
  3. Buy assets at deep discounts.
  4. Convert patience into asymmetric acquisition power.

- Ramsey real-estate snowball
  1. Start with the first property.
  2. Let debt-free cash flow come off the property.
  3. Use that excess cash to help fund the next property.
  4. Repeat the process.
  5. The first few properties are hardest because the snowball is still small.

- Jimmy’s measurement principle
  1. The mentor’s lesson is “if you can’t measure it, you can’t manage it.”
  2. Once numbers are known, process can be disciplined.
  3. Once process is disciplined, it can be copied across stores.
  4. Store growth then becomes repeatable rather than improvised.

- Jimmy’s intelligibility filter
  1. Do not buy what cannot be understood.
  2. Reduce the decision to something legible on one sheet of paper.
  3. Prefer assets one can relate to concretely.
  4. Land and farm ground pass that test for Jimmy.

- Jimmy’s operator bet
  1. Asset stories are noisy.
  2. Entrepreneur quality is treated as the decisive filter.
  3. Performance matters more than narrative excuses.
  4. Capital is therefore allocated to the jockey before the horse.

- Jimmy’s turnaround and franchise redesign
  1. A large store base contains many failing units.
  2. The response is not to sell more aggressively.
  3. The response is to tell the truth about the grind.
  4. Franchises are sold one at a time rather than in large territories.
  5. Better selection and truth-telling improve operating quality.

- Jimmy’s delivery/location arbitrage
  1. Prime A locations are too expensive.
  2. B/C locations reduce rent burden.
  3. Delivery plus campus sampling expands the demand radius.
  4. Lower rent is combined with stronger off-site demand.
  5. The result is A-level sales on cheaper real estate.

- Charlie reserve logic
  1. Income streams can stop.
  2. One must not assume the spigot is permanent.
  3. Save during strong periods.
  4. Let reserves cover drought periods.
  5. The time horizon may be much longer than a single “rainy day.”

- Charlie restart logic
  1. Public institutions can impose a timeout.
  2. That does not automatically destroy inner agency.
  3. Self-belief preserves restart capacity.
  4. Therefore a fresh start remains available after public collapse.

## Notation Choices

- Use \(R\) for business revenue and sales-like flow at the company level.
- Use \(V\) for asset or company valuation.
- Use \(D\) for debt and \(DS\) for debt service; do not overload \(D\) for duration or discount.
- Use \(F\) for fragility when formalizing Ramsey’s anti-debt claim.
- Use \(CF_t\) for operating cash flow and \(K_t\) for retained capital / available capital stock.
- Use \(I_t\) only for reinvestment into productive assets; do not also use \(I\) for generic investment return.
- Reserve \(I(a)\) specifically for the intelligibility indicator in Jimmy’s “one page” filter.
- Use \(N\) for counts: stores, failing stores, Pizza Huts, etc.
- Use \(q_{\mathrm{operator}}\) for operator quality.
- Use \(r_{BC}\) for B/C-location rent and \(Q_A\) for A-level sales; avoid \(S_A\) because \(S_t\) is reserved for savings.
- Use \(Y_t\) for personal income inflow in Charlie’s section and \(S_t\) for reserve savings.
- Prefer \(\approx\) for quoted dollar figures and counts, since the transcript is conversational rather than audited.
- Prefer \(\Rightarrow\) for causal or heuristic business logic, not for strict mathematical implication.
- Keep any strong maxims such as “Debt equals risk” or “measure it/manage it” in prose first, with notation only as a light formal shadow.
