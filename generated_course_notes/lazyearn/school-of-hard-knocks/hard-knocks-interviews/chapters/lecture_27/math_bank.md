## Definitions And Objects

- \(W_t\): investable household wealth or portfolio wealth at time \(t\).

- \(r\): period return for the index-fund compounding box.
- \(K_t\): founder/company cash runway at time \(t\).
- \(P_t\): payroll or near-term required cash outflow.
- \(q_t\): product state or product viability/quality level.
- \(N_t\): customer count after \(t\) sales periods.
- \(e\): equity share granted in lieu of cash compensation.
- \(C\): control rights, especially voting control.
- \(E\): economics / cash-flow ownership share.
- \(t_0\): founding year marker.
- “signal event”: a scarce but high-status adoption event that raises perceived business viability without being revenue itself.
- “social capital”: non-cash status/network/relational leverage; should stay verbal in the chapter, not formalized heavily.
- “meaningful ambition”: target variable defined by mission/product/cause rather than by wealth alone.

## Derivation Steps

1. Whoop near-failure schematic

   2. Encode these as \(K_t \downarrow\), \(P_t\) binding, \(q_t\) uncertain.
   3. The founder’s named missing variable is not strategy but courage.
   4. The LeBron commercial acts as a signal event: low installed base plus elite adoption.
   5. In notes, use this to justify a survival diagram, not a formal solvable model.

2. Whoop signal-event interpretation
   1. Founding year is \(2012\).
   2. By 2015 the product is still struggling.
   3. Only about \(100\) devices exist.
   4. One device appears on LeBron James in a Kia commercial.
   5. The lecture’s mechanism is: scarcity + elite validation \(\Rightarrow\) renewed belief and persistence.

3. Index-fund compounding box

   3. He says not to overcomplicate investing.
   4. The clean editorial recurrence is \(W_{t+1}=(1+r)W_t+s_t\).
   5. Use this only as a standard compounding reconstruction, not as a quoted lecture formula.

4. Sales-first operating rule
   1. Jim Cook’s uncle dismisses setup work that does not produce customers.
   2. The instruction is one customer every day.
   3. Minimal reconstruction: \(N_{t+1}=N_t+1\).
   4. The real point is not linear-growth realism but discipline: daily customer acquisition dominates administrative perfectionism.

5. Shoestring startup allocation
   1. Strip the business to absolute minimum requirements.
   2. Remove fixed-cost items that are not essential: no owned brewery, no office.
   3. Keep only what matters for product and launch: ingredients, top brewmaster, production access.
   4. Substitute contracts and borrowed infrastructure for owned infrastructure.
   5. Substitute equity for cash where expertise is essential and cash is scarce.

6. Product-versus-marketing comparison
   1. Jim Cook says two names tested about equally well.
   2. Therefore naming/branding was not the decisive variable in his retrospective account.
   3. The winning variables were better product and aggressive selling.
   4. Supporting counts: best beer in America after about 6 weeks; no marketing hire for 10 years; 80 salespeople by then.
   5. In the notes, treat this as a causal comparison, not an exact regression claim.

7. Control-versus-economics founder tradeoff
   1. Jim Cook says the firm went public in 1995.
   2. He says he retained the voting shares / voting control.
   3. He explicitly says he sacrificed some economics for control.
   4. Encode this as a qualitative tradeoff: more control may require less economic share.
   5. Keep the representation schematic; do not force corporate-finance formalism the lecture never states.
