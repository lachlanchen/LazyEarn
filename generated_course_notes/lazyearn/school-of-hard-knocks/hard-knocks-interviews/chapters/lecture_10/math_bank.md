## Definitions And Objects
- \(W_{\text{durable}}\): durable wealth or long-run capital position; not mere one-time cash extraction.

- \(X\): execution intensity; responsiveness, effort, operational sharpness, follow-through.
- \(T\): time and endurance; patience long enough for growth, brand, or assets to mature.
- \(R_{\text{ret}}\): retention/holding behavior; refusal to liquidate too early.

## Derivation Steps
1. Early-market/founder entry logic
1. Observe a large value gap inside somebody else’s system.
2. Compare captured value: firm-scale collections versus small personal compensation.
3. Infer that the market is new enough that incumbents do not yet own all the know-how.
4. Move from employee role to founder role.
5. Convert informational flatness into a chance to out-execute larger players.

2. AI allocation logic
1. Partition work into repetitive tasks and critical-thinking tasks.
2. Delegate repetitive tasks to AI.
3. Recover founder/operator time.
4. Reallocate that time to judgment, insight, and decisions requiring a human.
5. Treat AI as productivity infrastructure, not as a substitute for responsibility.

3. Teddy scaling logic
1. Early traction can arrive quickly.
2. Quick traction does not prove durable business quality.
3. Brand and category position are built over multiple years.
4. Trend leverage works only after some base of credibility exists.
5. Large scale therefore appears as delayed compounding, not instant magic.

4. Jody preservation logic
1. Build a focused niche rather than diffuse effort across many directions.
2. Preserve capital by avoiding lifestyle inflation.
3. Place wealth into structures such as trusts.
4. Teach heirs and operators to manage, not just consume.
5. Add reputation and employee care as preservation mechanisms, not just moral extras.

5. Scott crisis-to-scale logic
1. Start from small capital with concrete operating knowledge.
2. Use crisis as a moment of mispricing and forced opportunity.
3. Take a concentrated bet on an asset you already understand operationally.
4. Identify an adjacent structural demand hole in the market.
5. Scale aggressively in execution while staying patient in ownership.

6. Urgency-versus-patience synthesis
1. Urgency governs calls, follow-up, motion, and daily operating tempo.
2. Patience governs brand-building, asset holding, and exit timing.
3. These are not opposites at the same level.
4. Fast execution without patience causes churn.
5. Patience without urgency causes stagnation.
