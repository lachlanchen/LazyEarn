## Derivation Steps
Balance-sheet discipline
1. Start below zero: \(W_0<0\).
2. Growth therefore cannot be narrated as pure self-funding abundance.
3. The speaker immediately links survival to not over-levering.
4. He then places partners and banks ahead of self in payout order.
5. The implied model is residual owner extraction after counterparties are protected.

Sales and negotiation
1. The billionaire reduces sales advice to `listen`.
2. He says customers, friends, and bankers reveal the pathway.
3. The chapter can encode this as information arrival before persuasion.
4. He then adds `deliver equal or greater value`.
5. Negotiation extends the same logic: silence preserves informational advantage.

Shock propagation
1. Hotel business depends on travel and occupancy.
2. 9/11 halts flights.
3. No flights implies no guests.
4. No guests implies \(R_{\text{hotel}} \to 0\).
5. Crisis enters as an exogenous shock, not an operating mistake.

Operational-detail failure
1. Businesses fail not only through lack of capital but through ignored detail.
2. Feedback from customers and employees identifies friction points.
3. If those frictions are ignored, felt quality declines.
4. If felt quality declines, customers defect.
5. Hence local detail compounds into survival probability.

TAM cap
1. Define revenue by \(R=sM\).
2. If \(M=\$10\,\text{million}\) and \(s=1\), then \(R=\$10\,\text{million}\).
3. Full domination of a tiny market still leaves a hard ceiling.
4. A very large \(M\) lets even small \(s\) produce large \(R\).
5. Therefore small TAM can be the bottleneck before capital is.

Reinvestment logic
1. Patel states nine-figure annual revenue.
2. He caps owner distributions near \(\$4\text{--}5\,\text{million}\).
3. Remaining profit is retained for acquisitions, hiring, and expansion.
4. The implied rule is growth-first deployment of cash rather than maximal extraction.
5. That rule links operating scale to future compounding.

Early agency arithmetic
1. First client pays \(P_1=\$5{,}000/\text{month}\).
2. Claimed client outcome is \(\Delta R_{\text{client}}=\$25\,\text{million}\).
3. That performance generates \(N_{\text{referrals}}=3\).
4. Four clients at the same fee imply \(4 \times \$5{,}000 = \$20{,}000/\text{month}\).
5. The lecture treats good work as the referral engine.

Acquisition from zero
1. Start with a boring, profitable seller-run business.
2. Ask retiring owner to carry part of the price as seller financing.
3. Add bank financing supported by SBA structure.
4. Underwrite the debt against business cash flows rather than personal cash on hand.
5. The claim is that zero starting capital need not prevent acquisition if financing is layered.

Place-based pricing
1. Keep the good \(w\) fixed.
2. Move it from \(L_1\) to \(L_2\).
3. Price changes while physical substance does not.
4. Therefore context and placement enter price formation.
5. The lecture uses this as a compact valuation metaphor, not a full pricing model.

## Notation Choices
- Use \(W_0\) for starting net worth or capital position at the opening of a case.
- Use \(R\) for revenue and reserve \(D\) for owner distributions; do not mix business revenue with personal income.
- Use \(V_{\text{eq}}\) and \(V_{\text{EV}}\) for the hospitality valuation pair instead of unlabeled \(V_1,V_2\).
- Use \(M\) for total addressable market and \(s\) for market share, with the canonical relation \(R=sM\).
- Use \(E\) for equity fractions and \(C\) for external capital raised.
- Use text subscripts to keep interview cases separate: \(\text{hotel}\), \(\text{salon}\), \(\text{firm}\), \(\text{agency}\), \(\text{owner}\), \(\text{client}\).
- Use \(\prec\) only for precedence or priority ordering; do not treat it as a numeric inequality.
- Use \(\Rightarrow\) only for lecture-level heuristic implication, not theorem-proof implication.
- Use \(f_{\text{micro}}\) for follower counts rather than generic \(N\), because the transcript ties the interval directly to micro-influencers.
- Use \(L_1,L_2\) for place-based pricing contexts and \(w\) for the unchanged good.
- Do not introduce leverage ratios, discount rates, CAC, LTV, or formal game-theory notation; the transcript does not support them.
- No notation should be attributed to board evidence; every symbol here is editorial notation imposed on transcript content.
