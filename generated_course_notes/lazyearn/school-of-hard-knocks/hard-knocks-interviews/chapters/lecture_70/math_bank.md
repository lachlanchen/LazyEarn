## Definitions And Objects

- \(K_{\text{buffer}}\): liquid capital available to survive “bumps along the road.”
- \(B\): baseline burn / fixed obligations / operating cash need per unit time.
- \(T\): time horizon over which the firm must survive before conditions improve or revenue catches up.
- \(S\): shock reserve for overruns, delays, or unforeseen losses.
- \(W_t\): wealth or business capital position at step \(t\).
- \(s_t\): fresh saving added during period \(t\).
- \(I_t\): deployed investable capital; this is the cleanest formal proxy for the lecture’s “money has to keep moving.”
- \(r_t\): return rate on deployed capital.
- \(L_t\): loss term; use for major setbacks such as wipeouts, failed bets, or destructive personal-financial events.
- \(T_{\text{rec}}\): recovery time after a loss.
- \(E_{ij}\): net exchange value of an interaction between agent \(i\) and agent \(j\).
- \(G_{ij}\): gains received from an interaction; can include information, opportunity, revenue, trust, or exposure.
- \(C_{ij}\): cost of an interaction; can include time, cash, distraction, reputational drag, or bad incentives.
- \(R_t\): reputation stock; an editorial object for “keep your word,” “speak the truth,” and client satisfaction.
- \(K_t\): kept commitments during period \(t\).
- \(B_t\): broken commitments during period \(t\).
- \(\mathcal{S}(b)\): screening tuple for business \(b\), built from the Marcus-Limonis-style “people, process, product, profits” test.
- \(\Pi\): profitability; keep this symbol reserved for actual profit/profitability rather than generic success.
- “Three-dimensional business card”: the delivered work product itself; not a symbol, but conceptually the realized output that markets the firm.
- “Fear of failure”: motivational driver, not an equation term unless explicitly modeled as part of discipline or effort.

## Derivation Steps

Capital buffer from the “under financed” claim

1. Start from the transcript claim that many firms fail because they are underfinanced.
2. Translate “underfinanced” into insufficient liquid capital relative to ordinary burn and unexpected shocks.
3. Represent ordinary survival need by \(B\,T\).
4. Represent unavoidable disruptions by an added reserve term \(S\).
5. Conclude that survival requires \(K_{\text{buffer}} \ge B\,T+S\).

Savings versus movement of money

1. One interview gives the rule “save every penny you can.”
2. Another gives the rule “money got to keep moving.”
3. Do not force these into contradiction at the same level.
4. Let \(s_t\) represent disciplined saving and \(I_t\) represent deployed capital.
5. Use \(W_{t+1}=W_t+s_t+r_t I_t-L_t\) as the editorial reconciliation.
6. Read saving as capital formation and motion as capital deployment.

Recovery-time logic

1. The transcript says the real issue after loss is recovery time.
2. Model the loss by \(L\).
3. Model rebuild capacity by an effective reconstruction rate \(\dot W_{\text{rebuild}}\).
4. Then \(T_{\text{rec}} \approx L/\dot W_{\text{rebuild}}\).
5. Older age or shorter remaining horizon does not necessarily change \(L\), but it changes how punishing \(T_{\text{rec}}\) becomes.

Exchange model from inhale / exhale

1. The speaker moves from breathing to ecosystem barter to human interaction.
2. Preserve that as a structural analogy, not literal physics.
3. Formalize each interaction by \(E_{ij}=G_{ij}-C_{ij}\).
4. If \(E_{ij}>0\), treat the relation as compounding or asset-like.
5. If \(E_{ij}<0\), treat it as draining or liability-like.
6. This is the cleanest formal bridge to the lecture’s “what are we exchanging here?”

Reputation as compounding stock

1. The lecture repeatedly ties longevity to honesty, truth, and keeping one’s word.
2. Treat reputation as a state variable \(R_t\), not a one-off trait.
3. Let kept promises add to \(R_t\); let broken promises subtract from it.
4. Use \(R_{t+1}=R_t+\alpha K_t-\beta B_t\).
5. Interpret client satisfaction as repeated positive updates rather than isolated sentiment.

Business-screen logic

1. The final interview warns against falling in love with an idea.
2. Replace idea-attachment with a screen \(\mathcal{S}(b)=\bigl(People,Process,Product,\Pi\bigr)\).
3. Ask whether the business can be profitable and support employment.
4. If \(\Pi\) is structurally weak, do not let emotional attachment dominate.
5. Apply the same detachment principle to both businesses and trades.

## Notation Choices

- Use \(W_t\) for wealth / capital position in discrete time.
- Use \(K_{\text{buffer}}\) for liquid reserve; do not reuse \(K\) for reputation or capital gains.
- Use \(B\) for burn / baseline obligations.
- Use \(S\) for shock reserve.
- Use \(I_t\) for invested or deployed capital; this matches the lecture’s “moving money” idea better than using \(M_t\).
- Use \(L_t\) for losses and \(T_{\text{rec}}\) for recovery time.
- Use \(R_t\) only for reputation stock.
- Use \(E_{ij}\) for exchange value between people or entities.
- Use \(\Pi\) for profitability, not for total revenue.
- Use \(R_{\max}\) for the claimed maximum annual revenue figure and \(V_{\text{trade}}\) for daily trading volume.
- Use \(\approx\) for quoted or approximate interview magnitudes.
- Use “claim” language in prose whenever a number comes from an interviewee and is not independently verified.
- Keep all formalism in discrete-time notation unless a continuously compounding argument is explicitly needed later.
