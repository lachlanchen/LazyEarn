## Definitions And Objects

- \(I_{\mathrm{gross}}\): gross annual earnings or gross business take, when the speaker explicitly says “gross.”
- \(I_{\mathrm{net}}\): net annual earnings after costs, only when the speaker explicitly contrasts net with gross.
- \(R\): annual revenue.
- \(P\): annual profit.
- \(m\): profit margin, \(m=P/R\).
- \(Y_{\mathrm{labor}}\): income earned directly from one’s work or trade.
- \(Y_{\mathrm{business}}\): income generated by owned business operations.
- \(Y_{\mathrm{capital}}\): income generated by owned capital or assets.
- \(K\): capital stock or asset base that can produce future income.
- \(r\): effective return on deployed capital.
- \(s\): saving or reinvestment rate out of current income.
- \(q\): annual business scale or throughput; used only as an editorial placeholder for the solo-to-team threshold.
- “Hard assets”: transcript-backed phrase for shifting from e-commerce into real estate or owned asset positions.
- “Valuable skill”: a marketable capability that the economy rewards; the lecture example is website building.
- “Solo regime”: founder-centered operation where one person can still carry the business.
- “Team regime”: organization-centered operation where delegation and specialization become necessary.

## Derivation Steps

1. Physician med-spa implied net
   1. Take the reported figure \(I_{\mathrm{gross}}=15.5\,\mathrm{MUSD}\).
   2. Read the statement “Net would have been half of that.”
   3. Compute \(I_{\mathrm{net}} \approx 0.5 \times 15.5 = 7.75\,\mathrm{MUSD}\).
   4. Mark the result as inferred, not separately reported.

2. E-commerce founder implied margin
   1. Take the reported profit \(P \approx 8.7\,\mathrm{MUSD}\).
   2. Take the reported revenue range \(R \approx 17\text{--}18\,\mathrm{MUSD}\).
   3. Compute the low-end margin \(8.7/18 \approx 0.483\).
   4. Compute the high-end margin \(8.7/17 \approx 0.512\).
   5. Record \(m \approx 48\%\text{--}51\%\).

3. Barber’s “money makes money” rule
   1. Start with active earnings \(Y_t\).
   2. Retain or reinvest a fraction \(sY_t\) instead of consuming all of it.
   3. Accumulate capital \(K_{t+1}=K_t+sY_t\).
   4. Let that capital generate future income \(Y_{\mathrm{capital}}=rK\).
   5. Interpret the lecture’s advice as a shift from labor-only income to capital-backed income.

4. Solo-to-team scaling threshold
   1. Record the explicit verbal claim: zero to one million can be done alone.
   2. Record the second claim: one million to ten million needs a team.
   3. Infer a change of regime in operating structure, not just effort level.
   4. Translate the mechanism as founder-duplication through specialized hires.
   5. Reserve the stronger formalism for prose, since the lecture states this qualitatively.

5. Skill-to-wealth loop
   1. Learn a skill.
   2. Do not impose arbitrary short deadlines.
   3. Fail repeatedly.
   4. Use failure to eliminate ineffective actions.
   5. Apply the mastered skill in a market the economy values.
   6. Convert skill income into business scale or capital accumulation.

6. Capital-discipline rule from the tech founder
   1. Raise money or receive capital.
   2. Do not spend it immediately.
   3. Hold cash and measure outcomes.
   4. Spend only in ways that create value.
   5. Treat cash preservation as part of business survival.

- Use USD throughout; write millions as \(\mathrm{MUSD}\) in the working bank, but allow prose in the final notes to say “million dollars.”
- Use \(\approx\) for all self-reported or rough figures.
- Use interval notation or a dash for speaker-given ranges, e.g. \(17\text{--}18\,\mathrm{MUSD}\).
- Use subscripts \(\mathrm{gross}\) and \(\mathrm{net}\) rather than introducing separate letters for those categories.
- Use \(R\) for revenue and \(P\) for profit; do not overload \(P\) as “price.”
- Use \(m=P/R\) for margin whenever the chapter needs a compact profitability measure.
- Use \(Y_{\mathrm{labor}}, Y_{\mathrm{business}}, Y_{\mathrm{capital}}\) rather than trying to force speaker language into more technical finance notation.
- Use \(K\) for owned capital/assets only in editorial reconstruction; make clear this is not speaker notation.
- Use \(r\) and \(s\) only for compact modeling of return and saving/reinvestment; these are editorial devices, not lecture symbols.
- Keep “solo regime” and “team regime” as prose labels even if \(q\) is used internally for scale.
- Do not introduce operator notation, optimization symbols, or stochastic notation; the lecture does not support that level of formalization.
