## Derivation Steps
Validation heuristic:
1. A failed reinvestment shows that founder belief is not enough.
2. The lecture replaces internal conviction with external evidence.
3. That evidence is named as sales.
4. The exposure process is operationalized as at least $1000$ conversations.
5. Over roughly six months, that becomes a manageable cadence rather than an impossible total.

Conversation-rate unpacking:
1. Take $C_{\min}=1000$.
2. Take $T \approx 6$ months.
3. Compute $1000/6 \approx 167$ conversations per month.
4. Re-express that as about $38$ per week or about $5.6$ per day.
5. Use these rates only as editorial clarification, not as quoted lecture arithmetic.

Sunk-cost accounting:
1. The speaker estimates the earlier stop would have cost about $\$1{,}000{,}000$.
2. The revival costs about another $\$1{,}000{,}000$.
3. Add the two losses to reconstruct about $\$2{,}000{,}000$ total.
4. Use this as a numerical illustration of “throwing good money after bad.”

Local marketing model:
1. Treat the restaurant as geographically constrained.
2. Represent its reachable audience as a local catchment around $x_0$.
3. Concentrate outreach inside that region through flyers, apartment complexes, and direct contact.
4. Judge the marketing plan by whether sales rise inside that zone.
5. If sales do not respond, the lecture says to discard the plan and start over.

Founder bottleneck model:
1. Centralize decisions in the founder.
2. Team members become quieter and more dependent.
3. Problem-solving capacity collapses back onto one person.
4. Organizational throughput is therefore bounded by the founder.
5. Scale requires redistributing ownership of solutions.

Marketing-doctrine chain:
1. Start from the product or problem being solved.
2. Define that clearly enough to produce a purpose statement.
3. Use the purpose statement to clarify the story.
4. Use the story to identify the right audience.
5. Align the organization around that purpose before optimizing website or content output.
