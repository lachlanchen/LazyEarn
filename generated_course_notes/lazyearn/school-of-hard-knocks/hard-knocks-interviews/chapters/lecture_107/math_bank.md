## Definitions And Objects
- \(T_{\mathrm{tox}}\): toxic trauma; White’s examples are addiction, alcohol, drugs, food sensitivity, poisons.
- \(T_{\mathrm{phys}}\): physical trauma; examples include football injury, bicycle fall, head impact, traumatic brain injury, war injury.
- \(T_{\mathrm{emo}}\): emotional trauma; examples include loss of a loved one and betrayal by a loved one.
- \(B\): the brain as described by White, modeled schematically as having two systems.
- \(E\): the brain’s “electrical system”; White later associates this with EEG and frequencies.
- \(C\): the brain’s “chemical system”; White names dopamine, serotonin, and acetylcholine in this register.
- \(R_{\mathrm{ff}}\): fight-or-flight response; the brain’s protective reaction under traumatic overload.
- \(D_{\mathrm{post}}\): post-traumatic dysregulation or imbalance after the immediate trauma has passed.
- “Life on life’s terms”: White’s phrase for ordinary functional life; use as prose, not formal notation.
- “Natural sales moment”: the state produced when visible passion and communication generate curiosity rather than a hard close.
- “Structure”: the closing self-improvement principle; fixed times, repeated acts, and bounded commitments.
- Achilles heel: the personally dominant destabilizer that must be identified honestly before change can be structured.

## Derivation Steps
1. Opportunity-recognition chain
   1. Notice that hotel guests leave their cars idle while dining.
   2. Notice that new detailing inputs create a high-visibility improvement.
   3. Offer the service during the idle window rather than asking for extra customer time.
   4. Fix the offer at a low demonstration price.
   5. Use the visible result as proof of value.
   6. Expand from in-place hotel service to pickup-and-detail routine work.

2. Sales-as-communication chain
   1. Start by asking why the audience might say no.
   2. Ask questions before delivering the pitch.
   3. Listen for needs, knowledge level, and resistance points.
   4. Tailor the message to what the listener actually needs.
   5. Avoid telling people what they already know better than you.
   6. Let respect and humility increase receptivity.

3. Trauma-mechanism chain
   1. Define trauma broadly, not only as catastrophic physical injury.
   2. Sort trauma into toxic, physical, and emotional forms.
   3. Treat the brain as a coupled electrical-and-chemical regulatory system.
   4. Under overload, the brain triggers fight-or-flight protection.
   5. That protection may save the person in the moment.
   6. After the event, the system may remain dysregulated.
   7. This lingering dysregulation is where White places PTSD-like outcomes and other imbalances.

4. Treatment-order chain
   1. Do not assume the first move is medication.
   2. Recognize that medication-first treatment may be operating by guess.
   3. Begin with non-invasive technologies and supportive foundations.
   4. Add nutrition, talk/support structures, and spiritual/community support in White’s ordering.
   5. Expect gradual rather than instantaneous restoration.

5. Small-wins self-improvement chain
   1. Pick one simple act with a definite time or condition.
   2. Complete it exactly as planned.
   3. Reflect on the completion at the end of the day.
   4. Convert completion into a sense of efficacy.
   5. Repeat before scaling the program.
   6. Use this to avoid the shame spiral caused by all-at-once resolutions.
