## Derivation Steps
Commission-income dependence
1. Start from the lecturer’s pay structure: stockbroking pays commissions, not fixed asset income.
2. Model income as depending on executed trades.
3. Set \(N_{\text{trades}}=0\) for a week with no activity or a vacation week.
4. Conclude \(I_{\text{comm}}=0\).
5. Use this to motivate the move toward real estate ownership and recurring cash flow.

First-property transformation
1. Acquire the first property at \(P_0=\$575{,}000\).
2. Push personal liquidity down to \(B_{\text{post-close}}=\$50\).
3. Improve the asset operationally: clean up, repaint, re-tenant, write new leases.
4. Reach a later refinance-related level \(V_1 \approx \$800{,}000\).
5. Borrow \(L_1=\$600{,}000\) against that improved position.
6. Recover the original money and closing costs according to the narration.
7. Arrive at recurring \(CF_{\text{mo}}=\$5{,}000/\text{month}\) by January.
8. Treat the total narrated transformation as occurring over roughly \(7\) months.

Entry-barrier inversion
1. Start from the common claim that large personal capital is required to buy property.
2. Introduce the mall example with \(V_{\text{mall}}\approx \$3\text{B}\) and a hypothetical \(P_{\text{hyp}}=\$1\text{B}\).
3. Use the gap between ordinary price and extraordinary deal quality to argue that financing becomes easier when the opportunity is exceptional.
4. Recast the scarce object as \(D_{\text{good}}\), not \(K_{\text{own}}\).
5. Conclude: the hard part is finding the deal; the money is downstream.

Retirement-property heuristic
1. Start with one correctly chosen property.
2. Hold it through a payoff horizon of roughly \(20\) to \(25\) years.
3. End with an owned asset rather than only exhausted labor income.
4. Use that owned asset as the lecturer’s minimal retirement-security mechanism.
