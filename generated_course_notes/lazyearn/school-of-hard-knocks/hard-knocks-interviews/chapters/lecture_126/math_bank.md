## Definitions And Objects
- \(R\): relationship capital; accumulated person-to-person trust and access.
- \(S\): customer service quality; how well the customer is treated.
- \(T\): remembered trust; the residue left by good service that can travel beyond the immediate transaction.
- \(B\): repeat business or return-customer tendency.
- \(W\): word-of-mouth effect; referrals, recommendations, and reputation spread.
- \(O\): downstream opportunities; in this lecture, specifically fashion-show judging invitations.
- \(A\): authenticity/personalization of service, contrasted with scripted politeness.
- “Scripted greeting”: a low-\(A\) service act that signals routine rather than sincere attention.
- “Make it personal”: operational rule pushing \(A\) upward, thereby strengthening \(B\) and \(W\).

## Derivation Steps
1. Relationship-to-opportunity chain
   1. Start from the first principle: relationships matter most.
   2. Compress it into the heuristic “who you know.”
   3. Support it with the anecdote: judging three fashion shows this year.
   4. Infer the mechanism: prior service created a relationship.
   5. Conclude the compact chain \(R \to O\), or more cautiously \(S \to T \to R \to O\).

2. Service-to-return-business chain
   1. Mark the hinge with “Secondly.”
   2. State the explicit claim: customer service makes people come back.
   3. Translate that into \(S \to B\).
   4. Add the next spoken claim: word of mouth is the best advertising.
   5. Extend the chain to \(S \to B \to W\).

3. Authenticity refinement
   1. Introduce the Walmart/Dollar Tree example as a counterexample.
   2. Note that repeated greetings alone do not count as effective service.
   3. Distinguish scripted service from personal service.
   4. Represent that distinction by the variable \(A\).
   5. Use \(A \uparrow \Rightarrow W \uparrow\) only as editorial shorthand for “make it personal.”
