## Derivation Steps
1. Wealth-preservation logic
   1. Start from reported income \(Y_t\).
   2. Split it into spending and retained money: \(S_t = Y_t - C_t\).
   3. The lecture’s repeated “save bread” instruction requires \(S_t > 0\).
   4. Retained savings plus returns update the wealth stock: \(W_{t+1} = W_t + S_t + R_t\).
   5. “Don’t spend it” is the practical condition keeping \(C_t\) from swallowing \(Y_t\).

2. Delayed-gratification logic
   1. The lecture rejects the idea that wealth appears “overnight.”
   2. Replace the short fantasy horizon \(T_{\mathrm{overnight}}\) with a long build horizon \(T_{\mathrm{career}}\).
   3. During the learning phase, one studies under people who have already done the work.
   4. Early compensation is not expected to match the mentor’s result.
   5. Only after learning and execution does scale or large income become plausible.

3. Restaurant scaling path
   1. Initial state is a very small operation inside a gas station.
   2. The transcript suggests a starting staff of owner plus one additional worker.
   3. Continued effort expands the business from one site to \(N_{\mathrm{loc}} = 5\).
   4. Staffing grows to \(N_{\mathrm{emp}} \in [85,100]\).
   5. Reported annual income reaches roughly \(Y_{\max}^{\mathrm{restaurant}} \approx \$10\,\text{million}\).

4. Poverty-to-risk asymmetry
   1. The speaker describes being so broke that the downside already felt exhausted.
   2. In that state, the perceived loss from trying falls sharply.
   3. The lecture then turns this into forward motion: “whatever you do, you just go forward.”
   4. This is why rock bottom is narrated not only as suffering, but as a shift in decision posture.

5. Career-durability logic
   1. High income alone is not treated as sufficient.
   2. The lecture adds retention, sustainability, and preparation.
   3. Physical readiness, mental health, and self-awareness stabilize the long run.
   4. The result is a career horizon \(T_{\mathrm{NFL}} \approx 14\,\text{years}\), rather than a short burst.
