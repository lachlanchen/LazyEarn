## Definitions And Objects
- \(E_{\mathrm{lived}}\): lived experience; direct passage through real sales encounters.
- \(E_{\mathrm{textbook}}\): secondhand instruction; what can be taught verbally or read.
- \(T\): teachable surface inputs, especially tricks, lines, and appearance.
- \(N\): repeated negative encounters; the “no after no after no” variable.
- \(A\): attitude stability under rejection.
- \(M\): ability to deliver the message the right way under pressure.
- \(V\): audience variation; differences in ethnicity, background, age, and experience.
- \(S\): practical sales skill, understood as embodied communicative competence.
- \(f\): editorial causal map from exposure conditions to skill; not lecture-defined notation.

## Derivation Steps
Mechanism A: why textbook instruction is insufficient

1. Some components of selling are teachable: tricks, phrases, and appearance.
2. The lecture explicitly grants this before denying sufficiency.
3. Real selling introduces adverse live feedback: rejection, insults, hostility.
4. Under that pressure, one must preserve \(A\) and still maintain \(M\).
5. Therefore \(T\) alone does not produce \(S\); lived exposure is required.

Mechanism B: how rejection becomes training

1. The lecture stacks rejection examples rather than treating “difficulty” abstractly.
2. Each repetition increases \(N\).
3. Survival of each encounter requires stable \(A\) and functioning \(M\).
4. Repeated operation under those constraints strengthens \(S\).
5. Hence adversity is presented as training input, not merely pain.

Mechanism C: why audience diversity forces improvement

1. Sales requires communication with many kinds of people, summarized here by \(V\).
2. Different audiences make a single canned delivery inadequate.
3. Higher \(V\) forces adaptation in wording, tone, and framing.
4. Adaptation under live conditions feeds back into \(S\).
5. This is why the lecture ends with inevitability: under enough \(N\) and \(V\), improvement is hard to avoid.

## Notation Choices

- Use \(T\) for teachable surface material; keep it as a set, not as a scalar.
- Use \(E_{\mathrm{lived}}\) and \(E_{\mathrm{textbook}}\) to preserve the lecture’s core asymmetry.
- Use \(N, A, M, V\) only as editorial shorthand in working notes; they are not speaker-given symbols.
- Prefer \(f(N, A, M, V)\) over a more formal operator notation; the lecture gives a mechanism, not a measurable law.
- Keep English words in math mode upright with \(\text{...}\) when needed.
