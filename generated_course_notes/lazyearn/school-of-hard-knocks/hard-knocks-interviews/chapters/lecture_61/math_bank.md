## Notation Choices
- Use \(N\) for population counts such as millionaires and billionaires in Austin.
- Use \(Y\) for personal or loosely stated one-year money figures when the transcript is ambiguous about income versus business revenue.
- Use \(R\) for annual business revenue where the lecture is clearly speaking at company scale.
- Use \(r\) for rates such as bank interest or investment return.
- Use \(V\) for asset or portfolio value across time in the dot-com story.
- Use \(W_t\) for wealth stock in the save-versus-invest reconstruction.
- Use \(s_t\) for savings flow and \(r_t\) for investment return in the final speaker's class distinction.
- Use \(a\) for age markers and \(T\) for rebuild duration.
- Use \(p\) for market-implied probabilities in the election-betting section.
- Use \(p_{s,c}\) specifically for state-by-candidate election odds.
- Use \(\Pr(\cdot)\) when reconstructing the visible market cards directly from the screenshot.
- Use implication arrows \(\Rightarrow\) for business doctrines and mechanism summaries, not equality signs.
- Keep dollar units explicit in displayed equations.
- Treat binary outcome sets with braces when summarizing speaker-side claims, e.g.
  \[
  \{\text{hugely successful},\ \text{out of business}\}.
  \]
- Keep all election-market notation probabilistic rather than pretending the lecture gives polling equations or statistical estimators.
