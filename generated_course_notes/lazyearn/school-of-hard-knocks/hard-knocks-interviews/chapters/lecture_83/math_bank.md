## Definitions And Objects
- \(M_0\): reported starting cash at the beginning of a speaker’s trajectory.
- \(I_{\text{annual}}\): reported annual personal income.
- \(G\): gross revenue / top-line business output.
- \(N\): net result after costs; use this for the lecture’s “what I truly cared about” distinction.
- \(K\): aggregate costs, expenses, or dilution between gross and net.
- \(R_y\): reported business revenue in year \(y\).
- \(C_t\): cash flow available in period \(t\).
- \(A_t\): stock of income-producing assets in period \(t\).
- \(C^{\text{practice}}_t\): cash flow from the core operating practice or main business.
- \(C^{\text{asset}}_t\): cash flow from acquired assets such as shopping centers or medical buildings.
- \(\Delta A(C_t)\): incremental asset purchases financed from current cash flow.
- \(T_{\text{market}}\): time-to-market / speed-to-market variable; use only as a schematic timing label.
- Producer: agent who creates, sells, or controls value creation according to the lecture’s classification.
- Consumer: agent who mainly purchases or consumes value.
- Seller: operational role aligned with value capture in the lecture’s first abstraction.
- Buyer: operational role contrasted with the seller in the opening commercial classification.
- \(D\): a business decision under evaluation.
- \(E_{\text{cust}}(D)\): effect of decision \(D\) on the customer / consumer.
- \(E_{\text{team}}(D)\): effect of decision \(D\) on the worker / agent / contractor / consultant side.
- \(E_{\text{firm}}(D)\): effect of decision \(D\) on the company itself.
- \(P_t\): prospecting effort in period \(t\).
- \(L_t\): relationship / lead / conversation stock built through prospecting.
- \(S_t\): realized sales output.
- \(B_t\): self-belief / conviction variable; use only for heuristic summaries.
- \(W_t\): work intensity / disciplined effort variable; use only for heuristic summaries.

## Derivation Steps
Producer / Seller Logic
1. The lecture classifies people into consumers and producers.
2. It then maps the commercial role question into buyer versus seller.
3. Consumers consume; producers produce.
4. Sellers sit on the producing / value-capturing side of the exchange.
5. Therefore the lecture claims the money-making edge lies with producers and sellers rather than consumers and buyers.

Gross-to-Net Discipline
1. A speaker reports \(G=\$5.5\,\mathrm{M}\).
2. The same speaker reports \(N=\$2.4\,\mathrm{M}\).
3. He explicitly says the net figure is what mattered.
4. Therefore chapter-level evaluation should rank retained result above top-line volume.

Speed-to-Market Logic
1. The lecture says an idea today can be matched tomorrow.
2. That makes the opportunity window short.
3. A shorter window raises the value of rapid execution.
4. Great people and capital are then introduced as the supports that make speed possible.

Reinvestment Loop
1. The core practice grows and throws off cash flow.
2. That cash flow is deployed into shopping centers and medical buildings.
3. Those assets then generate additional cash flow.
4. The next cycle begins from a larger cash-flow base.
5. Wealth growth is therefore described as recursive reinvestment, not one-off acquisition.

Accountability / Staying Broke Logic
1. The lecture states that bad events may not be your fault.
2. It insists they still become your problem.
3. Problems not solved persist as conditions of continued brokenness.
4. Money management and time management are then named as concrete failure points.
5. Prospecting is added as a necessary operational act without which talent cannot monetize.

Three-Legged-Stool Sustainability Test
1. Start with a candidate business decision \(D\).
2. Ask whether \(D\) works for the customer.
3. Ask whether \(D\) works for the worker / agent / contractor side.
4. Ask whether \(D\) preserves the company.
5. If one leg fails, the model is described as unstable or unsustainable.

Sales Loop
1. Lack of belief weakens effort.
2. Weak effort reduces prospecting.
3. Low prospecting reduces relationships and sales opportunities.
4. More work increases outbound activity and exposure.
5. Sales skill only becomes economically relevant after prospecting creates opportunities to use it.

Freedom-versus-Richness Horizon
1. Early business years may underpay the owner.
2. The lecture advises feeding the business rather than extracting from it.
3. That delays immediate visible wealth.
4. The intended output is freedom and later wealth, not instant richness.
5. Patience is therefore treated as a functional input to scale.
