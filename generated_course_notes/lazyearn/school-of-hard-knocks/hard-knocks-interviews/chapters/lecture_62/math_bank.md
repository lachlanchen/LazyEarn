   1. Start from the spoken valuation path \(V_0=\$5\,\text{million}\), \(V_1=\$2.1\,\text{billion}\).

2. VC portfolio asymmetry:
   1. Take the speaker’s portfolio size \(N_{\text{portfolio}}=30\).
   2. Assign \(N_0=25\) total failures.

   4. Compute shares \(25/30=5/6\) and \(5/30=1/6\).

## Notation Choices
- Use \(\approx\) for nearly all money figures unless the lecture presents a count or age as exact.
- Use \(Y\) for annual money made by a person and \(R\) for business revenue when the transcript explicitly distinguishes the two.
- Use \(V\) for company valuation, not for realized proceeds.
- Use \(M\) for realized multiplicative outcomes such as \(33\times\).
- Use \(N\) for counts, with subscripts indicating the object counted.
- Use \(p\) for probabilities or implied frequency statements; keep all sponsor odds and failure rates in this family.
- Use \(a\) for ages and \(T\) for time horizons.
- Use \(x_i\) for portfolio outcomes only in the abstract venture-compression formula.
- Use \(\Pr(\cdot)\) rather than plain text probability when introducing \(p_s\).
- Keep the Arizona notation minimal: \(s=\text{Arizona}\). Do not invent state-specific odds not present in the frame.
- Keep inequality arrows and directional heuristics for qualitative rules:
  \[
  \uparrow,\ \downarrow,\ \Rightarrow
  \]
  rather than turning spoken advice into stronger equalities.
