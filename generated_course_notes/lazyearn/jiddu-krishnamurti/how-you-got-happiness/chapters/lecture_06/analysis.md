# Chapter Plan

## Lecture Arc

The lecture begins by resuming an unfinished distinction: “responsible for my action” versus “being responsible.” Krishnamurti immediately turns the first phrase into a warning about direction, will, formula, and preconceived action, while the second becomes a total ground from which action takes place.

The discussion then pivots from responsibility as a general state to responsibility in education, politics, behavior, and daily life. Anderson restates the problem as one of crisis and immediacy: if action is not something outside me, then the phrase “my action” already risks abstraction. Krishnamurti accepts this and sharpens it into action as “doing now,” free from the past.

A literary and scriptural detour follows: Anderson invokes the I Ching and later other traditions, but Krishnamurti redirects the inquiry away from authority. Even if there were no books, leaders, or teachers, the problem would remain: can the mind be clear, not rooted in the past, and therefore responsible now?

From there the lecture returns to relationship as the foundation of life. The question becomes: how does total responsibility express itself in relationship? This leads through the problem of choice, decision, and freedom, where Krishnamurti insists that a clear mind does not choose; it acts.

The middle movement examines image-making. Relationship fails when one relates through images of oneself or the other; then only images are in contact. Anderson’s Keats reference helps open the connection between the unbetrayed present, beauty, goodness, and relationship, but Krishnamurti keeps pulling the inquiry back to the fact of human non-relationship.

The next pivot is from image to abstraction. “If” becomes the grammatical symptom of postponement: instead of meeting violence as fact, the mind invents the ideal of nonviolence, which produces conflict and delays action. This becomes one of the strongest conceptual spines of the chapter: abstraction is escape from fact.

The latter part turns responsibility toward education, care, dependence, gurus, permissiveness, and the training of children. Krishnamurti distinguishes care from prohibition and responsibility from dependence, then uses education as the concrete test case: teacher and student must learn together, not merely transmit information.

The final movement raises the social question: what is the responsibility of a serious person among the irresponsible? Krishnamurti rejects planned attack or propaganda; care must speak without violence, perhaps reaching where resistance is less defended. The lecture closes by gathering freedom, responsibility, care, no-image relationship, fear, pleasure, and order in freedom as the next threshold.

## Section Outline

1. **Responsible For, Or Being Responsible**  
Open with the resumed question from the previous conversation: responsibility “for” implies direction, will, and a particular object, whereas being responsible is total and undirected. The section should preserve the rhythm of Anderson’s setup and Krishnamurti’s immediate distinction.

2. **Action Now, Not Action From Formula**  
Follow the movement from education and children to the claim that action based on an inherited formula is not responsible action. Include a standalone `Question & Answer` subsection around the obstacle: “How can action be responsible if it is based on a preconceived formula?”

3. **Choice, Decision, And The Confused Mind**  
Develop the exchange where Anderson proposes “clean decision” and Krishnamurti questions whether decision exists at all. The `Question & Answer` subsection should preserve the local puzzle: “Is freedom the capacity to choose, or does choice show confusion?”

4. **Relationship Without Image**  
Track the return to relationship as the foundation of life: neighbor, nature, wife, husband, and the image between people. This section should keep the distinction between actual relationship and the relationship of images, without turning it into psychological theory beyond the transcript.

5. **The Fact And The Escape Into Abstraction**  
Use the violence/nonviolence passage as the chapter’s central analytic hinge: the fact is violence; the ideal of nonviolence is the abstraction of the fact and therefore postpones action. Add a standalone `Question & Answer` subsection: “Why does the mind create an ideal instead of meeting the fact?”

6. **Responsibility As Care, Not Prohibition**  
Move from education, nature, children, and gurus into the distinction between care and rule-bound prohibition. Keep the examples concrete: dependence on teachers or gurus, the child trained into conformity, and permissiveness mistaken for freedom.

7. **Teacher And Student Learning Together**  
Preserve the late educational inquiry: a teacher is not merely an informer, and mathematics is invoked as order, not as technical content to be solved. This section should connect learning, companionship, attention, and order without manufacturing a formal pedagogy.

8. **The Responsible Among The Irresponsible**  
Close with the social problem: how does one meet an irresponsible consciousness without attack, propaganda, or designed intervention? End by pointing forward to fear, pleasure, love, and “order in freedom,” since the lecture itself explicitly hands those questions to the next conversation.
