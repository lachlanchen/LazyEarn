## Definitions And Objects

- `responsible for X`: Responsibility narrowed to an object, direction, or prescribed field; associated with directed will and preconceived action.

- `being responsible`: Total, undirected responsibility; the ground in which action takes place.

- `formula`: Any inherited, ideational, social, religious, political, or traditional pattern that prescribes action before direct seeing.

- `action now`: Doing in the active present; not action derived from formula, authority, past, or repetition.

- `choice`: The movement between alternatives when the mind does not see clearly; not treated as freedom.

- `clarity`: Seeing directly enough that action occurs without choice or decision.

- `relationship`: Actual contact without image; tested in wife/husband, children, neighbor, nature, teacher/student, and society.

- `image`: The known representation one has built of oneself or another; the source of division in relationship.

- `fact` / `what is`: What is actually present or being done, not the record, idea, or abstraction of it.

- `abstraction`: The movement away from fact into idea, ideal, postponement, or non-fact.

- `non-fact`: The idealized opposite of fact, especially “not being violent” as an abstraction from the fact of violence.

- `negation`: Not prohibition or willful denial; a direct seeing that rejects the false because it understands the implications.

- `care`: Affection, consideration, attention, diligence, and non-negligence; flows with responsibility.

- `permissiveness`: Doing what one wants; explicitly not freedom.

- `order`: Introduced through mathematics as the highest form of order, but applied to living and teaching rather than formal calculation.

## Derivation Steps

1. Responsible action versus formula
   1. Start from the phrase “I am responsible for my action.”
   2. Ask what the action is based on.
   3. If it is based on a handed-down formula, it follows the past.
   4. Action based on the past is repetition.
   5. Repetition is not responsible action.
   6. Therefore responsible action must be doing now.

2. Choice versus freedom
   1. Decision implies choosing between alternatives.
   2. Choosing between alternatives implies the mind does not see clearly.
   3. Lack of clear seeing is confusion.
   4. Confusion produces conflict.
   5. A clear mind has no choice.
   6. Therefore freedom is not the capacity to choose; freedom appears as clear action.

3. Relationship through image
   1. Person A has an image of Person B.
   2. Person B has an image of Person A.
   3. The two images interact.
   4. The persons are not actually in relationship.
   5. Therefore responsibility in relationship means no image.

4. Abstraction from violence
   1. The fact is violence.
   2. The mind cannot, will not, or does not want to deal with the fact.
   3. It invents the ideal of nonviolence.
   4. The ideal is an abstraction from the fact.
   5. The abstraction is a non-fact.
   6. Trying to live the non-fact postpones action.
   7. Postponement produces conflict, misery, and confusion.

5. Care versus prohibition
   1. Responsibility includes care, attention, affection, consideration, and diligence.
   2. Care is not “I must do this” or “I must not do that.”
   3. Prohibition remains a rule or formula.
   4. Care acts from responsibility in the present.
   5. Therefore responsibility is not moral rule-following.

6. Education and shared learning
   1. If the teacher merely informs, the teacher stands above the student.
   2. That creates division.
   3. If teacher and student are both learning, the division lessens.
   4. Shared learning becomes companionship and a journey together.
   5. Mathematics becomes an example of order, not just subject matter.
   6. Teaching should awaken intelligence and attention, not merely transmit memory.

7. Present and past
   1. To understand the present, one must understand the past.
   2. The past is not merely history; it is the self as known.
   3. If the past is not understood, the present is only modified continuation.
   4. Modified continuation is not the now.
   5. Therefore understanding oneself as the known is necessary to understand the present.

## Notation Choices

- Use `\text{...}` expressions rather than algebraic variables for conceptual diagrams; this avoids pretending the lecture supplied formal mathematics.

- Use `\longrightarrow` for causal or developmental movement in transcript-derived flowcharts.

- Use `\not\equiv` for explicit conceptual non-equivalence, e.g. permissiveness is not freedom.

- Use `X` only in the phrase `responsible for X`, where `X` means any particular object, field, or action.

- Use `A` and `B` only if a compact relationship diagram needs two persons. Prefer labels like `Person A`, `Image of B`, `Image of A`, `Person B`.

- Keep `fact`, `what is`, `image`, `abstraction`, `ideal`, `non-fact`, `choice`, `freedom`, `care`, and `attention` as plain-text conceptual objects.

- Do not introduce physics variables, operators, Hilbert spaces, functions, or formal logical quantifiers.
