## Definitions And Objects

- Psychological security: the felt need for permanency, stability, continuity, or something imperishable to hold onto.

- Physical security: bodily and material safety; not the main object of the lecture’s critique.

- Belief: a projected support that gives comfort or continuity, such as God, heaven, communism, reincarnation, professional identity, or future improvement.

- Image: the picture or conclusion through which one sees another person, oneself, work, status, or relationship.

- Conclusion: the psychological surplus added to actual facts, such as “I am a doctor” as identity rather than functional fact.

- Actual fact: what is directly so, for example training, knowledge, and daily operation in the doctor example.

- Idea: abstract assent or rationalized understanding that does not alter action.

- Perception: direct seeing of what is so; in the transcript, action follows from this rather than from ideation.

- Occupation: more than doing tasks; the psychological absorption that keeps the brain in an apparently ordered state.

- Mechanical order: repetitive, conditioned order that gives temporary safety but becomes dull, unstable, or disturbed.

- Total order: the order the brain needs, but which the lecture distinguishes from mechanical repetition.

- Past: memory, image, reputation, word, picture, symbol, and accumulated conclusion.

- Time movement: the continuation of the past through the present into psychological becoming.

## Derivation Steps

1. Security As Becoming
   1. A person experiences discomfort, danger, loneliness, or uncertainty.
   2. Thought projects a future in which things will be better.
   3. That projection gives comfort now.
   4. Therefore psychological security is bound to becoming in time.
   5. The lecture then asks whether such psychological tomorrow is real.

2. Idea Versus Fact
   1. One hears “there is no psychological security.”
   2. The statement is accepted as an idea.
   3. The old movement of seeking security continues.
   4. Therefore abstract agreement has not acted.
   5. Krishnamurti redirects the inquiry to actual perception.

3. Image As Security
   1. One depends on a belief, role, house, profession, wife, God, or ideology.
   2. Thought forms an image or conclusion around it.
   3. The image becomes the center of felt security.
   4. Other people are seen through their function in that structure.
   5. The question becomes why the image feels more real than actuality.

4. Doctor Example
   1. A doctor has training, knowledge, experience, and daily operation.
   2. These are actual facts.
   3. Thought adds status, comparison, continuity, and protection from loneliness.
   4. That addition becomes the psychological identity “I am a doctor.”
   5. The identity must be maintained through occupation.

5. Occupation To Mechanical Order
   1. The mind or brain without occupation becomes unstable or “wild.”
   2. Occupation produces a relatively smooth, ordered state.
   3. This order is mechanical because it depends on repetition and content.
   4. When the occupation stops, instability returns.
   5. Therefore occupation is not freedom from disorder but a temporary mechanism.

6. Mechanical Order To Disorder
   1. Mechanical order appears safe for the time being.
   2. It becomes repetitive, dull, or unsatisfying.
   3. If disturbed, it produces fear.
   4. The person seeks a new occupation or habit.
   5. Thus mechanical order is disorder continuing under the appearance of order.

7. Past Meeting Present
   1. One meets another person through memory, image, reputation, word, and symbol.
   2. This means the past is meeting the present.
   3. If the past continues, relationship is governed by time, bondage, and fear.
   4. If this movement is completely observed, the movement may stop.
   5. Then relationship is described as meeting freshly.
