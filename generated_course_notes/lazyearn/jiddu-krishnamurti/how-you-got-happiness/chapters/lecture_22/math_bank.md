## Definitions And Objects

- `Control`: psychological movement in which one fragment tries to dominate other fragments.

- `Controller`: not separate from the controlled; a fragment within the same field.

- `Direction`: goal-oriented movement; in the inward field it carries will, time, and becoming.

- `Will`: desire focused toward an end; legitimate only in practical know-how when tied to knowledge, decision, calculation.

- `Field of knowledge`: practical domain of language, driving, technology, calculation, decision, and ordinary choice.

- `Inward field`: domain of perception, attention, silence, and freedom from psychological control.

- `Choice`: necessary in the field of knowledge; in the inward field it indicates confusion and lack of perception.

- `Space`: not private mental room; inward space appears when consciousness is emptied of its content.

- `Little space`: thought-created enclosure of “me,” “you,” “we,” and “they.”

- `Silence`: not interval between noises, not cessation of noise, not thought-made, not induced by practice, not temple atmosphere.

- `Creation`: creation in living; flowering that does not know it is flowering, not merely expression or production.

- `Measure`: thought; because thought is measure, it cannot reach the immeasurable by pursuit or assertion.

- `Energy`: lecture term for gathered, undissipated seriousness/attention; do not use physical-energy formalism.

- `Sacred mind`: mind in silence where energy is gathered; not a thought-invented sacred object.

- `Prayer`: rejected as petition, supplication, begging, asking; no petition allows looking.

- `Attention`: care, seriousness, responsibility, and gathered energy in daily living.

- `Wisdom`: ending of suffering through direct observation, not callousness or escape.

## Derivation Steps

1. Opening control chain:
   1. Begin with “controller is the controlled.”
   2. Control implies a controller directing what is controlled.
   3. Direction implies will.
   4. Will establishes goal, decision, and achievement.
   5. Carrying out decision implies duration.
   6. Therefore psychological control contains time.

2. Practical knowledge versus inward perception:
   1. Practical life requires direction.
   2. Direction includes calculation, decision, and choice.
   3. This is valid in knowledge: driving, language, technical action.
   4. Inwardly, choice appears when perception is absent.
   5. Where perception is clear, there is no choice.
   6. Therefore meditation cannot be controlled practice or willful achievement.

3. From direction to space:
   1. Direction means time.
   2. Time and direction occupy the mind.
   3. Occupation leaves no space.
   4. Thought creates a small enclosed space around the self.
   5. Moving beyond that enclosure appears as fear or terror.
   6. Meditation is the freeing of the mind from consciousness as content.

4. From space to silence:
   1. If there is inward space, there is silence.
   2. Silence is not a gap between noises.
   3. Silence is not produced by thought.
   4. Silence is not induced by practice.
   5. Silence can coexist with necessary knowledge only without division.
   6. This is the “two rivers” movement.

5. From silence to creation:
   1. Silence in daily living makes the question of creation possible.
   2. Creation is not merely expression.
   3. Expression under pressure can be tension or neurosis.
   4. Creation is flowering in living.
   5. This leads to thought as measure.
   6. The immeasurable cannot be found through measured thought.

6. Energy and sacredness:
   1. Conflict dissipates energy.
   2. Control dissipates energy.
   3. Seeking, asking, demanding, waiting, and praying dissipate energy.
   4. In silence these dissipations are absent.
   5. Energy is gathered.
   6. That gathered silence is called sacred, not thought-made sacredness.

7. Prayer and no asking:
   1. Prayer as petition presumes lack, conflict, sorrow, or fear.
   2. Petition divides petitioner and receiver.
   3. No petition means no asking.
   4. No asking allows looking.
   5. This is real peace, not verbal or institutional peace.

8. Ending movement:
   1. Meditation is not one activity among others.
   2. Meditation means attention and care.
   3. Seriousness brings attention, care, and responsibility.
   4. Perception is action.
   5. Wisdom is the ending of suffering.
   6. Suffering flowers under choiceless observation and withers away.

## Notation Choices

- Use text-based relation notation only: `\text{control}`, `\text{direction}`, `\text{will}`, etc.

- Prefer `\to` for transcript-backed movement or implication, not strict logical entailment.

- Use `=` only for “controller = controlled,” and immediately qualify it as Krishnamurti’s spoken identity.

- Use `\approx` only for editorial shorthand such as attention and gathered energy; avoid `=` there.

- Avoid symbols such as \(E\), \(t\), \(S\), \(M\), operators, state spaces, or differential notation. They would falsely mathematize the lecture.

- Do not use physics terminology for energy, field, space, or time beyond Krishnamurti’s language.

- Label diagrams as “transcript-based reconstruction,” not “blackboard derivation.”

- Keep “field of knowledge” and “inward field” as prose labels, not formal domains like \(K\) and \(I\), unless a later layout absolutely requires abbreviations.
