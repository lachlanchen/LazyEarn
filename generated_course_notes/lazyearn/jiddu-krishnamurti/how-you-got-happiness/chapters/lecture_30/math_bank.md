## Definitions And Objects

- `image`: A constructed mental picture about oneself, another, security, entitlement, relationship, hurt, pleasure, or the future. It is put together by thought and memory.

- `self-image`: The central image around which other images are organized; the “me” that wants, is hurt, claims, depends, or seeks a method.

- `relationship`: The practical field of inquiry. Not an abstract bond, but daily contact in office, factory, home, sex, family, insult, hope, disappointment, and nature.

- `right relationship`: Relationship not mediated or forced by image. Do not define it positively as a technique or moral ideal.

- `energy`: Ordinary vitality, seriousness, drive. Explicitly not scientific energy.

- `registration`: The brain’s recording of insult or flattery before thought and memory take it over.

- `hurt`: Image with insult or injury as content; not separate from the image.

- `pleasure`: Image with flattery or gratifying content; paired with hurt as the same coin.

- `observer`: The apparent one who looks at the image; later shown as past, thought, memory, and image.

- `observed`: The image being looked at; not ultimately separate from the observer in the dialogue’s central insight.

- `thought`: The movement that constructs, sustains, and feeds images; it also has a “right place” in knowledge and practical functioning.

- `time`: Psychological movement from past to projected future; not clock time.

- `reality`: Use carefully. The dialogue distinguishes thought-made reality from reality independent of thought, such as nature.

- `meditation`: Introduced only near the end as the question of what remains when image-making thought ends. Do not define as practice.

## Derivation Steps

1. **From Relationship To Image**
   1. Begin with daily relationship as the concrete field.
   2. Notice disappointment, hurt, hope, and demand in relationship.
   3. Identify images of self and other behind these reactions.
   4. See that claims and expectations force relationship into a model.
   5. Conclude: image prevents the beauty of relationship.

2. **From Energy To Seriousness**
   1. Relationship is seen as of greatest importance.
   2. If relationship matters, wasted energy becomes visible.
   3. Drink, smoke, chatter, and dependence are examples of waste.
   4. The deeper waste is the image “someone else will do it for me.”
   5. Responsibility returns to oneself, but not as self-help method.

3. **From Images To Consciousness**
   1. Images appear in relationship.
   2. Images are interrelated, not isolated.
   3. They are centered on the self.
   4. The content of consciousness is largely these images.
   5. Ending image-making therefore implies transformation of consciousness as known.

4. **The Trap Of “How”**
   1. One asks how to end image-making.
   2. The “how” implies a method or system.
   3. The method implies a “me” who will apply it.
   4. That “me” is the image-maker.
   5. Therefore the question “how?” rebuilds the image it seeks to end.

5. **Registration Of Hurt And Pleasure**
   1. An insult or flattery occurs.
   2. The brain registers it.
   3. Thought and memory take it over.
   4. An image is created or reinforced.
   5. With insult-content the image is hurt.
   6. With flattery-content the image is pleasure.
   7. Pleasure and hurt are two sides of the same image-process.

6. **Past And Future Hurt**
   1. One asks whether old hurt and future hurt are different.
   2. The response says the process is the same.
   3. Future hurt is conditioned by past reaction.
   4. Remembered hurt operates like fresh insult.
   5. Do not divide past and future hurt.
   6. Look at the structure of image, not each content separately.

7. **Observer And Observed**
   1. One asks how to look at the image.
   2. But the one who looks may already be another image.
   3. The assumed observer creates division from the observed.
   4. Division creates interval, time, and conflict.
   5. The observer is the result of past memory and thought.
   6. The observer is therefore not independent of the image.
   7. The observer is the observed.

8. **Ending Image-Making**
   1. If the observer is the observed, there is no separate image-maker.
   2. If there is no thinker apart from thought, the usual image-process loses its center.
   3. Hidden images need not be separately unearthed within this logic.
   4. Consciousness as known undergoes transformation.
   5. The lecture asks what remains when thought as image-making ends.
   6. The answer is left open, except to say it is not an experience in the ordinary sense.
