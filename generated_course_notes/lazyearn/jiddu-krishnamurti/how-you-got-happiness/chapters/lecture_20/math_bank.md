## Definitions And Objects

- `Search`: the movement of seeking truth, God, a perfect life, hope, companionship, or a sustaining object.

- `Image`: the prior pattern, idea, contour, colour, or substance already held by the mind before the search begins.

- `Recognition`: the act by which the mind knows it has found what it sought; in the lecture this implies prior knowledge.

- `WhatIs`: the actual fact to be observed without escape, hope, resentment, explanation, justification, or choice.

- `Order`: virtue/righteousness arising from understanding disorder, not social respectability or imposed morality.

- `Disorder`: the condition sustained by conflict, inwardly and outwardly.

- `Control`: suppression, rejection, exclusion, or a division between controller and controlled.

- `Controller`: the apparent inner agent who tries to control anger, jealousy, despair, desire, or thought.

- `Controlled`: the thing apparently controlled; the lecture identifies this with the controller.

- `Observer`: the apparent one who observes anger, jealousy, despair, desire, or the movement of thought.

- `Observed`: the content observed; the lecture states that the observer is the observed.

- `Method`: formula, system, practice, or technique offered as meditation.

- `Practice`: repetitive, mechanical action that introduces the practitioner/practised division.

- `Observation`: seeing what is without observer, choice, support, encouragement, prejudice, formula, or accumulation.

- `Exploration`: inquiry involving an explorer/analyser and therefore accumulation.

- `Experience`: a recognized event or state; because it is recognized, it is tied to the past.

- `Attention`: complete listening or seeing without practice; includes awareness of inattention.

- `Inattention`: wandering or non-attentive movement of mind; not to be battled, but known.

- `QuietMind`: mind free from noise because seeing clearly requires quietness, not because quietness has been cultivated.

- `StillBody`: body/organism not restless, fidgeting, or mechanically compelled.

- `Love`: love not touched by pleasure, desire, jealousy, competition, sentimentality, or division.

- `Joy`: love as joy/enjoyment without motive or repeat-seeking.

- `Harmony`: total harmony of body, mind/brain, and heart.

- `Attention_day`: attention during waking life, watching every movement of thought.

- `Attention_sleep`: attention in sleep, contrasted with dreams as continuation of daily disorder.

4. Therefore seeking moves within the already-known.
5. Therefore meditation cannot be another search.

3. Control creates division between controller and controlled.
4. Division produces contradiction and distortion.
5. Therefore control cannot produce order.
6. Order comes through understanding disorder.

1. The controller appears separate from the controlled.
2. The controller tries to suppress, reject, or overcome the controlled.
3. This separation produces conflict.
4. Careful observation reveals the controller is the controlled.
5. When this is seen, the division ends.
6. When division ends, control loses its necessity.

1. A method gives a formula for meditation.
2. Practising the formula creates a practitioner and something practised.
3. This creates division.
4. Division creates conflict.
5. Conflict creates distortion and disorder.
6. Therefore method cannot lead to truth as a living thing.

1. Observation is direct seeing of what is.
2. Exploration/analysis requires an explorer or analyser.
3. The explorer accumulates.
4. Action from accumulation is action from the past.
5. Observation is continuous learning, not accumulation.

1. Experience requires recognition.
2. Recognition depends on the already-known.
3. The already-known is the past.
4. Therefore craving for experience remains within the past.
5. Demanding greater experience becomes escape from what is.

1. Clear observation requires quietness.
2. Quietness cannot be cultivated as an achievement in time.
3. Cultivation implies a cultivator and a goal.
4. That division repeats the structure of control.
5. Therefore the mind becomes quiet when it sees the necessity of quietness for observation.

1. Complete listening excludes translation, comparison, judgment, and condemnation.
2. Complete listening is attention.
3. Practising attention creates a practitioner and a goal.
4. That practice is already inattention.
5. When inattention is known choicelessly, that awareness is attention.

1. Mental silence is incomplete if the organism remains restless.
2. Bodily stillness is required as part of sensitivity.

1. Daily life continues into dreams when thought is not watched.
2. Dreams repeat the movement of waking disorder.

1. Description is not the described.

## Notation Choices

- Use descriptive roman labels, e.g. \( \mathrm{Search} \), \( \mathrm{Image} \), \( \mathrm{Attention} \), rather than symbolic variables like \(S, I, A\). This avoids false mathematical precision.

- Use \( \to \) for transcript-backed conceptual movement or implication, not strict logical entailment.

- Use \( \Rightarrow \) only for the strongest chapter-level claim, such as meditation requiring the ending of search.

- Use \( = \) sparingly for Krishnamurti’s explicit identity claims:  
  \( \mathrm{Controller} = \mathrm{Controlled} \) and \( \mathrm{Observer} = \mathrm{Observed} \). Explain that these are spoken identity claims, not algebraic identities.

- Use \( / \) to mark apparent division:  
  \( \mathrm{Controller} / \mathrm{Controlled} \), \( \mathrm{Practitioner} / \mathrm{Practised} \).

- Use \( \neg \) for negation only in compact diagrams:  
  \( \neg \mathrm{Search} \), \( \neg \mathrm{Motive} \). Avoid dense symbolic logic.

- Use subscripts for time/state distinctions only where helpful:  
  \( \mathrm{Attention}_{\mathrm{day}} \), \( \mathrm{Attention}_{\mathrm{sleep}} \).

- Avoid physics notation, operators, Hilbert-space language, dynamics, or neuroscience terms. None are transcript-backed.

- Treat all “equations” as conceptual notation for note structure, not mathematical derivations.
