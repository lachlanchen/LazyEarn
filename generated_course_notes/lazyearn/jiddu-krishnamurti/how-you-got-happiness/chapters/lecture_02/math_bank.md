## Definitions And Objects

- `K`: knowledge. Use cautiously; better in prose as “knowledge” unless a diagram needs shorthand.

- `K_p`: practical knowledge. Includes technological, navigational, physical, and ordinary functional knowledge. Transcript-backed: “knowledge has its place.”

- `K_\psi`: psychological knowledge. Use only in diagrams/tables as shorthand for accumulated image, hurt, praise, insult, and tradition.

- `E`: experience. Transcript-backed as the event that leaves a mark.

- `M`: mark or memory. Keep “mark” and “memory” visibly connected; Krishnamurti moves from mark to memory to knowledge.

- `I`: image. In the lecture, image is not a neutral mental representation; it is the past meeting the present and blocking relationship.

- `P`: past. The known is the past; knowledge has roots in the past.

- `R`: relationship. Direct relationship is blocked when image meets image or when image meets the other.

- `D`: division. Includes religious, national, ideological, inner/outer, me/you, we/they divisions.

- `C`: conflict. Use only in the transcript-backed relation `D \to C`.

- `W`: world. Use with `H` for human being only in a simple conceptual diagram, not as a social-science variable.

- `H`: human being. Prefer “human being” over “individual” unless explaining the lecture’s correction of the word individual.

## Derivation Steps

1. Knowledge As Past

1. Experience occurs.
2. Experience leaves a mark.
3. The mark becomes memory.
4. Memory is accumulated as knowledge.
5. The accumulated known is the past.
6. Therefore psychological knowledge carries the past into the present.

2. Image Blocking Relationship

1. I meet you.
2. The meeting leaves an image.
3. The image is knowledge, tradition, and past.
4. Next time, the image meets you.
5. I do not meet you directly.
6. Therefore relationship is blocked by the image.

3. Division And Conflict

1. The mind divides: East/West, Christian/Hindu/Muslim/Buddhist, capitalist/socialist/communist, we/they, I/you.
2. Division is taken as security.
3. Division produces opposition.
4. Opposition produces conflict.
5. Therefore security sought through division becomes insecurity.

4. Practical Knowledge Versus Transformation

1. Knowledge has a legitimate practical place.
2. Practical knowledge can guide technological, physical, and ordinary functional action.
3. Transformation concerns the quality of the mind.
4. The quality of the mind is not changed by accumulated knowledge alone.
5. Therefore the inquiry asks for the limit of knowledge, not the abolition of practical knowledge.

5. Word And Thing

1. A word or description names something.
2. The word is not the thing.
3. The description is not the described.
4. If one lives in description, one avoids direct contact with what is.
5. When confronted with what is, action becomes possible.

## Notation Choices

- Keep notation minimal and explanatory. This is not a physics lecture with operators, Hilbert spaces, Hamiltonians, or blackboard derivations.

- Use `\longrightarrow` for transcript-backed conceptual movement: experience to mark, division to conflict, image to blocked relationship.

- Use `=` only where the transcript explicitly supports a near-equivalence: knowledge as the known, the known as past, image as knowledge/tradition/past.

- Use `\neq` only for transcript-backed negations: word not thing, description not described. For practical versus psychological knowledge, mark as standard reconstruction and explain in prose.

- Avoid formalizing “freedom” as a variable or equation. The lecture leaves freedom from the known as the next inquiry, not a solved expression.

- Avoid using “operator,” “state,” “map,” or “function” language unless clearly framed as a pocket-safe conceptual diagram, not as mathematics.

## Uncertain Mathematics

- There are no visible equations, boards, or mathematical diagrams from validated frames.

- The lecture is conceptual and philosophical, not mathematical in the Susskind sense. Any symbolic notation must remain shorthand for transcript structure.

- “Knowledge is the past” is transcript-backed, but should not be turned into a formal identity outside the chapter’s conceptual diagramming.

- “Division implies conflict” is a central claim, not a theorem. Present it as Krishnamurti’s basic law-like formulation.

- “Action and understanding are concurrent” is supported by Anderson’s non-temporal framing, but should not be over-formalized.

- “Reincarnate now” should not be given a mathematical or doctrinal reconstruction; use “regenerate now” or “renew now” cautiously in explanatory prose.
