## Definitions And Objects

- **Thought:** conditioned, fragmentary, never new, never free; not to be abolished, but unable to capture the immeasurable.

- **Religion:** not organized belief, ritual, superstition, fear, pleasure, romanticism, or sentimentality; in this lecture, the gathering of total energy into attention.

- **Attention:** total energy of brain, mind, heart, nerve, and whole being; no exclusion, resistance, effort, frontier, or limit.

- **Concentration:** bringing thought to a point by exclusion; involves barrier-building and focus.

- **Receptivity / waiting:** rejected or questioned because each implies a receiver, an expected object, or duality.

- **Psychological hurt:** the central object of inquiry; distinct from physical pain and tied to image, comparison, identification, and memory.

- **Innocence:** used cautiously as a mind not hurt or incapable of being hurt; do not overstate the uncertain etymology.

- **Image:** the self-structure built from comparison, name, body, status, tradition, education, and social valuation; the “wall” whose tender point is touched.

- **Relationship:** possible only when there is no image between persons.

- **Education:** not merely information transfer; the shared seeing of hurt by teacher and child.

## Derivation Steps

1. Religion is usually approached through inherited belief, God, ritual, or organized authority.
2. These inherited forms are products of thought, fear, pleasure, and image.
3. Thought is conditioned and fragmentary, so it cannot capture what is immeasurable.
4. Therefore the inquiry needs another movement: gathered energy as attention.
5. Attention is not concentration because concentration excludes and builds barriers.
6. Attention is not receptivity or waiting because those imply duality: receiver/received or waiter/expected.
7. A mind capable of attention must be quiet and not psychologically hurt.
8. Psychological hurt begins and persists through comparison, imitation, and self-image.
9. The image is the vulnerable structure; when touched, it is hurt.
10. Hurt then produces fear, withdrawal, violence, and the search for safety.
11. Past hurt and future hurt are two linked questions: healing what has happened and not adding more hurt.
12. Attention cannot be used as a technique to erase hurt; understanding hurt gives rise to attention.
13. In education, when teacher and child see their hurt together, relationship changes.
14. That changed relationship is named as an act of love and prepares the next lecture.
