## Derivation Steps

1. Enjoyment becomes pleasure:
   1. There is immediate enjoyment.
   2. Thought remembers it.
   3. Thought wants the experience repeated.
   4. The carried-over enjoyment becomes pleasure.
   5. Pleasure divides.

2. Love cannot be approached through cultivation:
   1. The mind says it has no love.
   2. It tries to acquire, practice, or work at love.
   3. That movement is desire for achievement.
   4. Desire for achievement belongs to pleasure and becoming.
   5. Therefore cultivated love is a denial of love.

3. Sex becomes enormous:
   1. The mind is intellectually second-hand.
   2. Life in work, society, and thought is mechanical or unfree.
   3. Emotion becomes romantic or sentimental.
   4. Sex appears as the one field of relief or freedom.
   5. The mind gives sex exaggerated importance.

4. Celibacy is not chastity:
   1. Religious systems prescribe outward abstention.
   2. Desire and image may continue inwardly.
   3. Continued image and desire mean conflict.
   4. Chastity must therefore be a quality of mind, not merely an act.
   5. The chaste mind has no hurt, no image, no imaginative possession.

5. Pleasure and killing are connected:
   1. Pleasure is divisive.
   2. Division appears as pride, nationalism, power, ambition, and possession.
   3. These divisions support organized violence and killing.
   4. A mind that participates in designed killing cannot coherently speak of love.
   5. Seriousness implies the ending of killing as inward orientation and social design.

6. Seeing danger is action:
   1. The mind sees the danger of continuing thought as pleasure.
   2. Real seeing is not analysis over time.
   3. Seeing danger acts immediately.
   4. If danger is not seen, thought continues.
   5. Therefore the question “What must I do?” is often a search for means, not seeing.

7. Aloneness is not isolation:
   1. Ambition, nationalism, possession, and self-fulfillment isolate.
   2. The mind sees the falseness of these movements.
   3. It negates them without violence or withdrawal.
   4. The mind is alone because it is not that corruption.
   5. This aloneness is described as pure, compassionate, and intelligent.

8. Love as intelligence:
   1. Sentimental forgiveness may simply walk away.
   2. Love is not sentimental escape.
   3. Compassion gives rise to intelligence.
   4. Intelligence is sensitivity to the situation.
   5. Sensitive action is discovered in the situation, not fixed in advance.
