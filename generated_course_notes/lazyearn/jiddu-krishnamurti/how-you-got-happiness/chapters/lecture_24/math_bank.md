## Definitions And Objects

- `function`: Skill, profession, technique, career, and measurable social role. Necessary in the field of knowledge, but dangerous when treated as the model for religious inquiry.

- `measure`: The standard by which function is assessed. Krishnamurti’s point is that inquiry into religion, freedom, or truth has no such measure.

- `authority`: Dependence on another’s knowledge, scripture, priesthood, guru, belief, or inherited structure in the field of inquiry.

- `freedom`: Required at the beginning of inquiry, not at the end. Specifically freedom from authority, belief, prejudice, conclusion, image, and the structures thought has built around religion.

- `attention`: Whole, non-fragmentary attention. It is linked with intelligence, action, and the gathering of energy.

- `intelligence`: Not accumulated knowledge. In this lecture it is tied to sensitivity, perception, attention, and immediate action.

- `thought`: Response of memory, knowledge, and experience stored in the brain. It has a valid function in the field of knowledge but is limited in religious inquiry.

- `image`: The dead mental construction through which one looks at another person, nature, or oneself instead of seeing directly.

- `aloneness`: Not isolation, withdrawal, or building a wall around oneself. It is the condition that comes when the mind puts away the accretions of thought.

- `religion`: Not belief, institution, scripture, priestly authority, or verbal doctrine. In this lecture it is tied to attention, regeneration, conduct, relationship, love, death, care, and meditation.

- `meditation`: Introduced only as the next question. It is not divorced from daily life and is linked to care.

## Derivation Steps

1. Opening hesitation:
   1. Inquiry reaches a brink.
   2. Fear and trembling appear.
   3. The mind withdraws or runs away.
   4. Krishnamurti asks why seeing does not become action.

2. Education and dependence:
   1. Education trains the mind for function.
   2. Function is measurable.
   3. Status grows around function.
   4. The mind trained by measure enters a field with no measure.
   5. Not knowing how to measure, it depends.
   6. Dependence becomes authority.

3. Freedom before inquiry:
   1. Inquiry requires freedom first.
   2. Prejudice blocks inquiry.
   3. Conclusion blocks inquiry.
   4. Image blocks direct seeing.
   5. Without direct seeing, the mind depends on authority.

4. Authority and religion:
   1. Functional fields require technical discipline.
   2. The mind imports that obedience into religion.
   3. Religion, as inquiry, demands freedom from the beginning.
   4. Therefore authority and freedom cannot coexist in this field.

5. Perception, intelligence, action:
   1. Attention is not fragmentary.
   2. Sensitivity is intelligence.
   3. Perception is intelligence.
   4. Perception is therefore action.
   5. If translated into concept, the living movement is lost.

6. Book and freedom:
   1. The teacher has inherited books and functional knowledge.
   2. The student can become second-hand through them.
   3. Begin with freedom, quiet, observation, and attention.
   4. Then open the book.
   5. The book can be read with a clean eye.

7. Thought and time:
   1. Thought is response of memory, knowledge, and experience.
   2. Memory, knowledge, and experience are stored from the past.
   3. Therefore thought is of the past/time.
   4. Thought can function in knowledge.
   5. Thought cannot create or reach the timeless.

8. Falsehood and desire:
   1. The mind sees the accretions of thought.
   2. Seeing the false as false strips inward deception.
   3. Desire to experience or achieve produces illusion.
   4. Freedom requires understanding fear, desire, and pleasure at depth.
