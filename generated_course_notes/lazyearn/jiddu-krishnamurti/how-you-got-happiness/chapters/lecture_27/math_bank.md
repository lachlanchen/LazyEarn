## Definitions And Objects

- `D`: disorder, confusion, neuroticism; source-condition out of which authority is created.

- `A`: authority; not merely external power, but the psychological demand that someone else know what to do.

- `I` or `I_{\mathrm{old}}`: the “me,” centre, planner, or entity that wants to change.

- `P`: pattern of change; a method, system, discipline, ideology, book, therapy, or image of what one wants to become.

- `C`: consciousness, limited here to accumulated fragments, beliefs, memories, conclusions, and thought-movements.

- `T`: thought, especially thought as movement, not as a fixed object.

- `R_{\mathrm{psychological}}`: psychological reality produced or sustained by belief, word, image, conclusion, or illusion.

- `N`: nature; explicitly not created by thought, though thought can describe, measure, and theorize about it.

- `F`: fragmentation; political, religious, social, and psychological divisions operating separately and in conflict.

- `Q`: inquiry/question; should remain active where the transcript does not close the issue.

## Derivation Steps

1. Failed Change: The Planner And The Pattern
   1. Human beings want change.
   2. The one who wants change designs a pattern of change.
   3. The pattern may look new.
   4. The planner remains the old centre.
   5. Therefore the old centre reproduces itself through the new pattern.

2. Authority From Disorder
   1. Human beings are in disorder.
   2. Disorder creates confusion and the wish to be told what to do.
   3. This wish creates authority.
   4. Authority promises order through obedience, discipline, method, or dependence.
   5. Because authority is born from disorder, following it does not end disorder.

3. Self-Deception And Authority
   1. A method says: follow authority and observe your dependence on authority.
   2. This requires genuine dependence or self-deception.
   3. Genuine self-deception cannot simultaneously see itself clearly.
   4. Therefore self-deception is not a reliable path to understanding self-deception.
   5. The demand for such a method is itself authority.

4. Correct Action Reframed
   1. The question begins as: what is correct action?
   2. A neurotic or confused mind cannot know correct action.
   3. No external authority can supply it.
   4. Therefore the question shifts to: can the mind be free of neuroticism?
   5. This produces seriousness and energy because the mind no longer looks outward.

5. Fragmentation As The Me
   1. Consciousness contains accumulated fragments.
   2. These fragments appear as beliefs, memories, conclusions, actions, desires, and divisions.
   3. The “me” is made of this proliferation.
   4. The question becomes whether consciousness can see itself as this movement.
   5. This leads into the problem of language: if one is that movement, how can one look at it?

6. Word, Belief, Reality
   1. The word is not the thing.
   2. The word is thought.
   3. Thought creates words, images, beliefs, conclusions, and psychological constructions.
   4. Belief gives these constructions a sense of reality.
   5. Investigation stops when belief is treated as fact.
   6. The next question is how thought creates this sense of reality.

7. Thought Observing Thought
   1. The “me” is disorder because it separates.
   2. Correct action requires no disorder.
   3. Consciousness must become aware of itself.
   4. The formulation becomes: can thought be aware of its own movement?
   5. The transcript suggests “it stops” or “undergoes a radical change.”
   6. Leave this as an open inquiry, not a solved mechanism.

- Use `A` for authority.

- Use `I` only for the “I,” “me,” centre, planner, or root-self. Avoid treating `I` as a stable metaphysical entity; it is an editorial shorthand.

- Use `P` for pattern, method, system, discipline, or planned change.

- Use `C` for consciousness only in the limited sense used in this lecture: accumulated fragments, memories, conclusions, beliefs, and thought-movement.

- Use `T` for thought as movement. Avoid fixed-object notation such as `T = ...` except where explicitly marked as editorial.

- Use `R_{\mathrm{psychological}}` for psychological reality, not objective reality.
