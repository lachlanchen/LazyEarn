## Definitions And Objects

- **Seeing / perceiving:** Direct contact with what is, contrasted with looking through screens of knowledge, image, prejudice, belief, fear, pleasure, and memory.

- **Screen:** Any conditioning layer between perceiver and object: prejudice, idiosyncrasy, experience, wishes, pleasures, fears, image, belief, or cultivated memory.

- **Image:** A mental construction about the object, oneself, or another person; especially important in relationship.

- **Hearing / listening:** Hearing without translation, transformation, twisting, interference, interpretation, conclusion, like/dislike, or command-driven effort.

- **Statement:** A spoken proposition that the mind may either hear directly or turn into words, conclusions, and ideas.

- **Abstraction:** The conversion of a heard statement into an idea that then becomes a problem to implement.

- **Attention:** Complete listening without border or frontier; not a result, not caused, not maintained for reward.

- **Time-binding action:** Action based on belief, conclusion, idea, or formula; marked by interval, succession, postponement, regret, conflict, and sorrow.

- **Mechanical learning:** Accumulative learning in the field of knowledge: language, driving, craft, livelihood, technical skill, memory, and routine.

- **Knowledge:** The accumulated past operating as memory, skill, image, belief, and thought.

- **Freedom:** Not an idea or philosophical object; appears only in relation to understanding the movement and limit of knowledge.

- **Silence:** The condition from which listening is possible without wanting, reward, punishment, or self-improvement.

## Derivation Steps

1. **From seeing to time-binding action**
   1. The mind usually sees through screens.
   2. If screens intervene, the thing itself may not be seen.
   3. If the thing is directly seen, there is no postponement.
   4. If action is based on belief, conclusion, or idea, an interval appears.
   5. That interval makes action time-binding.
   6. Time-binding action brings conflict, sorrow, regret.

2. **From hearing to abstraction**
   1. A statement is heard.
   2. The mind registers words.
   3. It draws a conclusion.
   4. The conclusion becomes an idea.
   5. The mind asks how to carry out the idea.
   6. The idea becomes a problem.
   7. Competing ideas produce clash.

3. **From total listening to freedom to act**
   1. The statement is heard without abstraction.
   2. There is no comparison, agreement, disagreement, or conclusion.
   3. Listening becomes total attention.
   4. Attention has no border.
   5. Without border, concepts do not arise as resistance.
   6. The mind is free from the statement as authority.
   7. The mind is free to act.

4. **From reward-seeking to the false maintenance question**
   1. The mind asks how to maintain attention.
   2. The wish to maintain implies wanting a result.
   3. Wanting a result belongs to reward and exchange.
   4. Reward-seeking follows cause and effect.
   5. The effect becomes a new cause.
   6. Attention is not in that circle.

5. **From learning to mechanical mind**
   1. Ordinary learning accumulates knowledge.
   2. Knowledge becomes memory.
   3. Memory operates as skill and routine.
   4. Routine gives security.
   5. Education trains the brain in this security.
   6. The mind becomes mechanical.

6. **From experience to non-learning**
   1. People say they learn from experience.
   2. Experience stores memories.
   3. Memories prevent, reward, or punish.
   4. War and sorrow recur despite experience.
   5. Therefore, in this deeper sense, one has learned nothing except in knowledge.

7. **From knowledge to freedom**
   1. Thought is always old.
   2. The mind moves from known to known.
   3. This movement is time-binding.
   4. Freedom as an idea remains within knowledge.
   5. To inquire into freedom, the mind must understand the movement of knowledge.
   6. Listening out of silence is introduced as attention.
