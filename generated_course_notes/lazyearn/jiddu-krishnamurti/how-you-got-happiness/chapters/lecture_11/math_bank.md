## Definitions And Objects

- \(F\): the whole field or movement of fear; not a measurable set.

- \(F_{\mathrm{physical}}\): fears connected with physical security, food, clothing, shelter, work, pain, death, survival.

- \(F_{\mathrm{psychological}}\): fears connected with dependency, loneliness, rejection, public opinion, reputation, achievement, identification, not being.

- \(f_i\): one particular fear or “branch” of fear; useful only for the branch-by-branch contrast.

- \(T\): thought; includes recognition, naming, memory, projection, and the observer structure in the late part of the lecture.

- \(O\): observer; use only if needed for the diagram \(O=T\). Avoid making this into formal ontology.

- \(K_{\mathrm{practical}}\): necessary knowledge for language, driving, science, mathematics, ordinary skills.

- \(K_{\mathrm{psychological}}\): accumulated psychological knowledge, tradition, inherited image, experience as conditioning.

- \(E\): energy; avoid symbolizing in final prose unless a diagram needs compact labels. If used, define as undissipated attention/energy after escape and analysis end, not physical energy.

- \(I\): intelligence; practical and direct response when the whole movement is seen. Do not equate with intellect or knowledge.

## Derivation Steps

1. **From Many Fears To Root Inquiry**
   1. List common fears: death, loneliness, security, job, pain, opinion, dependency, achievement.
   2. Observe that handling \(f_1, f_2, \ldots\) one by one creates fragmentation.
   3. Analysis of fragments becomes paralysis.
   4. Therefore ask for the root or whole movement of fear.

2. **From Physical Security To Disorder**
   1. The brain needs security.
   2. Physical security requires food, clothing, shelter, and social cooperation.
   3. National, religious, occupational, and economic divisions destroy security.
   4. The mind seeks security while acting in ways that deny it.
   5. This contradiction reveals disorder.

3. **From Outer Fear To Psychological Fear**
   1. Physical pain occurs.
   2. Memory carries the emotional trace.
   3. Thought projects recurrence: “it may happen again.”
   4. Fear arises through remembered pain and projected time.

4. **From Dependency To Identification**
   1. One depends on person, belief, guru, priest, country, or image.
   2. Loss of the dependent object threatens the self-image.
   3. Loneliness and not-being appear.
   4. Identification is used to fill the gap.
   5. Identification becomes another factor of fear.

5. **Negation And Energy**
   1. Escape is seen as futile.
   2. Rationalization is seen as futile.
   3. Analysis is seen as paralysis.
   4. Identification and religious refuge are seen as futile.
   5. These movements no longer dissipate energy.
   6. The mind is left with the fact of fear.

6. **Why The “I” Cannot Act On Fear**
   1. Fear is present.
   2. The impulse arises: “What can I do?”
   3. The “I” is implicated in producing and sustaining fear.
   4. Therefore the “I” cannot solve fear as an external object.
   5. The inquiry shifts to what has brought fear about.

7. **Thought, Recognition, And Fear**
   1. A sensation or fact appears.
   2. Memory recognizes it.
   3. Recognition names it as fear.
   4. Thought projects it through time.
   5. The observer is formed as thought.
   6. Thought sustains fear.
   7. Observation without that observer ends the movement.

8. **Knowledge And Freedom**
   1. Practical knowledge is necessary.
   2. Psychological knowledge is accumulated conditioning.
   3. Psychological knowledge cannot transform fear.
   4. Looking at “what is” does not require accumulated knowledge.
   5. Freedom is not produced by knowledge.
   6. Freedom is present when burdens are absent.

- Avoid writing \(F=0\). “Fear is not” should remain a phrase or a schematic endpoint, not a numerical vanishing.
