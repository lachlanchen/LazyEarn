## Definitions And Objects

- `fact`: what is directly present, before naming, condemnation, justification, explanation, or escape.

- `violence`: not only physical killing or war; includes aggression, competition, becoming, self-discipline according to a pattern, suppression, bullying oneself, resistance, division, belief, and fulfilment-seeking.

- `me`: the ego/self/centre from which division radiates.

- `division`: separation into me/not-me, mine/not-mine, family/not-family, community/not-community, nation/nation, belief/belief.

- `cause-effect chain`: Krishnamurti’s description of cause becoming effect and effect becoming cause; use as a conceptual loop.

- `recognition`: the past naming a present response because a similar response has been known before.

- `name`: verbal identification that relates the present to the past.

- `image`: accumulated marks left by insult, flattery, memory, and inattention; governs relationship when unexamined.

- `attention`: direct awareness in which the mark is not made; not an act of will.

- `inattention`: the condition under which marks are left and images are built.

- `will`: desire, demand, resistance; described as the essence of violence.

- `resistance`: movement against what is; breeds division and violence.

- `energy`: transcript term for the force involved in violence, love hedged by jealousy/fear, and movement beyond both. Do not formalize physically.

- `freedom`: not freedom from or freedom to; “freedom is freedom.”

## Derivation Steps

1. Cause-effect chain

1. A student asks for the source or cause of violence.
2. Krishnamurti identifies the me as centre but then questions the usefulness of cause-hunting.
3. Finding a cause does not end the fact of violence.
4. Cause becomes effect.
5. Effect becomes cause.
6. Therefore the causal chain can continue indefinitely.
7. The proposed move is to look at violence as a whole.

2. Centre of division

1. The me is the centre.
2. The me divides itself from the not-me.
3. The me identifies with family, community, nation, belief, and not their opposites.
4. Each division radiates outward like waves from a stone dropped in water.
5. As long as the centre survives, violence remains.

3. Recognition and naming

1. Anger occurs.
2. At the exact moment of anger, recognition may not yet operate.
3. A moment later, memory says, “I have been angry.”
4. Recognition depends on having known anger before.
5. The past interferes with the present.
6. The present response is translated through the past.
7. Fresh seeing requires observing without that translation.

4. Image formation

1. Someone insults or flatters.
2. If there is inattention, the event leaves a mark.
3. Repeated marks accumulate.
4. The accumulation becomes an image.
5. Relationship becomes image-to-image.
6. If there is attention at the moment of insult or flattery, no mark is made.

5. Escape from the fact

1. There is the fact of violence.
2. The mind names, condemns, justifies, explains, seeks a cause, or tries to get rid of it.
3. Each movement is a distraction from the fact.
4. Distraction sustains violence.
5. Remaining with the fact without escape is the necessary movement of inquiry.

6. Will and resistance

1. A student asks whether attention is an act of will.
2. Krishnamurti defines will through “I want,” “I won’t,” “I shall,” demand and desire.
3. Will is a form of resistance.
4. Resistance is violence.
5. Therefore attention cannot be an act of will.

7. Fulfilment and frustration

1. A student asks about frustration and whether some violence is healthy.
2. Krishnamurti asks what fulfilment is.
3. The me wants expansion, achievement, fame, or importance.
4. When expansion fails, there is frustration.
5. Frustration becomes bitterness.
6. The desire to expand and its bitterness are violence.
7. Seeing this ends the structure of fulfilment/frustration.

8. Energy and resistance

1. Violence is described as energy.
2. Love surrounded by jealousy, anxiety, fear, and bitterness is also another form of energy.
3. Resistance wastes energy.
4. Resistance breeds division.
5. Division breeds violence.
6. Seeing the truth of resistance changes the movement of energy.
7. Do not turn this into a physics derivation.

9. Belief and freedom

1. Belief divides people.
2. Division breeds violence.
3. Therefore belief is violence.
4. Freedom from and freedom to are not freedom.
5. Freedom is not movement away from or toward an object.
6. Freedom is stated simply as freedom.

10. Meeting violence in another

1. A student asks how to meet another person’s violence.
2. Krishnamurti asks whether the question would arise if there were no violence in oneself.
3. If there is no violence in oneself, action is not predetermined by formula.
4. Others may call the action violent.
5. The key is the absence of violence in one’s own heart and mind.
6. A formula for non-violent action breeds violence.

## Notation Choices

- Use ordinary words as primary notation. These are conceptual diagrams, not formal mathematical definitions.

- Use \(C,E\) only for the cause-effect loop if a compact diagram or equation needs labels. Define them locally as “cause” and “effect.”

- Use `me` rather than \(I\), ego, or self as the central diagram label, because the transcript repeatedly says “the me.”

- Use `not-me` with a hyphen for consistency with transcript language.

- Use `fact` for “what is.” Avoid capitalizing it into a technical doctrine.

- Use `mark` and `image` as separate objects. A mark is the immediate trace; an image is the accumulation.

- Use `attention` and `inattention`, not mindfulness, awareness practice, meditation technique, or psychological intervention.

- Use `energy` only in prose or diagram labels. Avoid \(E\) for energy because \(E\) is already useful for effect and because the lecture’s “energy” is not physical energy.

- Avoid operators such as \(T\), \(M\), \(R\), or functions unless a diagram truly needs them. The final chapter should not read like a mathematical model of mind.

- If using arrows, keep them schematic: \(\rightarrow\) means “leads into” or “is described as breeding,” not causal proof.

## Uncertain Mathematics

- There is no visible blackboard mathematics and no frame-backed notation.

- The lecture is Krishnamurti, not Susskind. Any “math bank” material is a disciplined conceptual notation for later writing, not actual lecture equations.

- The cause-effect recurrence is a standard reconstruction from transcript language. Do not claim Krishnamurti wrote or formalized \(C \rightarrow E \rightarrow C'\).

- The recognition equation is a reconstruction. It should remain a reader aid, not a formal cognitive model.

- “Violence is energy” must not be stated as physics. No conservation law, transformation law, or energetic calculus should be introduced.

- “Attention prevents marking” is transcript-backed, but not a technique or guaranteed procedure. Phrase cautiously.

- “Freedom is freedom” resists notation. Avoid symbolic versions such as \(F\neq F_{\text{from}}\) unless used only as a tiny margin-style summary.

- Some transcript lines are garbled and should not become notation: “Rofnuda,” “self-mortgaging,” “in trust,” “sense of each other’s intentions,” and “observe the balance between the power of the mind and the existence of the person.”
