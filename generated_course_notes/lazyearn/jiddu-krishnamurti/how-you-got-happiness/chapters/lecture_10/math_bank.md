## Definitions And Objects

- `fear`: Not treated as an isolated object; described as a movement away from what is, and later as sustained through thought, memory, comparison, division, and naming.

- `what is`: The actual fact being avoided, escaped, compared, judged, or named.

- `movement`: Flight, escape, avoidance, comparison, analysis, resistance, or any psychological movement away from the actual.

- `comparison`: Measuring oneself against another, or measuring what one is against what one should be.

- `will`: The movement that says “I will not be afraid”; rejected as unable to resolve fear.

- `analysis`: A process involving time, an analyser, division, and assumed authority.

- `analyser`: A fragment of the `me`, not an independent observer outside fear.

- `thought`: Response of memory; needed for daily functioning and technological knowledge, but also sustains pleasure and fear.

- `memory`: Storehouse needed practically; psychologically, it carries past pain or pleasure into projection.

- `pleasure`: Sustained by thought and image; interrelated with fear.

- `joy`: Explicitly distinguished from pleasure; not a product of thought and not cultivable by repetition.

- `centre` / `me`: The self-centre from which one thinks, acts, judges, names, divides, and tries to overcome fear.

- `naming`: The act that places the arising fact into the past and creates division.

- `attention`: Required for inquiry and direct observation; blocked by comparison, interpretation, judgment, agreement, and disagreement.

## Derivation Steps

1. Fear As Movement Away From What Is
   1. Begin with many particular fears.
   2. Do not analyse each separately.
   3. Look for the general structure.
   4. Fear appears as escape, flight, avoidance.
   5. Therefore fear is tied to movement away from what is.
   6. Comparison is one form of that movement.

2. Why Will Does Not End Fear
   1. Fear is present.
   2. The mind says, “I will not be afraid.”
   3. This is a movement against fear.
   4. It does not understand the structure of fear.
   5. Therefore will has no meaning as an ending of fear.

3. Why Analysis Does Not End Fear
   1. Hidden fear seems to require exposure.
   2. Analysis proposes to expose causes.
   3. Analysis takes time.
   4. Analysis implies an analyser.
   5. The analyser is a fragment of the same conditioned `me`.
   6. The analyser divides itself from the thing analysed.
   7. Division sustains conflict.
   8. Therefore analysis is not the ending of fear.

4. Why Dreams Are Rejected
   1. Analysis is set aside.
   2. The question becomes whether dreams reveal hidden fear.
   3. Dreams are described as continuation of waking movement.
   4. They contain action, doing, happening.
   5. Therefore dreams are not a privileged route to freedom from fear.

5. Thought Produces Fear From Past Pain
   1. There was pain yesterday.
   2. Memory stores that pain.
   3. Thought returns to the memory.
   4. Thought projects possible repetition tomorrow.
   5. The projection is fear.
   6. Therefore thought brings about fear.

6. Thought Sustains Pleasure And Fear Together
   1. There was pleasure yesterday.
   2. Thought forms or sustains an image of it.
   3. Thought desires repetition.
   4. The repetition may not happen.
   5. Fear of loss or frustration appears.
   6. Therefore pleasure and fear are interrelated through thought.

7. Thought Is Necessary Yet Dangerous
   1. Thought is the response of memory.
   2. Memory is needed for daily and technological functioning.
   3. Thought must operate efficiently in practical matters.
   4. The same thought sustains fear and pleasure psychologically.
   5. Therefore thought cannot simply be killed, resisted, or forgotten.
   6. The question becomes how the mind observes thought.

8. Centre And Fear
   1. Thought creates a centre as `me`.
   2. The centre acts, thinks, judges, evaluates, condemns.
   3. The centre divides.
   4. It separates itself from fear.
   5. It says it must get rid of fear, analyse it, overcome it, resist it.
   6. This strengthens fear.
   7. Therefore fear must be observed without the centre.

9. Naming And Division
   1. Fear arises.
   2. The mind names it “fear.”
   3. Naming invokes the past.
   4. Naming divides the observer from the observed.
   5. Division produces resistance, conflict, and fear.
   6. Therefore the inquiry asks for observation without naming.
