## Definitions And Objects

- `C`: content of consciousness. Includes personal, collective, racial, acquired, hidden, transitory, and conditioned material.

- `K`: the known. Includes possessions, relationships, experiences, beliefs, memory, identity, and acquired structures of security.

- `M`: the me. Editorial shorthand for the self as identified with content.

- `A`: analysis. A process that divides analyser and analysed, requires time, and cannot produce a complete act.

- `O`: observer/analyser. In this lecture, the observer or analyser is not separate from what is observed or analysed.

- `T`: time. Use only in Krishnamurti’s psychological sense: continuity, duration, memory, becoming, and thought-made preservation.

- `Th`: thought. Thought creates objects, ideals, doctrines, names, images, gods, and attempted immortality within time.

- `W`: whole map of consciousness. A transcript-backed metaphor for seeing all content without direction or choice.

- `CA`: choiceless awareness. Awareness without direction, choice, effortful discernment, or analysis.

- `S`: stream of consciousness. The continuing river of unemptied human content: struggle, pain, unhappiness, fear, accumulation.

- `B`: beauty itself. Distinguished from beautiful objects; not to be reduced to an object in consciousness.

- `I_t`: thought-made immortality. Books, statues, cathedrals, family name, doctrine, righteous self-image, gods made by thought.

## Derivation Steps

1. Fear Of Death As Fear Of The Known
   1. Ordinary thought treats death as something to avoid.
   2. The feared loss is named concretely: wife, house, acquisitions, content.
   3. These are instances of the known.
   4. Therefore fear of death is fear of losing the known.
   5. Since the known is content of consciousness, fear of death is tied to the preservation of consciousness as content.

2. Emptying Content As Real Death
   1. The organism may die biologically, but the inquiry asks what dies psychologically.
   2. Consciousness has content: attachment, dependency, acquisition, anxiety, position, power.
   3. This content makes the limits of consciousness.
   4. Emptying the content ends those limits.
   5. Therefore real death, in this inquiry, is the emptying of content, not merely bodily ending.

3. Why Analysis Fails
   1. Analysis proposes to expose the whole content.
   2. Analysis requires an analyser and the analysed.
   3. The analyser is itself part of the analysed content.
   4. Analysis also requires time.
   5. Each analysis is incomplete.
   6. Incompletion is carried over as memory.
   7. Memory becomes the basis for the next analysis.
   8. Therefore analysis repeats the field it tries to end.
   9. Hence analysis is paralysis.

4. From Analysis To Choiceless Awareness
   1. If analysis cannot empty the content, another question arises: how can the whole content be seen?
   2. The map metaphor introduces two modes: directed route-seeking and whole seeing.
   3. Direction narrows attention to a path.
   4. Choiceless awareness sees the whole map without selecting a route.
   5. Seeing the whole content without choice gives energy to go beyond it.
   6. The movement is not analytical, progressive, or time-bound.

5. Reincarnation Reframed
   1. Conventional reincarnation looks to a next life.
   2. Krishnamurti asks why no one speaks of incarnation now.
   3. If content is not emptied, it continues like a river.
   4. This river is not an individual soul-theory but the continuity of shared human content.
   5. If content is emptied, one does not belong to that stream.
   6. Then living each moment anew is described as dying each moment.

6. Immortality And Time
   1. Human beings try to immortalize themselves through works, buildings, names, religious ideals, and thought-made gods.
   2. These are products of thought.
   3. Time is thought.
   4. Therefore thought-made immortality lies within the field of time.
   5. What lies in time is corruptible and destructible.
   6. Therefore immortality is not in thought-made objects.
   7. Beauty itself, not the object of beauty, is named as immortal.
   8. Beauty itself is not in the field of consciousness.

7. Final Synthesis
   1. The content of consciousness is the me.
   2. Dying to content is dying to the me.
   3. Time is thought, and dying to time is love.
   4. Therefore love is not pleasure, sex, possession, or thought’s image of love.
   5. Living is dying when content is emptied.
   6. Living, love, and death are one indivisible movement.

## Notation Choices

- Use `\equiv` sparingly for editorial conceptual equivalence, not strict mathematical identity.

- Prefer words inside `\text{...}` rather than symbolic abstraction when the relation must remain source-conscious.

- Use `\Rightarrow` for lecture-supported direction of implication: for example, analysis leading to time and incompletion.

- Use `\subset` only for editorial grouping, such as thought-made immortality belonging within the field of time.

- Do not use physics notation, operators, Hilbert-space language, dynamics, or formal psychology notation.

- Keep `time` explicitly psychological and transcript-bound. Do not let it drift into physical time.

- Use `content`, `known`, `me`, `thought`, `time`, `analysis`, `awareness`, `stream`, `beauty`, `living`, `love`, and `death` as the governing vocabulary.

- If using abbreviations like `C`, `K`, `M`, define them once and immediately return to plain-language notation.

- Treat Anderson’s lateral/vertical contrast as a diagrammatic metaphor, not as a formal coordinate system.
