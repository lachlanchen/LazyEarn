## Definitions And Objects

- `living`: what is actually taking place in daily existence; in this lecture, initially exposed as struggle, conflict, escape, routine, violence, frustration, and battle.

- `conflict`: division, opposition, fragmentation, and the battle between fragments.

- `fragmentation`: inward and outward division; examples include “you and me,” “we and they,” nations, classes, roles, and inner fragments.

- `perception`: direct seeing of what is, without the separate observer trying to change, adjust, or integrate the seen.

- `action`: what follows from perception; not delayed method, not theoretical conclusion, and not action born from conflict.

- `energy`: transcript term used in two modes: energy released through conflict and energy released through perception.

- `observer / observed`: the central distinction around perception; in perception there is no separate observer standing apart from what is seen.

- `consciousness`: its content; not an empty container separate from thoughts, fears, pleasures, beliefs, suffering, and identifications.

- `content`: the concrete contents named in the transcript: thoughts, anxieties, identifications, conflicts, attachments, fears, pleasures, agony, suffering, beliefs, neurotic actions.

- `death`: introduced through the question of whether consciousness with its content ends or continues.

- `karma`: in this transcript, glossed simply as “to act”; do not expand into doctrinal detail.

## Derivation Steps

1. Conflict chain:
   1. Human beings accept life as battle.
   2. Battle is rooted in division.
   3. Division appears as fragmentation inwardly and outwardly.
   4. Where there is fragmentation, there is conflict.
   5. Conflict releases divisive energy.
   6. Divisive energy expresses as violence, anxiety, escape, and disorder.

2. Perception-action chain:
   1. One sees fragmentation as it is.
   2. One does not try to integrate fragments.
   3. The separate observer is absent in perception.
   4. There is only seeing.
   5. Seeing is already movement into action.
   6. This action is different from the action of conflict.

3. Education chain:
   1. Student lives in conflict.
   2. Teacher also lives in conflict.
   3. The teacher need not wait to become conflict-free.
   4. Teacher and student begin by acknowledging shared conflict.
   5. They look together in relationship.
   6. This shared inquiry can dissolve conflict and produce care.

4. Love chain:
   1. Battle means competition, domination, and self-advancement.
   2. In such battle, love has no place.
   3. Love appears only where perception and action are not divided.
   4. This is linked to care, responsibility, and non-fragmentary living.

5. Consciousness-to-death chain:
   1. Living, love, and death are introduced as one movement.
   2. To inquire into death, one must ask what consciousness is.
   3. Consciousness is its content.
   4. The content is common, though particular forms vary.
   5. Death raises the question: ending of consciousness or continuity of consciousness?
   6. Belief in continuity can become comfort and postponement.
   7. Postponement contradicts immediate action.

## Notation Choices

- Use ordinary text labels inside equations: `\text{conflict}`, `\text{perception}`, `\text{content}`. Avoid physics-style notation that implies a formal theory.

- If using energy notation, define it as editorial shorthand only: `E_{\text{conflict}}` and `E_{\text{perception}}`. Do not imply measurable physical energy.

- Use arrows `\to` for narrative or conceptual sequence, not causal proof.

- Use `=` only where the transcript explicitly supports non-separation or identity-like phrasing, especially `\text{consciousness} = \text{content}` and `\text{observer} = \text{observed}`.

- Prefer `\neq` for the contrast between conflict-action and perception-action: `\text{action}_{\text{conflict}} \neq \text{action}_{\text{perception}}`.

- Avoid set notation except for a clearly provisional content list. If used, say it is not exhaustive.

- Do not introduce operators, Hilbert spaces, derivatives, commutators, or physics notation. There is no lecture basis for them here.

## Uncertain Mathematics

- There is no frame-backed mathematics. Every symbolic expression here is either transcript-backed conceptual compression or standard editorial reconstruction.

- `\text{observer} = \text{observed}` should be stated cautiously because one transcript line reads “The observer is the observer,” likely a transcription error.

- `E_{\text{conflict}}` and `E_{\text{perception}}` are useful shorthand but not physical variables. Avoid writing as if energy is conserved, transformed, or quantified.

- The content set of consciousness is not exhaustive and should not become a formal taxonomy.

- The reincarnation/karma chain must remain a map of Krishnamurti’s critique of postponement, not a formal account of Indian doctrines.

- “Cause/effect” appears in the reincarnation discussion, but no formal causal model should be introduced.
