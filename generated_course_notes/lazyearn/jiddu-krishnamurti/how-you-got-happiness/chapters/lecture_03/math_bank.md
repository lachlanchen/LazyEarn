## Definitions And Objects

- **Division**: nationalistic, linguistic, religious, professional, social, and inward separation; source condition for conflict.

- **Contradiction**: the inward and outward state of being split, opposed, or fragmented.

- **Fragment**: one partial movement of the self, such as belief, prejudice, desire, fear, ambition, or judgement.

- **Observer**: a fragment that takes the position of examiner, censor, analyser, judge, or controller.

- **Censor**: the judging observer that says “right/wrong,” “I will/I won’t,” “this path/not that path.”

- **Image**: accumulated knowledge, memory, hurt, flattery, insult, belief, or relationship-picture through which one observes.

- **Whole action**: action that is complete, non-contradictory, not broken up, not dependent on authority, and not productive of further sorrow.

- **Learning**: observation without the observer and without accumulation; not the storage of yesterday’s perception.

- **Accumulation**: the carrying forward of knowledge, insult, flattery, or previous observation as the basis for the next act of seeing.

- **Real meditation**: in this lecture, watching without the observer and without image; do not expand beyond that.

## Derivation Steps

1. From world disorder to the question of action:
   1. Human life shows division, conflict, sorrow, and contradiction.
   2. Standard answers appear: political direction, ideology, belief, authority, organized religion, personal experience.
   3. Each standard answer can operate as a fragment.
   4. Therefore the question becomes whether there is action not born of fragmentation.
   5. This yields the search for whole action.

2. From whole action to right relationship:
   1. Whole action cannot be a theory, abstraction, or philosophical concept.
   2. It must be an actual way of living.
   3. The speaker cannot function as authority or teacher.
   4. The listener must observe his own reactions through the words.
   5. Communication becomes shared observation.

3. From society to oneself:
   1. The world contains greed, anger, ambition, competition, violence, prejudice, and division.
   2. These are also inward facts.
   3. Therefore the world is not merely outside oneself.
   4. The question “what should I do about the world?” changes meaning.
   5. The working question becomes “how do I understand myself?”

4. From self-understanding to observation:
   1. The self is fragmented.
   2. One fragment may take charge as observer, censor, or analyser.
   3. That observer claims authority over other fragments.
   4. This authority is itself fragmentary.
   5. Therefore observation by a fragment remains contradictory.

5. From image to separation:
   1. One observes a person, tree, or event through knowledge.
   2. Knowledge forms an image.
   3. The image becomes the observer.
   4. The observer stands apart from the observed.
   5. Separation creates conflict.

6. From violence to observer = observed:
   1. Violence is present as anger, jealousy, brutality, ambition, comparison.
   2. The censor condemns, justifies, or tries to escape violence.
   3. This implies an observer separate from violence.
   4. Krishnamurti questions that separation.
   5. The observer is the observed; the observer is violence.
   6. Where observer and observed are divided, violence continues.

7. From learning to non-accumulation:
   1. One observes oneself and says one has learned.
   2. The next observation may be made through what was learned.
   3. That accumulated knowledge prevents fresh perception.
   4. Accumulation becomes the observer/censor/conditioned entity.
   5. Learning requires looking without accumulating.
