## Definitions And Objects

- `thought`: In this lecture, the movement that creates images, symbols, rituals, permanence, religious experience, and authority structures. Do not define as cognition in a scientific or psychological sense.

- `image`: A construction of thought that is then treated as sacred, permanent, protective, or authoritative.

- `religion-as-image`: Organized or ritualized religion insofar as it consists of belief, image, worship, superstition, ritual, and tradition.

- `negation`: Not suppression, rejection for reward, or cutting off. It is the perception of the false as false.

- `observer`: The self-reference that enters direct looking, as in “I am looking” or “I must say the right thing.”

- `experience`: Something sought as spiritually special, but known only through recognition.

- `recognition`: The act by which an experience is identified; it implies prior knowledge.

- `the new`: What cannot be recognized as an object already known.

- `beauty`: In Krishnamurti’s usage here, connected with the absence or abandonment of the self, not with aesthetic gratification.

- `gratification through beauty`: Satisfaction, relief, sacred feeling, or comfort obtained through words, chant, robes, incense, architecture, or atmosphere.

- `knowledge`: In the disputed spiritual claim “I know,” knowledge is tied to the past, the dead, the already finished.

- `authority`: Must be split into practical authority and inward/spiritual authority. Practical authority includes laws, road rules, and cautious medical advice. Inward authority includes priests, gurus, intermediaries, and claimed possessors of spiritual knowledge.

- `education without authority`: A learning relation where teacher and student question together rather than one obeying the other psychologically.

## Derivation Steps

1. Idolatry of the mind
   1. Human beings want something beyond everyday thought and routine.
   2. Thought cannot directly find that “something more.”
   3. Thought creates symbols, images, rituals, and ideals.
   4. The mind worships what it has created.
   5. Therefore the worship returns to the self-made image.

2. Negation
   1. The mind sees religious images, beliefs, rituals, and traditions as false.
   2. It does not deny them in order to gain a better state.
   3. The perception of falseness is itself the action.
   4. Therefore negation is seeing, not pursuit.

3. Observer interruption
   1. A student looks at a tree without obvious blockage.
   2. The question arises: “Were you thinking of yourself as looking?”
   3. Self-reference appears.
   4. The student hesitates and falls out of the act.
   5. Krishnamurti identifies this as the observer.

4. Experience-recognition
   1. A person seeks a special religious experience.
   2. To know that the experience has occurred, the person must recognize it.
   3. Recognition depends on prior knowledge.
   4. What depends on prior knowledge is not new.
   5. Therefore the sought “new experience” is self-deception.

5. Beauty/gratification
   1. Chant, words, robes, incense, architecture, and colored light can produce satisfaction.
   2. This satisfaction is interpreted as sacredness or contact with something.
   3. Krishnamurti distinguishes this from beauty without self.
   4. Therefore religious aesthetics can become gratification through beauty.

6. Knowledge as dead
   1. A guru or priest says, “I know” or “I have experienced.”
   2. What is known belongs to what is already past.
   3. A living thing is moving and never the same.
   4. Therefore the living cannot be possessed as knowledge.

7. Authority and obedience
   1. The mind wants security, comfort, and protection.
   2. Fear, despair, loneliness, and ignorance make a person accept someone who claims to know.
   3. This acceptance suspends reason and intelligence.
   4. The person obeys spiritually while perhaps rejecting political tyranny.
   5. Therefore inward authority becomes a form of slavery.

8. Education without authority
   1. Current education trains thought, memory, acceptance, obedience, and reaction.
   2. Students are shocked when asked to inquire together without following.
   3. They approach attention, then freeze at the precipice.
   4. The unresolved problem is whether education can exist without psychological authority.
