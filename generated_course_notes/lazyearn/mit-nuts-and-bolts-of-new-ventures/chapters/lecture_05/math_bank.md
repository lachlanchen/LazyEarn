## Definitions And Objects
- `n`: employee headcount.
- `\operatorname{support}(n)`: stage-appropriate people/organizational support structure as a function of headcount.
- `t_{\mathrm{fit}}`: time required to judge whether a candidate or consultant is a real fit.
- `n_{\mathrm{RSA}}`: RSA headcount in the growth example.
- `n_{\mathrm{Thermo}}`: Thermo Fisher headcount in the mission-scale example.
- `n_{\mathrm{retrofit}}`: headcount of the company that had to retrofit systems and structure.
- `t_{\mathrm{stand\mbox{-}alone\ acquired\ unit}}`: period during which an acquired company remains largely intact before deeper integration.
- `P`: amount of process / documentation / structure in the organization.
- `B(P)`: bureaucracy indicator; `1` means process has crossed into obstruction.
- `F_{\mathrm{critical}}`: set of functions the business must execute reliably in order to scale.
- `S`: scalability indicator for the operating model; `S=0` means non-scalable.
- `T`: technical contribution or strategic capability of a hire.
- `R`: cultural or behavioral risk of a hire.
- `D(T,R)`: hiring decision rule balancing technical contribution against cultural risk.
- `\succ`: qualitative ordering, read as “more failure-critical than” or “more often sinks ventures than.”

## Derivation Steps
Failure-mode ordering
1. The lecture opens by ranking causes of venture failure.
2. Technology and funding are explicitly demoted relative to people issues.
3. The session then treats organizational design as a first-order variable, not a cleanup task.

Headcount-to-support threshold
1. Culture starts with founders, alignment, trust, and routines.
2. Very small teams can carry that informally.
3. As `n` enters the `25` to `50` range, outside advice becomes useful.
4. By about `50` people, some explicit people/organizational support becomes hard to avoid.

Trial-hire timing
1. A high-stakes hire is uncertain.
2. The suggested move is consultant-first or trial-first.
3. Observe actual contribution and interpersonal give-and-take.
4. In ordinary cases, fit can be judged in less than `3` months.
5. The older `90`-day probation period is offered as the institutional analogue.

Acquisition integration
1. A large firm may buy product plus talent, not product alone.
2. To preserve value, keep the acquired unit relatively intact at first.
3. Retain the founder and core technical people.
4. Use the intact period to assess talent and operating fit.
5. Integrate more deeply only after that value has been stabilized.

Process versus bureaucracy
1. Put simple process and documentation in place early.
2. Use systems and low-cost tools to avoid later retrofit.
3. Keep process tied to clarity, fairness, compliance, and execution.
4. Test whether process still helps the business run.
5. If process blocks customer response or revenue, it has become bureaucracy.

Scalability constraint
1. A founder initially does many things directly.
2. Continued direct execution of every critical function creates a bottleneck.
3. Bottlenecks lead to burnout, anger, and team attrition.
4. Therefore a founder-operated-everything model does not scale.
5. Delegation plus trusted complements is the necessary next step.

Hiring trade-off
1. Evaluate the candidate’s technical contribution `T`.
2. Evaluate behavioral and cultural risk `R`.
3. If `R` is a toxic character flaw, technical brilliance does not rescue the hire.
4. If `T` is exceptional and `R` is manageable, containment may be rational.
5. The final decision is a trade-off, not a single-axis ranking.

Conflict resolution
1. Execution slippage often appears before the real interpersonal cause is visible.
2. Ask what remains unsaid or “undelivered.”
3. Bring the covert issue into the open.
4. Once the real issue is explicit, it becomes discussable and solvable.
5. If the pattern is ego-driven and resistant, outside facilitation may be needed.

## Notation Choices
- Treat all symbols here as editorial working notation; the lecture itself provides no board notation.
- Use `n` for headcount and `t` for time.
- Use `\approx`, `\lesssim`, and `\gtrsim` throughout; the lecture’s numbers are heuristic and approximate.
- Use `\succ` only for qualitative ranking, never for numerical comparison.
- Keep qualitative states inside `\text{}` rather than forcing false algebra.
- Use `P` for process level and `B(P)` for the point where process becomes obstructive bureaucracy.
- Use `T` and `R` for the hire-quality trade-off because the lecture explicitly balances capability against cultural disruption.
- Use `S=0` only as a compact note-taking shorthand for “not scalable”; do not present it as formal lecture notation.
- Prefer simple implication arrows `\Rightarrow` and process arrows `\rightarrow` over optimization notation; the lecture is rule-based, not calculus-based.
