# Chapter Plan
## Lecture Arc
- Hadzima opens by recalling the course’s first-day claim that a successful venture requires several elements to come together, then makes the sharper motivational point for this session: ventures are sunk more often by people issues than by technology or funding. That ranking is the lecture’s governing premise and explains why the session becomes a live panel instead of a doctrinal lecture.
- He then hands the room to Vivian, who reframes the topic in founder-operating language: how to build an organization, hire, pay, solve problems, and preserve what is distinctive about the company. The panel introductions matter because they establish three recurring lenses for the whole chapter: founder-scale people building, large-company HR and acquisition, and process/operations design.
- The first major pivot is from motivation to first principles: culture begins before formal HR, in founder alignment, trust, stated values, and daily routines. The panel then turns that intuition into rough thresholds by asking when outside help becomes necessary and when an actual people function starts to matter.
- From there the lecture narrows from culture in general to role structure: friendship versus professional role, the ambiguity of “co-founder,” the dangers of co-CEO arrangements, and the importance of deciding who actually runs what. This is the first place where the session repeatedly converts vague interpersonal advice into decision rules.
- A second pivot moves to hiring logic: try-before-you-buy arrangements, consultant trials, three-month evaluation horizons, and whole-person judgment rather than credential-only judgment. The lecture then folds back to culture by stressing that hiring behavior itself is one of the main ways culture is created.
- The middle of the panel broadens again into growth and acquisition. Small firms win on speed, flexibility, and relationship; large firms win on scale and reach. But the real transition is that even acquisition strategy is treated as a people problem: retention of founders, keeping critical talent, preserving what was valuable in the acquired company, and integrating without destroying the thing that was purchased.
- The later discussion pivots inward to founder psychology and conflict: authenticity, self-awareness, complementing one’s weaknesses, radical candor, trust, and how to surface hidden conflict before it damages execution. Hadzima’s own co-founder story is the lecture’s clearest case study of how team failure can emerge without anyone initially naming the real problem.
- The closing movement returns to process, bureaucracy, AI tools, when to hire HR, culture fit versus technical brilliance, and the CEO’s role in setting tone across scale and geography. The session ends not with a theorem but with a recap: people are the medium through which every other part of the venture either coheres or fails.

## Section Outline
- `People Issues As The Dominant Failure Mode`  
  Open with Hadzima’s framing claim that people issues sink ventures more often than technology or funding, then briefly orient the panel voices so the chapter reads as guided argument rather than a disconnected transcript.

- `Culture Starts Before HR`  
  Keep the first substantive answer in the lecture’s order: founder alignment, trust, shared values, cadence, and the idea that hiring behavior already constitutes culture formation. Include a standalone `Question & Answer`: `When should we start thinking about the HR function, and how do we preserve the company’s DNA as investors arrive?`

- `Roles, Friendship, And The Co-Founder Boundary`  
  Gather the discussion of friendship inside small teams, role clarity, why co-CEO structures usually fail, and why one should not hand out “co-founder” lightly. Include a standalone `Question & Answer`: `What is a co-founder, and when should title, control, or equity wait until fit is clearer?`

- `Hiring As A Decision Process, Not A Credential Filter`  
  Follow the lecture’s move into consultant-first trials, the rough three-month fit horizon, the older 90-day probation analogy, and the practice of assessing the whole person in formal and informal settings. Include a standalone `Question & Answer`: `How do we test uncertain hires without locking ourselves into the wrong long-term relationship?`

- `Growth, Acquisition, And The Structure–Bureaucracy Trade-Off`  
  Preserve the transition from startup nimbleness versus large-company scale into acquisition integration, SOPs, flat structures, and the difference between useful process and obstructive bureaucracy. Include a standalone `Question & Answer`: `When do process and hierarchy help the company, and when do they start slowing the business down?`

- `Founder Self-Awareness, Conflict, And Letting Go`  
  Keep together the responses on authentic leadership, complementing one’s weaknesses, co-founder conflict, radical candor, and the difficulty of surrendering control as the company grows. Include a standalone `Question & Answer`: `How do we surface conflict honestly, and how does a founder know when to step back rather than step in?`

- `Culture As A Selection Rule And A CEO Obligation`  
  End with the lecture’s final loop: when to hire HR, how to write mission and values, how to weigh technical brilliance against cultural risk, and how a CEO’s own behavior scales culture across a growing or global company. Include a standalone `Question & Answer`: `Should we hire the brilliant but culturally risky person, and what kind of culture can actually scale?`
