## Definitions And Objects
- **Technology project:** pre-traction exploration of applications for a technology with unresolved customer understanding.

- **Company project:** execution around a demo, package, or product direction with clear customer focus.

- **Customer-traction boundary:** the dashed line in `lecture_11_figure_02.png`; separates exploration without repeat customer traction from execution with repeat customer traction.

- **Non-recourse funding:** in the lecture’s practical usage, grants or grant-like funding where repayment or commercial success is not the funder’s expected return.

- **Recourse funding:** in the lecture’s broad usage, equity, debt, VC, angel, or growth capital that creates obligations before or after customer traction.

- **Market-driven agency:** agency where the marketplace is the customer; examples given include NSF and DOE.

- **Mission-driven agency:** agency where the agency or its contractors are the customer; examples given include DOD and NASA.

- **Phase I:** first federal funding stage; must be won before Phase II application.

- **Phase II:** larger follow-on stage after Phase I work.

- **Phase III:** commercialization tracking stage with no ordinary federal funding in the lecture’s framing.

- **Phase IIb:** matching program tied to qualifying private investment; not technically Phase III, though shown near the commercialization side.

- **SBIR:** standardized spelling for the chapter; transcript also says SBR/SIBR.

- **STTR:** related program requiring a research institution partner.

- **Solicitation/topic:** official agency funding call; the downloaded PDF is the ground-truth document.

- **Quad chart/project pitch:** compact pre-application summary used to approach a program manager and test agency fit.

- **Scope of work:** the work package that determines whether two proposals are distinct or impermissibly duplicative.

## Derivation Steps
**Research-to-market funding rule**

1. Start with disruptive technology that customers cannot yet evaluate as a usable product.
2. Before repeat customer traction, the project is still application exploration.
3. Recourse capital at this stage creates obligations before direction is known.
4. Grants and non-recourse funding are suited to reducing the technology to practice.
5. After repeat customer traction, recourse or growth capital becomes appropriate.

**VC-equivalent SBIR scale**

1. Use the lecture’s annual SBIR funding figure: \(\$4\text{B}\).
2. Use the lecture’s VC deployment heuristic: VCs deploy about \(1/5\) of capital per year.
3. Solve for the equivalent fund size: \(\$4\text{B}/(1/5)\).
4. Conclude the rough comparison: \(\$20\text{B}\) VC-equivalent fund.

**Phase IIb match**

1. Let \(P\) be qualifying private recourse investment.
2. The lecture states a two-to-one private-to-federal match.
3. Therefore \(M(P)=P/2\) before the cap.
4. The federal match is capped at \(\$500{,}000\).
5. Thus \(\$1\text{M}\) private yields \(\$500\text{K}\), while \(\$250\text{K}\) private yields \(\$125\text{K}\).

**Multiple applications and duplicate-award constraint**

1. Define each proposal by its scope of work, not merely by the final product.
2. Different scopes for the same product may be separately fundable if disclosed.
3. The same or similar technology may be submitted to multiple agencies with disclosure.
4. Duplicate work cannot be awarded twice.
5. If duplicate awards arrive, the company must choose rather than accept both.

**STTR allocation arithmetic**

1. Let total award budget be \(B\).
2. STTR requires the small business to receive at least \(0.40B\).
3. STTR requires the research institution to receive at least \(0.30B\).
4. The minimum required allocations sum to \(0.70B\).
5. The remaining \(0.30B\) can be allocated within the program and agency constraints.

**University overhead example**

1. Start with a \(\$300{,}000\) Phase I award.
2. Allocate \(30\%\) to a research institution: \(0.30\times \$300{,}000=\$90{,}000\).
3. Apply the lecturer’s rough overhead intuition: about half may disappear into overhead.
4. Actual research support may be roughly \(\$45{,}000\) or less.

**Proposal timing**

1. Broad first-time proposal preparation is roughly two months.
2. Specific mission solicitations require additional matching work.
3. Add roughly one month for specific solicitation fit.
4. Registration systems can take about 30 days.
5. Registration can run in parallel with proposal writing.

## Notation Choices
- Use `SBIR` throughout the chapter; mention `SIBR` only as a DOD pronunciation note if needed.

- Use `STTR` for the research-institution-partner program.

- Use `Phase I`, `Phase II`, `Phase III`, and `Phase IIb`; avoid `Phase 1` unless quoting speech.

- Use \(P\) for private investment in Phase IIb matching and \(M(P)\) for the federal match.

- Use \(B\) for total award budget when discussing percentage allocation.

- Use \(\operatorname{scope}(P_i)\) only if formalizing duplicate-work logic; otherwise write “scope of work” in prose.

- Use \(\Rightarrow\) as a practical decision-rule arrow, not as a theorem or legal implication.

- Use “repeat customer traction” as the key boundary term, because it is both slide-backed and transcript-backed.

- Treat “market-driven” and “mission-driven” as the lecturer’s operational taxonomy, not a universal statutory classification.
