Louis, the founder of a labor-force management company, describes the first large contract as nine million dollars of annualized revenue. The customer would pay invoices after thirty days. His company, however, had to fund payroll and other costs as the work was performed. By his rough calculation, five weeks of outflow required about one million dollars before the first payment could be trusted to arrive.

Working capital pays for the interval between those dates. It carries the inventory bought before sale, the labor performed before collection, the tax reserved before payment, the new employee trained before productivity, and the capacity installed before utilization. Revenue

is the price assigned to delivered or contractually earned work, according to the applicable accounting rules. It is not cash available to spend.

Gross profit

is revenue less the direct cost required to produce that revenue. It must still support overhead, financing, tax, repair, and loss.

Operating profit

includes the period’s operating expenses. It may contain revenue not yet collected and omit future cash commitments.

Cash

is money presently available to the relevant person or entity. Cash in a company is not automatically the owner’s personal spending money.

Balance-sheet capacity

is the stock of cash, receivables, inventory, obligations, debt, and equity that can absorb timing and loss. Its quality matters as much as its size.
