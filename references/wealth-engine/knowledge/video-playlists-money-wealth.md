# Best Video Playlists for Money, Wealth, Business, and Markets

Updated: 2026-04-13

This guide is a curated video-study path for the `wealth-from-first-principles` project.
It prioritizes official university or institution sources over finance-influencer content.

## Selection rules

- Prefer official course pages from MIT OpenCourseWare, Yale, Stanford, and Santa Fe Institute.
- Prefer full lecture series over clipped advice.
- Prefer courses that explain mechanisms, institutions, incentives, and decision structure.
- Treat "chaos" as nonlinear systems and dynamical behavior, not trading mysticism.

## Best-first path

If you only want one strong sequence, use this order:

1. MIT OpenCourseWare: Principles of Microeconomics
2. MIT OpenCourseWare: Principles of Macroeconomics
3. Yale Online: Financial Markets
4. Yale Open Courses: Game Theory
5. Stanford eCorner: Startups
6. MIT OpenCourseWare: Blockchain and Money
7. Yale Open Courses: Financial Theory
8. Yale Online: Capitalism: Success, Crisis, and Reform
9. Yale Online: The Global Financial Crisis
10. Santa Fe Institute / Complexity Explorer: Introduction to Dynamical Systems and Chaos

## Beginner

### MIT OpenCourseWare: Principles of Microeconomics

- Topic fit: economics, markets, firms, pricing, trade, incentives, commercial behavior
- Why it is high value: strong first-principles base for scarcity, production, competition, monopoly, labor, taxation, uncertainty, and trade
- Best for: understanding how firms, households, and markets behave before moving into finance
- Official page: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-01-principles-of-microeconomics-fall-2023/video_galleries/video-lectures/

### MIT OpenCourseWare: Principles of Macroeconomics

- Topic fit: money, inflation, growth, unemployment, policy, financial markets, exchange rates
- Why it is high value: strong foundation for aggregate output, financial conditions, rate policy, open-economy dynamics, and recession thinking
- Best for: understanding the macro environment in which wealth is built or destroyed
- Official page: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-02-principles-of-macroeconomics-spring-2023/video_galleries/1402-lecture-videos/

### Yale Online: Financial Markets

- Topic fit: finance, banking, securities, insurance, markets, bubbles, behavioral finance
- Why it is high value: one of the best free public introductions to how financial institutions manage risk and channel capital
- Best for: getting a broad picture of the modern financial system without jumping straight into technical math
- Official page: https://online.yale.edu/courses/financial-markets
- YouTube playlist: https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL8FB14A2200B87185

### Stanford eCorner: Startups

- Topic fit: entrepreneurship, startup building, product-market fit, venture formation, founder judgment
- Why it is high value: practical founder education from Stanford's entrepreneurship ecosystem with strong emphasis on execution and decision-making
- Best for: entrepreneurship and early business-building
- Official page: https://ecorner.stanford.edu/startups/

## Intermediate

### Yale Open Courses: Game Theory

- Topic fit: game theory, strategy, auctions, bargaining, signaling, competitive interaction
- Why it is high value: rigorous but accessible treatment of strategic behavior under incentives and incomplete information
- Best for: thinking clearly about negotiation, market structure, competition, and incentives
- Official page: https://oyc.yale.edu/economics/econ-159
- YouTube playlist: https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL6EF60E1027E1A10B

### MIT OpenCourseWare: Blockchain and Money

- Topic fit: money, payment systems, banking structure, digital assets, market infrastructure
- Why it is high value: useful for understanding ledgers, settlement, central-bank questions, and the architecture of modern money rails
- Best for: readers who want money and banking infrastructure, not just personal finance
- Official page: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-s12-blockchain-and-money-fall-2018/video_galleries/video-lectures/

### Yale Online: Capitalism: Success, Crisis, and Reform

- Topic fit: business, commercial society, institutions, capitalism, reform, history
- Why it is high value: gives historical and institutional context for firms, markets, and commercial order rather than only textbook models
- Best for: understanding the wider social and political frame around business and markets
- Official page: https://online.yale.edu/courses/capitalism-success-crisis-and-reform
- YouTube playlist: https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL2497FD1251EED4DD

### Stanford eCorner: Video Playlists

- Topic fit: entrepreneurship, leadership, product, venture capital, growth, execution
- Why it is high value: broad catalog of practical talks that can be used to deepen specific founder or operator questions after the startup basics
- Best for: focused follow-up study by topic after the main startup collection
- Official page: https://ecorner.stanford.edu/video-playlists/
- YouTube archive: https://ecorner.stanford.edu/youtube/

### Yale Online: The Global Financial Crisis

- Topic fit: banking crises, systemic risk, policy response, financial instability, crisis history
- Why it is high value: one of the strongest public treatments of crisis anatomy and how institutions behave under severe stress
- Best for: understanding what breaks in finance and why crisis management matters for wealth preservation
- Official page: https://online.yale.edu/courses/global-financial-crisis

## Advanced

### Yale Open Courses: Financial Theory

- Topic fit: finance theory, risk, return, hedging, mortgages, leverage, equilibrium
- Why it is high value: deeper and more analytical than introductory finance; connects asset pricing and financial structure to the real economy
- Best for: readers who already know the broad system and want a stronger analytical framework
- Official page: https://oyc.yale.edu/economics/econ-251
- YouTube playlist: https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLEDC55106E0BA18FC

### Santa Fe Institute / Complexity Explorer: Introduction to Dynamical Systems and Chaos

- Topic fit: chaos, nonlinear systems, bifurcations, feedback, instability, limits of prediction
- Why it is high value: strong formal foundation for understanding why some systems become unstable or hard to forecast
- Best for: readers who want a disciplined chaos foundation rather than market folklore
- Official page: https://www.complexityexplorer.org/courses/186-introduction-to-dynamical-systems-and-chaos

## Notes and limits

- There is no single elite official playlist that fully covers "wealth" by itself. Wealth is better studied by combining economics, finance, entrepreneurship, and institutional history.
- For "commercial" understanding, business history and institutional structure matter as much as startup tactics.
- For "chaos", use a real nonlinear-systems course. Avoid treating price charts as proof of deep scientific insight.
- If you want to tie this guide directly into the book, the clean next step is to convert these series into chapter-linked study paths and reading companions.
