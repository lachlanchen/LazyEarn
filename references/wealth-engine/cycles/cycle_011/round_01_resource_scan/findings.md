## Focus and rationale
Searched for official archives and recent institutional reports that can deepen money/wealth chapters with:
- named historical episodes (banking design, inflation regimes, commodity shock transmission),
- institutional architecture (central bank design, fiscal systems, global financial stability),
- and constraint channels (energy and foreign investment patterns).

## Sources reviewed and use case

- `Federal Reserve History: Jekyll Island conference`
  - Added to give a named, primary archival anchor for the origin story of modern U.S. central banking.
  - Use for section-level history where the book discusses "who built money institutions".
- `Federal Reserve History: The Great Depression`
  - Strongly supports long-cycle mechanism where panic episodes force changes in lender-of-last-resort design.
  - Use for risk-construction and institutional response sequence.
- `Federal Reserve History: The Great Inflation`
  - Useful for credibility, inflation-anchoring, and mandate-evolution discussion.
  - Use for policy-process and expectation sections.
- `Federal Reserve History: Oil Shock of 1973-74`
  - Useful for wealth and money mechanics under real-resource constraints.
  - Use to bridge financial signals with physical bottlenecks.
- `UNCTAD World Investment Report 2025`
  - Official long-form treatment of FDI structure and productivity-relevant investment episodes.
  - Use in production-capital and wealth inequality context.
- `IMF Fiscal Monitor, October 2025`
  - Official fiscal policy diagnostics and datasets for sovereign budget constraints and growth tradeoffs.
  - Use for sections on public finance, tax capacity, and distribution.
- `IMF Global Financial Stability Report, October 2025`
  - Official macro-financial stress diagnostics with global risk-channel structure.
  - Use in stress-testing and debt/channel framing for wealth strategy.
