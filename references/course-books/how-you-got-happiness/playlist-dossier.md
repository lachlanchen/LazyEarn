# How You Got Happiness? playlist dossier

Date: 2026-04-21

## Purpose

This note records the best official or primary-source video series I found for a future book tentatively titled `How You Got Happiness?`

The goal is not to collect generic motivational content. The goal is to collect:

- the strongest science-backed material on well-being
- the strongest philosophy material on flourishing
- the strongest contemplative material on attention, freedom, and the limits of pleasure-seeking

## First clarification: the “Krishnamurti” question

If the remembered name was “the yoga master called Krish... something,” the most likely person is:

- `Jiddu Krishnamurti`

But he is not best described as a yoga teacher. He is better treated as:

- a philosopher-like spiritual teacher
- a radical critic of authority, organized belief, and fixed methods
- a speaker on awareness, fear, suffering, freedom, and the structure of thought

This matters because for a happiness book he should not be used as “happiness technique” material. He is most useful as a corrective against the naive idea that happiness is something you can simply chase like a product.

## Selection rule

I prioritized resources that satisfy at least one of these:

1. official university source
2. official foundation or institution
3. direct relevance to happiness, flourishing, freedom, or well-being
4. enough depth to support real book writing instead of short-form inspiration

## Best starting set

If you only study three sources first, use these:

1. `Yale Online / Laurie Santos` for the modern science of well-being
2. `Open Yale / Tamar Gendler` for the philosophy of flourishing
3. `J. Krishnamurti Official` for the deepest challenge to pleasure-seeking and method-chasing

## Ranked sources

### 1. The Science of Well-Being

- Official source: `Yale Online`
- Instructor: `Laurie Santos`
- Direct YouTube playlist:
  - <https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLWHJHzhtiAuS30X_NfVz37vXfZ-T3XyFK>
- Official course page:
  - <https://online.yale.edu/courses/science-well-being>

Why it matters:

- This is the strongest clean science-first starting point.
- It directly addresses mistaken beliefs about what makes people happy.
- It is unusually useful for book structure because it separates:
  - what people think will make them happy
  - what research actually suggests
  - what habits and interventions improve well-being

Best use in a book:

- chapter on false models of happiness
- chapter on habit, perception, and cognitive bias
- chapter on practical rewiring rather than vague inspiration

### 2. Philosophy and the Science of Human Nature

- Official source: `Open Yale Courses`
- Instructor: `Tamar Gendler`
- Official course page:
  - <https://oyc.yale.edu/philosophy/phil-181>

Why it matters:

- This is the best philosophy backbone I found for a serious happiness book.
- It explicitly frames the course around:
  - `Happiness and Flourishing`
  - `Morality and Justice`
  - `Political Legitimacy and Social Structures`
- It also pairs classical philosophy with cognitive science.

Most important parts for a happiness book:

- `Lecture 5` — The Well-Ordered Soul: Happiness and Harmony
- `Lecture 6` — The Disordered Soul
- `Lecture 7` — Flourishing and Attachment
- `Lecture 8` — Flourishing and Detachment
- `Lecture 11` — Weakness of the Will and Procrastination

Why it is different from Laurie Santos:

- Laurie Santos is better on empirical well-being and bias.
- Gendler is better on the philosophical structure of a good life.

Best use in a book:

- chapter on happiness versus flourishing
- chapter on harmony, attachment, discipline, and disordered desire
- chapter on why self-command matters

### 3. The Science of Happiness

- Official source: `Greater Good Science Center, UC Berkeley`
- Official video series page:
  - <https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/video/series/science_of_happiness>
- Expanded video-series archive page:
  - <https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/video/series/the_science_of_happiness_videos>

Why it matters:

- This is one of the best evidence-backed applied well-being sources.
- It is less like one linear university course and more like a curated series of practical, research-informed interventions and explanations.
- It is especially useful for:
  - gratitude
  - self-compassion
  - awe
  - apology
  - kindness
  - mindfulness
  - social connection

Why it is not the first source:

- It is stronger as a rich supplement than as the single master spine.
- It gives many practical pieces, but less one unified philosophical architecture.

Best use in a book:

- practical exercises appendix
- chapter endings with concrete practices
- bridging abstract theory and daily life

### 4. J. Krishnamurti – Full-Length Videos

- Official source: `Krishnamurti Foundation Trust`
- Official page:
  - <https://kfoundation.org/video/>
- Direct official YouTube full-length playlist:
  - <https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PL1n30s-LKus6_6oepw2kg1gIp_dg-MGZR>

Why it matters:

- This is the strongest anti-slogan, anti-guru, anti-method corrective in the entire set.
- Krishnamurti is not giving a “how to be happy” program.
- He is useful because he repeatedly asks whether the pursuit of pleasure, security, image, and psychological becoming is itself the trap.

Best use in a book:

- chapter on why chasing happiness often intensifies dissatisfaction
- chapter on observation, attention, and fear
- chapter on freedom from dependence on systems, methods, and authorities

### 5. J. Krishnamurti – A Wholly Different Way of Living

- Official source: `Krishnamurti Foundation Trust`
- Direct official playlist:

Best use in a book:

### 6. J. Krishnamurti – Ending All Fear

- Official source: `Krishnamurti Foundation Trust`
- Direct official playlist:
  - <https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PL1n30s-LKus4W9RzydXnZ-lFfcYDQVOfQ>

Why it matters:

- This series is useful because it treats fear not as a minor emotional inconvenience but as something deeply bound up with thought, time, self-image, and control.

Best use in a book:

- chapter on fear and happiness
- chapter on insecurity, anticipation, and psychological time

## Sources I would treat as secondary, not primary

These can still be useful, but I would not make them the spine of the book:

- `The School of Life`
  - useful for accessible framing and emotional vocabulary
  - weaker as a primary source than Yale or Krishnamurti
- generic motivational channels
  - usually too repetitive
  - too low on evidence
  - often collapse happiness into confidence, productivity, or inspiration

## Working book structure suggestion

If the next book is really `How You Got Happiness?`, the strongest architecture is:

1. `Misconceptions`
   - use Laurie Santos
2. `Flourishing`
   - use Tamar Gendler, Aristotle, and related Yale material
3. `Pleasure, fear, and image`
   - use Krishnamurti
4. `Connection, compassion, and practice`
   - use Greater Good Science Center
5. `A happiness system that does not depend on constant pleasant emotion`
   - synthesize all of the above

## Practical conclusion

If the book aims to be serious, the central argument should probably become:

- happiness is not just pleasure
- happiness is not just success
- happiness is not just positive mood
- happiness is closer to:
  - well-ordered living
  - freedom from false mental habits
  - sane desire
  - connection
  - attention
  - compassion
  - the ability to live without chronic inner contradiction

That is where the Yale material and the Krishnamurti material converge, even though they sound very different.
