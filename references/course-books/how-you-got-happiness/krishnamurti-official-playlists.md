# Krishnamurti official playlist note

Date: 2026-04-21

## Purpose

This note isolates the three Krishnamurti series that are most relevant to a future `How You Got Happiness?` book.

It is separate from the broader happiness playlist dossier so the Krishnamurti material can be used as its own study module.

## First clarification

The person here is:

- `Jiddu Krishnamurti`

He is not best classified as a yoga teacher.

For this project, it is more accurate to treat him as:

- a philosophical spiritual teacher
- a critic of authority, systems, and psychological dependence
- a speaker on fear, pleasure, thought, attention, freedom, suffering, and transformation

## Authorship and official source

### Speaker / author

- `Jiddu Krishnamurti` (1895–1986)

### Official institutional custodian

- `Krishnamurti Foundation Trust`

Official site:

- <https://kfoundation.org/video/>
- <https://kfoundation.org/start/>

The Foundation states that it exists to preserve and make available Krishnamurti’s teachings. That makes it the primary source to trust here rather than random reuploads or fan compilations.

### YouTube status

These playlists are official YouTube playlists associated with the Krishnamurti Foundation Trust material.

I am inferring the YouTube-side custodianship from the Foundation’s own site, which directly points readers to these series and treats them as part of its official video library.

## Why Krishnamurti matters for a happiness book

Krishnamurti is useful precisely because he does not offer a simple “be happy” formula.

He helps on these questions:

- Is the pursuit of happiness itself a trap?
- Is pleasure the same thing as joy?
- Can fear end without suppression?
- Can attention and self-observation change the structure of life?
- Is inner freedom possible without following a method or authority?

That makes him valuable as a counterweight to:

- self-help optimism
- productivity-based models of happiness
- consumer models of emotional well-being

## Playlist 1

### Official playlist name

- `J. Krishnamurti – San Diego 1974 – Conversations with Allan W. Anderson – A wholly different way of living`

Playlist link:

- <https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PL1n30s-LKus7XDJA0eXbd5n3f9qDcQOWP>

### Format

- video series
- long-form conversations
- dialogue format rather than monologue

### Setting

- `San Diego`
- `1974`
- conversation partner: `Allan W. Anderson`

### Why this series is important

The Foundation describes this as one of Krishnamurti’s best-known video series.

From the official description, the conversations range across:

- knowledge
- love
- pleasure
- meditation
- the sacred

That makes it the strongest single Krishnamurti series for a happiness project.

### Best use in `How You Got Happiness?`

- chapter on pleasure versus joy
- chapter on whether thought can ever produce freedom
- chapter on love, relationship, and psychological dependence
- chapter on whether a different way of living is possible without ideology

### Why it is the best first Krishnamurti series

- broadest topic range
- dialogue format makes it easier to enter than some of the denser public talks
- directly engages the themes most often confused with happiness

## Playlist 2

### Official playlist name

- `J. Krishnamurti – San Diego 1970 – Ending all fear`

Playlist link:

- <https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PL1n30s-LKus4W9RzydXnZ-lFfcYDQVOfQ>

### Format

- video series
- early colour recordings

### Setting

- `San Diego`
- `1970`

### Why this series is important

The Foundation describes this as a series on the possibility of fear ending, followed by a deeper inquiry into meditation, with discussion of whether human beings can be free from violence.

That is directly relevant because fear is one of the main destroyers of stable happiness.

### Best use in `How You Got Happiness?`

- chapter on fear as an obstacle to well-being
- chapter on psychological time, anticipation, and insecurity
- chapter on meditation without technique addiction
- chapter on the difference between calmness and actual freedom from fear

### Why it matters more than generic “anxiety content”

Krishnamurti does not treat fear as just stress management. He treats it as structurally linked to thought, memory, image, and projection.

That is much more useful for a serious book.

## Playlist 3

### Official playlist name

- `J. Krishnamurti – Brockwood Park 1976 – Discussions with D. Bohm and D. Shainberg – The transformation of man`

Playlist link:

- <https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PL1n30s-LKus4ipHYdBpKt1-AdSN181Lc0>

### Format

- video discussion series
- intellectually dense dialogue

### Setting

- `Brockwood Park`
- `1976`
- discussion partners:
  - `David Bohm`
  - `David Shainberg`

### Why this series is important

This is the most transformation-focused of the three.

The surrounding Foundation material on this series emphasizes:

- consciousness and its content
- fragmentation
- mechanical living
- deep change at the root
- image-making
- order and holistic action

For a happiness book, this matters when the project moves beyond mood and asks whether a human life can be reorganized at a deeper level.

### Best use in `How You Got Happiness?`

- chapter on transformation versus self-improvement theater
- chapter on fragmentation and inner contradiction
- chapter on mechanical living and disorder
- chapter on whether insight can alter the structure of consciousness

### Why it is harder but important

- denser than the other two series
- more abstract and philosophical
- best used after the reader already understands the fear and pleasure questions

## Recommended watch order

If the goal is a book, not casual inspiration, the strongest order is:

1. `A wholly different way of living`
2. `Ending all fear`
3. `The transformation of man`

Why:

- the first gives the widest entry into Krishnamurti’s concerns
- the second sharpens the fear problem
- the third pushes toward transformation and wholeness

### Book role 2: fear and insecurity

## Practical caution

Krishnamurti should not be the only source for a happiness book.

If used alone, the manuscript can become:

- too abstract
- too anti-method
- too detached from empirical psychology

He works best in combination with:

- `Laurie Santos / Yale` for empirical well-being
- `Tamar Gendler / Yale` for flourishing and philosophical structure
- `Greater Good Science Center` for practical exercises and relational science
